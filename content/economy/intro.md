The republic leaves the EU and the euro, takes the commanding heights into social ownership, builds free public services and plans its investment. The central question is the one opponents will ask first: **how does it pay its way?**

The answer here rests on Ireland's real accounts, not on hope. It starts from GNI\*, the measure of the economy without the multinationals' paper profits, and follows the money that actually reaches people here.

Three facts frame everything else:

- **The State is already in underlying deficit.** Without the windfall corporation tax paid by a handful of US firms, the 2025 surplus becomes a deficit of about 2–3½% of GNI\*. The republic starts from that position.
- **The multinationals matter through what they spend here**: about €26bn of wages, €17bn of purchases from Irish firms and €29bn of corporation tax a year. That spending is also the domestic economy's largest source of foreign currency.
- **The binding constraint is foreign currency, not money.** With its own currency the State cannot run out of punts. It can run out of the euro, dollars and sterling it needs for €23bn a year of energy, food and medicines.

The model shows where the design holds and where it breaks. On its central settings, it breaks. The pressure explorer shows by how much, and what it would take to hold.

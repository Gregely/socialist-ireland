**Who owns what.** Five forms of ownership (Art. 47), each suited to a different scale, rather than one form for everything. The bars show the drafter's target mix for each sector at the end of the transition, against each sector's real size today. The foreign-charter share shrinks as more multinationals leave; set that in the [pressure explorer](#/economy/pressure).

**What is planned, what is marketed, what is free.** This is the owner's answer to Q7:
- **free or at-cost public provision** for housing, health, care, childcare, education, public transport and a basic energy allowance (Art. 68);
- **negotiated planning** for investment and the major sectors (Art. 56), with at least a third of each county's capital budget allocated by its local assemblies (Art. 57);
- **markets**, mostly co-operatives and small firms, for most consumer goods and everyday services (Art. 58).

**Who decides investment.** The [[investment-council|National Investment Council]] prepares a five-year plan by negotiation among delegates of workplaces, users, counties and the Executive. The [[ecological-authority|Ecological Authority]] sits on it with a veto over physical budgets. The plan takes effect only when the [[national-assembly|National Assembly]] and the [[congress-of-councils|Congress of Councils]] both approve it. Credit flows through the [[public-bank|public bank and credit unions]]; long-term investment through the [[social-wealth-fund|Social Wealth Fund]]; money and capital controls through the [[central-bank|Central Bank]].

**How soft budgets are avoided (FM-06).** No public body or co-operative has a right to have its losses covered (Art. 54). A rescue needs a specific, published vote with its cost. Workers are protected through the Job Guarantee and retraining, not through the failing enterprise. Accounts and plans are published in full (Art. 59).

:::variant Q1=A
**The party and the economy.** Under the default party settlement, the leading party holds no seat, veto or power of appointment on any planning body, enterprise board or bank (Art. 30.2). Its members sit on these bodies only as delegates of workplaces, users or councils, like anyone else. **This is where the settlement is most likely to erode in practice**: a party whose members fill most delegate seats can steer the plan without any formal power. That is the nomenklatura risk (FM-01) in its economic form.
:::

:::variant Q1=B
**Parties and the economy.** With several socialist parties, no party holds any seat, veto or appointment on a planning body, board or bank (Art. 30.3). Competing parties make capture of the planning bodies harder, but bargaining between them can make the plan a matter of party deals.
:::

:::variant Q1=D
**Councils without parties and the economy.** With no parties, delegates to the planning bodies answer only to the workplaces, users and councils that sent them. The risk moves to informal slates: organised networks without a party's accountability (FM-01).
:::

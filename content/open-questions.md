These are the tensions the design has not resolved, and the strongest objections to it. Some are choices the project has made knowing the cost. Others are problems no one has solved.

## 1. The leading party is the design's weakest point

The default answer to the party question is a single socialist party, bound by protected factions, party–state separation, wage caps, an audit by lot and a ten-year renewal referendum. **By the design's own scoring, this is the riskiest choice against FM-02 (monopoly of power) and FM-17 (legitimacy decay).**

- **The historical record is against it.** Every ruling party began with internal democracy. The Bolsheviks had organised factions until March 1921. No single ruling party has stayed internally democratic over a generation. ([research note](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md))
- **An organised party against unorganised individuals.** Independents can stand and win, but in practice a party with organisation, money and a press will win most contests. Uganda's "no-party" system (1986–2005) shows how a dominant organisation wins under rules that formally treat everyone as an individual.
- **A minority with speech but no vehicle.** Pro-market citizens can say anything, publish anything and stand as individuals, but they cannot form an electoral party. If a third of the country feels permanently shut out, legitimacy decays and opposition may turn to outside backers (FM-10).
- **Law.** Restricting parties is likely to engage Article 11 of the European Convention on Human Rights and Article 2 of the EU Treaty. That conflicts with the choice to stay in the EU (Q4). Detailed legal research on this is still to be done.

**The strongest objection**, from a liberal and from Luxemburg alike, is that freedom confined to supporters of one party is no freedom at all. **The design's answer** is that the party's role is never a legal guarantee: it is tested at every election and in a referendum every ten years. Whether that is enough is the single most important open question here. You can [switch to the alternatives](#/choices) to compare.

## 2. Entrenchment versus the people's right to change their minds

Social ownership of housing, the grid, water, health and rail can be undone only by two Assemblies with an election in between, followed by a referendum. Democratic rights cannot be undone at all.

- **The asymmetry objection.** Opponents will say the transition used ordinary majorities, so reversing it should need no more. The design answers by putting the transition itself through the same double passage (Art. 111).
- **Entrenchment is a secondary defence.** Weimar's constitution did not save Weimar. What protects social ownership is organised popular strength: unions, councils, co-operatives and tenants. A clause cannot substitute for that.

## 3. The ecological limit versus the good life

- **Sufficiency versus variety.** Binding physical limits and planned cuts in named wasteful sectors (FM-16) pull against the variety and pleasure in consumption whose absence helped sink the GDR (FM-17). "Public luxury, private sufficiency" is a plausible reconciliation. It is not proven to satisfy people raised on consumer capitalism.
- **The herd.** The Ecological Authority's veto and the farmers' Land and Farm Transition Board can deadlock, and there is no easy rule for who wins. An income guarantee pegged to boom-year cattle prices is very expensive.
- **The throughput question.** The design takes no side on whether total material use must fall (degrowth) or can be decoupled from growth (eco-modernism). Hickel and Kallis find no evidence of decoupling at the necessary speed. ([research note](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md))

## 4. Bodies chosen by lot

Bodies chosen by lot have binding power over officials' pay, emergency extensions and security oversight.

- **Secretariat capture.** Members depend on staff. The Chamber hires and rotates its own staff, but this is untested.
- **Accountability.** Who removes a member chosen by lot who behaves badly? Only the Chamber itself can.
- **Deference.** In a real security crisis, people chosen by lot may defer to the executive's account of the threat.

## 5. The economy

- **The knowledge problem is answered only in part.** Negotiated planning answers Mises better than Hayek. Devine's model is thin on operational detail. ([research note](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md))
- **Innovation.** Incentives to innovate are weaker than under capitalism. Public R&D, open licences and the freedom to start co-operatives may not make up for that.
- **The transition will cost GDP.** Losing part of the multinational sector will make Ireland poorer by conventional measures, at least for a time. The design argues the trade (housing, health, care, time, security) is worth it. It does not promise otherwise.
- **The figures are guesses.** The 1:4 pay ratio, the 50-employee conversion threshold and similar numbers are reasoned guesses, not findings.

## 6. The North

The 26 counties first, with a standing offer of an all-island convention, means **two economic systems on one island with an open border**, managed under the Good Friday Agreement and the Windsor Framework. That is uncharted. And to unionists, a project rooted in Connolly and the First Dáil may read as nationalism by another name, however civic its text (FM-14).

## 7. Care

Socialising care can slide into institutionalisation, a fear sharpened by Ireland's own history of Magdalene laundries and mother-and-baby homes, which this research has not yet treated properly. Paying carers risks turning family relationships into wage relationships, the dilemma Silvia Federici identified. ([research note](research/11-social-reproduction.md))

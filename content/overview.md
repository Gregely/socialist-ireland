## What this is

This is a design for the government and economy of a democratic socialist republic in Ireland. It is Marxist in its analysis, socialist in its economics, and democratic in its structure. It is a **design document**, not a manifesto: it sets out institutions, says what each one is for, and says how each one could fail.

It starts from an uncomfortable fact. Almost every twentieth-century state that called itself socialist produced, within a generation, a privileged official class, a security service answerable to no one, a personality cult, or all three. Most of them ended by being dismantled by their own elites. The people who analysed this most sharply were socialists themselves: Rosa Luxemburg in 1918, Trotsky in 1936, Djilas in 1957. A design that does not take their analysis seriously does not deserve to be taken seriously.

So **every institution here is built against a named failure mode**, with a historical precedent for the mechanism, a statement of the risk that remains, and the strongest critique of it from liberal, market and left perspectives. You can see all of this on the [institution map](#/map) and the [failure-mode pages](#/threats).

## Why Ireland

Ireland has its own socialist tradition, and it is older and stranger than most people know.

- **William Thompson of Cork** argued in 1824, more than twenty years before *The Communist Manifesto*, that labour was entitled to its whole product, and that it should be organised in co-operative communities. With **Anna Doyle Wheeler** he wrote in 1825 that women's emancipation had to be economic, not only legal. ([research note](research/07-irish-lineage.md))
- **James Connolly** warned in 1897 that if you *"remove the English army to-morrow and hoist the green flag over Dublin Castle"* without changing who owns the country's wealth, *"England would still rule you to your ruin"*. ([source](https://www.marxists.org/archive/connolly/1897/01/socnat.htm))
- **The Democratic Programme of the First Dáil**, adopted on 21 January 1919, declared that *"all right to private property must be subordinated to the public right and welfare"*. It was never implemented, and a minister of the Free State later dismissed it as "largely poetry". ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Democratic_Programme_of_the_First_D%C3%A1il))
- **The soviets of 1919–23.** In April 1919 the Limerick strike committee ran the city's food supply and prices and printed its own money for nearly two weeks. In 1920 the workers of the Knocklong creamery hung a banner reading "We make butter not profits". Both sides of the Civil War suppressed these councils.

This design treats that lineage as a source, not as scripture. Some of those soviets were short strikes using a fashionable name. The Democratic Programme's own authors may not have meant it. Connolly never held state power.

## The choices behind it

The hardest questions (whether to have a leading party, how to relate to the EU, what to do about the North, how hard to make it to undo social ownership) are set out on the [design choices](#/choices) page with the reasons for each decision. **The party question** is so contested that you can **switch the whole site** to an alternative and see what changes.

The republic is **outside the European Union** and has its own currency. Its party settlement cannot meet the EU's founding values, so it leaves; the [design choices](#/choices) page says why, and what leaving costs: a new currency, tariffs, and an external frontier on the land border.

The default design makes one choice that is, by its own account, **the riskiest in the whole design**: a single leading socialist party, bound by strict rules, in place of open multi-party competition. The site says so wherever it matters, and shows [where the safeguards are thinnest](#/choices).

## How to read it

- **Not a game.** The [stress tests](#/stress) branch where there are real choices, but there are no points and no way to win, only consequences.
- **Honest about evidence.** Every claim is linked to a source. Where sources disagree, the text says so. Books that were read only through reviews are marked on the [sources page](#/sources).
- **Editable.** All the text lives in plain Markdown and JSON files and can be changed without touching code.
- **Irish-language text is provisional** and awaits review by a fluent speaker.

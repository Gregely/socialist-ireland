People cross the land border as before, under the Common Travel Area, which London and Dublin keep by agreement (Art. 107.2). Goods are another matter: the North applies EU rules, so food and animals entering it from the republic are checked. Under a three-way arrangement those checks happen at depots in Newry and Derry, and by trusted-trader records, not at the line. The republic builds nothing on its side (Art. 107.3).

There are protests at two crossings in the first months, and a camera mast is pulled down near Belcoo. Nobody is hurt.

## Where it could have gone wrong

The peace process was built around the absence of this frontier. Checks at the line, or a death, would have handed both nationalisms a grievance (FM-14). The republic can refuse to build a border; only agreement with London and Brussels can keep checks away from it ([13 §4.5](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md)).

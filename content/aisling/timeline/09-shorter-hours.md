The Assembly carries out its first five-year review under Article 72 and cuts the standard week to 35 hours, in line with the rise in productivity. Pay does not fall. Five years later it does the same again: **by year 10 the standard week is 32 hours, four days for most people**, and five weeks' paid leave is the floor (Art. 72.2).

In the economy model, pay per worker in real terms dips during the transition and is back above today's level by year 15. Because hours have fallen by a fifth, **pay per hour worked is about 40% higher than today by year 25** (best-case settings).

## Where it could have gone wrong

If shorter hours had cut output more than productivity rose, or if the time had been taken back in overtime, it would have been a pay cut by another name. The design's answer is that time is the reward that effort buys (FM-07).

After eighteen months of negotiation, the Land and Farm Transition Board, half of whose members are delegates elected by farmers (Art. 67.1), agrees a ten-year land-use plan. The national herd falls by 35% by year 10. Every farm household keeps an income no lower than its average of the five years before the transition, and is paid for rewetting, hedgerows, forestry and new crops (Art. 67.2). Nobody's land is taken (Art. 5.3).

The dairy co-ops, back in farmer and worker ownership (Art. 67.3), move part of their capacity into oats, cheese for the home market and plant proteins. The first rewetting contracts go out on the 45,000 hectares of bog that state schemes drained ([data: EAP09](research/14-geography-sources.md)).

## Where it could have gone wrong

The herd is the hardest constituency in the design. A ruined harvest, or a trade shock that cut exports before the plan did, would have turned negotiation into imposition ([harvest and energy shock](#/stress/harvest-energy-shock); [leaving the EU](#/stress/leaving-the-eu?path=s1.s2.s3a.s4.s5.s6.s7b)).

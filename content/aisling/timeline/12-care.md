The National Care Service now provides childcare from the end of parental leave, home care, and residential care, with care workers on public-sector terms (Art. 71.1). The second national time-use survey, required every five years (Art. 71.2), shows unpaid care hours falling and, for the first time since records began in 2005, shared more evenly between men and women.

Most home care is run by care co-operatives under the Service, in which care workers and the families they serve sit on the same board.

## Where it could have gone wrong

Socialising care can slide into institutionalisation, a fear sharpened by Ireland's history of Magdalene laundries and mother-and-baby homes. Paying for care can also turn family relationships into wage relationships ([11](research/11-social-reproduction.md); FM-18).

A Galway county councillor votes, against her local assemblies' published mandate, to rezone forty acres outside Oranmore for a private developer. Within three weeks, 22% of the electors in her area sign a recall petition (Art. 9.2). The recall vote is held on day 41. She loses it, and a by-election follows in which she may not stand. The rezoning is reversed at the next council meeting.

What makes it matter is what does not happen: no recall of anyone for their opinions, no wave of petitions. In the first ten years, eleven recall petitions reach the threshold across the whole country; four succeed.

## Where it could have gone wrong

Recall can be used to harass, or never used at all. With participation fading, as Porto Alegre's did ([06 §9](research/06-positive-models.md)), the 20% threshold would have been out of reach, and councils would have answered to no one between elections (FM-08).

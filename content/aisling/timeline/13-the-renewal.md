:::variant Q1=A
The first renewal referendum under Article 33 asks whether the party should keep the status Article 30 gives it. **It passes, 58% to 42%.** The campaign is hard and free. The No side is led by two of the party's own protected platforms, which argue for several socialist parties, and by the independents who now hold a third of council seats. The result is won, not granted. That is the whole point of putting it to a vote.
:::

:::variant Q1=B
The tenth year brings the third general election contested by the republic's several socialist parties. No party wins a majority; a coalition of three forms in public, on a published programme.
:::

:::variant Q1=D
The tenth year brings the third general election without parties. More than half of the National Assembly's members were first elected as nominees of local assemblies and workplace councils, not of any organisation.
:::

At the same time the citizens' assembly reviewing the Constitution's first decade reports (Art. 116). It recommends forty changes. The Assembly adopts twenty-six by the ordinary procedure; three go to referendum. The housing minister credited with the first estates has already left office at the end of her second term (Art. 28).

## Where it could have gone wrong

A popular leader, a party with every advantage of incumbency, and a shortcut through an emergency are the [term-limits](#/stress/term-limits) test. By the design's own scoring the party settlement is its riskiest choice (FM-02, FM-17).

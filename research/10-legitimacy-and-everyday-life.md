# 10 — Legitimacy decay and "grey socialism" (FM-17)

## 1. The problem

A system can work in the narrow sense, with people housed, employed, fed and educated, and still lose the belief of the people living in it. Late-Soviet and East German societies were not collapsing materially in the way famine-era states were. They lost legitimacy through *drabness, ritual and comparison*:

- daily life felt shabby next to the West;
- official language stopped meaning anything;
- access to good things depended on connections or hard currency, not on being a citizen.

A democratic socialist design is *more* exposed to this than an authoritarian one, because people can vote it out. That is good. It also means "the system works, technically" is not enough.

## 2. The historical record

### 2.1 Late-Soviet ritual and "performative shift" — Yurchak
- Alexei Yurchak, *Everything Was Forever, Until It Was No More: The Last Soviet Generation* (Princeton UP, 2005/2006). It won the 2007 Wayne S. Vucinich Book Prize ([ASEEES](https://www.aseees.pitt.edu/node/2538); [Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/30657)).
- **Core idea.** From the 1950s on, official ideological language became standardised and ritualised. People kept *performing* it (voting, attending meetings, repeating formulas) while its *literal meaning* stopped mattering. Yurchak calls this a "performative shift": the act of saying something becomes more important than what is said. Many Soviet citizens lived "inside and outside" the system at once. They were neither true believers nor dissidents ([IJOC review](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/57/14); [Kunstkamera review](https://anthropologie.kunstkamera.ru/en/06/2015_26/kormina); [Sheila Fitzpatrick, LRB](https://lrb.co.uk/v28/n10/sheila-fitzpatrick/normal-people)).
- **Hence the paradox.** The system seemed eternal right up until it suddenly wasn't, and when it went, most people found they had been "ready" for its end without knowing it ([Euxeinos](https://gce.unisg.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/HSG_ROOT/Institut_GCE/Euxeinos/26-25/Lugaric_Euxeinos_25_26.pdf)). The publisher's blurb uses the word "hypernormalisation", which Adam Curtis's 2016 film made famous. I could not confirm Yurchak's own definition of it in this pass.
- **Design lesson.** Rituals of participation that no longer change anything are *worse* than no participation. Meetings whose outcomes are known in advance, or votes with one candidate, teach cynicism. This links directly to FM-08: assemblies emptied of real power.

### 2.2 Consumer comparison: the GDR
- **Waiting lists.** The Trabant, the GDR's people's car, had waiting lists reported at 13–16 years, and some accounts say longer by 1989. The cheapest 1989 model cost about 12,000 marks, around fifteen months of average pay. Some professions and officials could jump the queue, and places on the waiting list were traded informally ([Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/trabant)). Figures vary across sources.
- **Intershop.** State shops selling Western goods *only for hard currency*. They were set up to earn foreign exchange, and some sold re-imported East German export goods ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intershop)). The *political* effect was a visible two-tier citizenship. Good things went to people with West German relatives sending money, not to workers as such. That reading is my inference; the sources describe the mechanism, not survey data on its effect.
- **Czechoslovakia's equivalent, Tuzex**, is covered in Paulina Bren's chapter "Tuzex and the Hustler" in Bren & Neuburger (eds.), *Communism Unwrapped: Consumption in Cold War Eastern Europe* (OUP, 2012). The volume argues that consumption pervaded everyday life under communism more than most political phenomena did ([Cambridge review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/austrian-history-yearbook/article/paulina-bren-and-mary-neuburger-edscommunism-unwrapped-consumption-in-cold-war-eastern-europe-new-york-oxford-university-press-2012-pp-432-illus/08998B52B3B7803F3217EE75F18B6059); [table of contents](https://hds.hebis.de/herder/Record/HEB307424200/TOC)).
- **The Kitchen Debate (24 July 1959).** Nixon conceded Soviet leadership in rockets but claimed American superiority in consumer goods, standing in a model American kitchen at the Moscow exhibition. Khrushchev dismissed the glorification of gadgets. Historians note a parallel debate *inside* the Soviet leadership about how to supply the new single-family flats replacing communal living ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchen_Debate)). **Socialism let capitalism choose the ground, consumer goods, and then lost on that ground.**

### 2.3 A counter-intuitive finding: entertainment *stabilised* the GDR
- Kern & Hainmueller, "Opium for the Masses: How Foreign Media Can Stabilize Authoritarian Regimes", *Political Analysis* 17(4), 2009. The study used the "Valley of the Clueless" (areas around Dresden that could not receive West German TV) as a natural experiment. **East Germans with access to West German TV reported *higher* life satisfaction and *more* support for the regime.** They mostly watched it for entertainment, not news. Exit-visa patterns and archival evidence support this ([paper, MIT](https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/OVM2009.pdf); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/CE2EAEB87BD8DAF009BE4BBA6C30A0F8)).
- **Design lesson.** Legitimacy depends heavily on whether daily life is *enjoyable*, and a state that rations culture, colour and fun pays a political price. It also shows that "access to Western media undermines socialism" is not a reliable law. Openness can *strengthen* a system people otherwise accept.

### 2.4 Price shocks as legitimacy shocks: Poland
- In **December 1970**, sudden food price increases set off strikes in Gdańsk and along the coast. Security forces killed at least 41–44 people. Gomułka was replaced by Gierek ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_1970_protests_in_Poland); [ENRS](https://enrs.eu/news/december-riots-in-poland-in-1970)).
- In **July 1980**, meat and food price rises of 40–60% set off strikes across more than 200 workplaces. Those strikes became the Gdańsk shipyard occupation and Solidarność, a *workers'* movement against a *workers'* state ([marxists.org/EROL](https://www.marxists.org/history/erol/ncm-7/lrs-poland-4.htm)). The single source here is partisan and should be cross-checked.
- **Lesson.** When the state sets prices, every price rise becomes a political decision that can be blamed on the government. Administered prices make *the state itself* the target of everyday grievance. In a market, by contrast, anger at prices is spread across many sellers.

## 3. The theory: Morris and Fisher

### 3.1 William Morris (1834–1896)
- **"Useful Work versus Useless Toil"** (lecture, 1884; [libcom text](https://libcom.org/article/useful-work-versus-useless-toil-william-morris); [1884 booklet scan](https://worrydream.com/booklets/Morris_1884_-_Useful_Work_versus_Useless_Toil_(booklet).pdf)):
  - *"Here, you see, are two kinds of work - one good, the other bad"*.
  - Worthy work carries *"the hope of pleasure in rest, the hope of pleasure in our using what it makes, and the hope of pleasure in our daily creative skill."*
  - Of goods made for profit and display: *"they are not wealth, but waste."*
  - On the conditions that make work attractive: *"pleasant surroundings"*, and *"We must begin to build up the ornamental part of life"*.
- ***News from Nowhere*** (serialised in *Commonweal*, January–October 1890; book 1891). It was a libertarian-socialist answer to Edward Bellamy's centralised, state-socialist *Looking Backward*. Morris imagines a society without private property, money or government, where the divisions between art, life and work have dissolved and labour is a pleasure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_from_Nowhere); [Project Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/ebooks/3261)).
- **Why Morris matters for FM-17.** He saw, before any socialist state existed, that the danger was a socialism of *efficient drabness*: Bellamy's industrial army. His answer was **beauty, craft and pleasure in work as core goals, not luxuries for later.** That is a direct rebuttal to "grey socialism".

### 3.2 Mark Fisher (1968–2017)
- ***Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?*** (Zero Books, 2009). It popularised the line, which Fisher attributes to Jameson and Žižek, that it is "easier to imagine an end to the world than an end to capitalism". After 1989, capitalism appears as the only viable system, and it becomes impossible even to *imagine* a coherent alternative ([Glasgow paper on Fisher](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/290199/2/290199.pdf); [New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/?p=3351)).
- **"Market Stalinism."** Fisher argued that neoliberalism did not abolish bureaucracy. It multiplied it, in audits, targets and performance metrics where "everything is for appearance". This is a Soviet pathology *reproduced inside capitalism* ([Spike Magazine](https://spikemagazine.com/mark-fisher-capitalist-realism/); [Stephen Diehl review](https://www.stephendiehl.com/posts/capitalist_realism/)). **This cuts both ways for the design.** Target culture is not unique to socialism, but a planned economy that runs on targets will reproduce it intensely unless it is designed against it. Compare Yurchak's ritualised performance.
- **"Post-capitalist desire" and "acid communism"** (unfinished at his death). The left must *"disarticulate desire from capitalism"*. It should not shame people for wanting things. It should show that the most powerful desires (for the new, the strange, collective joy, free time) are frustrated by capitalism and could be met better otherwise ([Weird Economies](https://weirdeconomies.com/fixes/from-critique-to-construction-acid-communism-and-postcapitalist-desire); [Mediations](https://mediationsjournal.org/articles/still-a-world)). Secondary accounts describe him engaging critically with the image of Soviet greyness. I could not locate a specific passage in this pass, so the site should not attribute a "grey socialism" quote to Fisher.
- **Why Fisher matters for FM-17.** He names the trap from the other side. If socialism is offered as sacrifice and sobriety, capitalism keeps a monopoly on desire. That is also the core of Huber's critique of degrowth's "politics of less" (file 09).

## 4. Root causes (for SYNTHESIS FM-17)

1. **Ritualised participation.** Forms survive and lose their content (Yurchak). Feeds and is fed by FM-08.
2. **Consumer scarcity and poor quality**, especially in *visible, desirable* goods (Kornai's shortage, file 03; the Trabant).
3. **Two-tier access** by connections or hard currency (Intershop, Tuzex, nomenklatura shops in file 01). This is worse for legitimacy than equal scarcity.
4. **Comparison with a richer neighbour** (Kitchen Debate, Western TV). For Ireland, that neighbour is the UK, the US and the EU core.
5. **Administered prices make the state the target** of everyday grievance (Poland 1970 and 1980).
6. **Aesthetic neglect.** Public space, design, colour and culture treated as secondary to production (Morris's warning).
7. **A sacrificial political offer** that cedes desire to capitalism (Fisher; Huber against degrowth).
8. **Target and audit culture** ("market Stalinism") that teaches everyone to perform compliance.

## 5. What the evidence suggests

- **Make public luxury genuinely luxurious.** Beautiful libraries, baths, parks, transit, food halls and venues: Morris's "ornamental part of life", Monbiot's "public luxury". Budget for design and beauty explicitly, and never let it be the first thing cut.
- **No hard-currency or connections tier.** If scarcity exists, ration *openly and equally*: queues by rule, lotteries, waiting lists published. Never through privilege (FM-01).
- **Keep markets where variety matters to people.** Clothing, food, crafts, music, small hospitality and design are where drabness is most visible and planning least competent (Nove, file 03). This is a trade-off with FM-16 (less consumption).
- **Cultural openness.** No restriction on foreign media. Kern & Hainmueller suggest openness can *help*. Protect a pluralist culture of fun as well as of seriousness.
- **Participation that visibly changes outcomes.** Better few binding assemblies than many symbolic ones (FM-08).
- **Free time as the headline good.** The most direct answer to Fisher's question of desire.
- **Price changes are made by rule, not by announcement.** Index-linked, published formulas with compensation built in, so that prices are not decided overnight by politicians (Poland).
- **Avoid target culture.** Use few, published indicators that are chosen democratically and rotated so they cannot be gamed. Use sortition-based audit panels instead of top-down metrics (Fisher's "market Stalinism").
- **Residual tension.** Variety and consumer pleasure pull against ecological sufficiency (FM-16). The reconciliation offered by Saito and Monbiot, *public* abundance with *private* sufficiency, is plausible. It is not proven to be enough for people raised on consumer capitalism. The site should say that plainly.

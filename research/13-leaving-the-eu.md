# 13 — Leaving the EU and the euro

Research for the owner's revised answer to Q4 (October 2026): **the republic is outside the European Union and has its own currency.** This note sets out why membership is incompatible with the design, what leaving the euro involves, how capital controls have actually performed, and what trade and the land border look like from outside. Every figure links to its source. Where a source was seen only in a search result, or could not be checked, it says so. Figures are as of October 2026.

## 1. Why membership is incompatible with the design

### 1.1 What the Treaties require

- **Article 2 TEU** names the values the Union is founded on: *"respect for human dignity, freedom, democracy, equality, the rule of law and respect for human rights … These values are common to the Member States in a society in which pluralism, non-discrimination, tolerance, justice, solidarity and equality between women and men prevail."* ([TEU Art. 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/2))
- **Article 49 TEU** makes those values the condition of membership: *"Any European State which respects the values referred to in Article 2 and is committed to promoting them may apply to become a member of the Union."* ([TEU Art. 49](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/49))
- **The Copenhagen criteria** (European Council, June 1993) add: *"Membership requires that the candidate country has achieved stability of institutions guaranteeing democracy, the rule of law, human rights and respect for and protection of minorities, the existence of a functioning market economy as well as the capacity to cope with competitive pressure and market forces within the Union."* They were written for economies *"weakened by 40 years of central planning"* and assume *"a rapid transition to a market economy"* ([European Council conclusions, via CVCE](https://www.cvce.eu/content/publication/2004/3/18/24104be4-664b-41b8-8e16-756c57868498/publishable_en.pdf)).
- **The Court of Justice** held in 2022 that these values *"define the very identity of the European Union as a legal order common to those States"*, and that compliance *"cannot be reduced to an obligation which a candidate State must meet in order to accede to the European Union and which it may disregard after accession"* (Cases C-156/21 *Hungary v Parliament and Council* and C-157/21 *Poland v Parliament and Council*, 16 February 2022; [CJEU press release 28/22](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-02/cp220028en.pdf)).

### 1.2 Where the design breaks them

1. **The party settlement (Q1, default A).** Article 30 lets one socialist party put a party label on a ballot and bars every other association from nominating candidates. That is a restriction of political pluralism by design. It cannot be squared with "democracy" and "pluralism" in Article 2 as the Court reads them, and the Copenhagen political criterion would rule out membership for any candidate with such a rule.
2. **The economic settlement.** The EU is formally neutral on ownership: *"The Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership"* ([TFEU Art. 345](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teec/article/345)). But the design's *instruments* collide with the internal market:
   - capital controls, against *"all restrictions on the movement of capital between Member States and between Member States and third countries shall be prohibited"* ([TFEU Art. 63](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teec/article/63)); the exceptions are narrow, and safeguards against third countries are limited to *"not exceeding six months"* ([Art. 66](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teec/article/66));
   - state support for co-operatives and the public bank, against the state-aid rule that aid *"which distorts or threatens to distort competition by favouring certain undertakings … shall, in so far as it affects trade between Member States, be incompatible with the internal market"* ([TFEU Art. 107](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teec/article/107));
   - procurement preferences, and the compulsory conversion of firms above fifty employees (Art. 47), which the Court has treated as restrictions on capital movement even when ownership itself is protected (*Essent*, 2013; see [08 §10](08-ireland-today.md)).

### 1.3 What honesty requires us to add

- **The EU cannot expel a member.** Article 7 TEU allows the Council to *"suspend certain of the rights deriving from the application of the Treaties … including the voting rights"*, and says the state's obligations *"shall in any case continue to be binding"* ([TEU Art. 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/7)). There is no expulsion clause. A European Parliament briefing says Article 7 *"only encompasses suspension and not expulsion"* (seen in a search result only: [EPRS 2023](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2023/751410/EPRS_BRI(2023)751410_EN.pdf)). So an existing member that adopted this settlement would not be thrown out. It would face Article 7, infringement proceedings and budget sanctions. Hungary lost access to €6.3 billion of cohesion funds under the conditionality regulation in December 2022 ([eucrim](https://eucrim.eu/news/council-adopted-historic-decision-on-budget-protection-vis-a-vis-hungary-conditions-also-for-rrf-payments/); [Regulation 2020/2092 Art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2020/2092/article/4)). But Article 7 has never gone beyond its first stage: the Polish procedure ran from December 2017 until the Commission closed it in May 2024 ([Notes from Poland](https://notesfrompoland.com/2024/05/29/eu-closes-article-7-rule-of-law-proceedings-against-poland-after-seven-years/)), and the Hungarian one, opened in September 2018, is still at that stage, with MEPs reported in September 2026 to be moving to end it ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/07/exclusive-meps-on-track-to-end-article-7-nuclear-option-against-hungary)).
- **So the republic leaves by its own choice, not because it is pushed.** Staying would mean permanent, open conflict with the Union over values the republic has signed up to, and a breach of Treaty obligations on capital and state aid every time the plan is used. That is the "stay but defy" path the owner rejected earlier because it normalises selective legality. Leaving under Article 50 is the lawful way out.
- **The Copenhagen criteria formally govern applicants, not members.** They matter here because Article 50(5) sends any state that wants to rejoin back through Article 49 ([TEU Art. 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/50)). While the party settlement stands, re-entry is closed.
- **The economic objection is weaker than the political one.** Under Q1-B (several socialist parties) or Q1-D (councils without parties), the Article 2 objection largely falls away. What remains is the conflict between the design's economic instruments and the internal market, which a member state could, in principle, try to negotiate. The owner's choice to be outside applies under every Q1 setting, and the site says that under B and D it rests on the economic case alone.

### 1.4 The European Convention on Human Rights is a separate, unsolved problem

Leaving the EU does not touch the ECHR, which the republic keeps (Art. 105). The Strasbourg court treats party pluralism as part of the Convention itself:

- *"there can be no democracy without pluralism"*; free elections are *"inconceivable without the participation of a plurality of political parties representing the different shades of opinion to be found within a country's population"*; *"only convincing and compelling reasons can justify restrictions on such parties' freedom of association"* (*United Communist Party of Turkey and Others v. Turkey*, Grand Chamber, 30 January 1998, §§43–46, unanimous violation of Article 11; [HUDOC](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-58128)). The same reasoning was applied in *Socialist Party and Others v. Turkey* (25 May 1998; [HUDOC](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-58172)).
- A ban was upheld in *Refah Partisi v. Turkey* (2003) only because the party's aims were incompatible with *"fundamental democratic principles"* and it *"did not exclude recourse to force"* ([HUDOC](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-60936), §§98, 132).
- Article 3 of Protocol No. 1 requires elections *"under conditions which will ensure the free expression of the opinion of the people in the choice of the legislature"* ([Convention text](https://www.echr.coe.int/documents/d/echr/convention_ENG)).

These cases concern dissolving parties, not a monopoly on ballot labels; how the court would judge Article 30 is our inference, not a holding. But the direction is clear. **The design keeps an outside check that the default party settlement is likely to fail.** That tension is listed on the Open Questions page and is not resolved here.

### 1.5 How long leaving takes

Under Article 50(3) the Treaties cease to apply when a withdrawal agreement enters into force *"or, failing that, two years after the notification"*, unless unanimously extended ([TEU Art. 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/50)). The United Kingdom notified on 29 March 2017 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/prime-ministers-letter-to-donald-tusk-triggering-article-50)), left on 31 January 2020 ([EU (Withdrawal) Act 2018 s.20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/16/section/20/2020-01-30)), and its transition period ended on 31 December 2020 ([Withdrawal Agreement Art. 126](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/withdrawal-agreement/article/126)): about 34 months to exit and 45 months to the end of transition.

### 1.6 The legitimacy cost

Support for EU membership in the State was **82%** in 2025 and 2026, and 76% in Northern Ireland ([08 §10](08-ireland-today.md)). A majority for this Constitution is not a majority for leaving. The design's answer is that the adoption referendum must say, on the ballot paper, that adopting it means leaving the EU and the euro (Art. 111). Even so, **the republic would begin its life against a large and settled majority view.** That is a direct FM-17 risk and the site says so.

## 2. Leaving the euro

### 2.1 The law

- There is no way to leave the euro and stay in the EU. An ECB legal paper concludes that *"withdrawal from EMU without a parallel withdrawal from the EU would be legally impossible"* and that *"a Member State's exit from the EU would automatically posit its exit from EMU"* (Athanassiou, [ECB Legal Working Paper No. 10](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scplps/ecblwp10.pdf), 2009). The same paper notes that a former member could keep *using* the euro, as Montenegro does, without a seat at the ECB. The design rejects that option because it would leave the republic with no lender of last resort and no monetary policy.
- **Lex monetae.** *"Where obligations in a contract are expressed in a particular currency, the general rule is that the law of the country of that currency will decide what constitutes its lawful currency at the time of payment"* ([Hogan Lovells, 2015](https://www.hoganlovells.com/~/media/hogan-lovells/pdf/publication/eurozonefinancecheckbroe_pdf.pdf)). Contracts under Irish law (wages, prices, rents, most mortgages and deposits in Irish banks) can be converted by Irish statute. **Contracts under foreign law may stay in euro**, because *"the lex monetae of the euro is the EU legislation which introduced the single currency"* (same source; and [Clifford Chance, 2015](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2015/05/the-greek-debt-crisis-and-eurobond-documentation.pdf), on Greece).
- **Irish government bonds** are now issued under Irish law, for example: *"The bond and all non-contractual obligations arising out of or in connection with it shall be governed by Irish law"* ([NTMA offering circular, 2036 bond, January 2026](https://assets.ntma.ie/general/Final-Offering-Circular-3.10-Treasury-Bond-2036.pdf)). They also carry the euro-area model collective action clauses. Some older programmes were governed by English law ([Finance Dublin](https://www.financedublin.com/articles/22461/Deals+of+the+Year+2025%3A+Update+of+legal+framework+for+Irish+sovereign+debt+enabling+Government+bonds+to+be+governed+by+Irish+law)). **How much of the debt is under Irish law has not been established in this pass.** Redenominating Irish-law bonds is lawful in Irish terms, but bondholders and rating agencies would treat it as a default.
- **Debt size.** General government debt was **€209.9 billion** at end-2025 ([CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-gfsa/governmentfinancestatistics2025april2026/)), *"the equivalent of 33% of GDP or 62% of estimated GNI*"* ([NTMA](https://www.ntma.ie/business-areas/funding-and-debt-management/statistics)).
- **The Central Bank's TARGET balance** was **€81.8 billion** at the end of August 2026 (provisional), a net claim on the Eurosystem ([ECB data](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/data/TGB/M.IE.N.A094T.U2.EUR.E?lastNObservations=4&format=csvdata)). How that claim would be settled on exit has no precedent. It is a large bargaining chip in both directions.

### 2.2 Ireland has done this before

The Currency Act 1927 created *"a new unit of value, to be known as the Saorstát pound, which would be maintained at parity with the pound sterling"*; coins were issued on 12 December 1928. When the European Monetary System began on 13 March 1979, *"By 30 March, sterling breached the upper fluctuation limit … As an EMS member, the Irish pound could not follow."* The euro became the currency of the State from 1 January 1999, at **IR£0.787564 = €1**, and *"The Irish pound ceased to be legal tender on 9 February 2002"* (Kelly, ["The Irish Pound: From Origins to EMU"](https://centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/consumer-hub-library/the-irish-pound-from-origins-to-emu.pdf?sfvrsn=2), Central Bank of Ireland *Quarterly Bulletin*, 2003). The 1979 break with sterling is the closest Irish precedent for a currency separation, and it was managed.

### 2.3 Currency separations and their costs

| Case | What happened | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Czechoslovakia, 1993** | Monetary separation *"at midnight on 7–8 February 1993 by affixing of banknote stamps"*; cash exchange limited to 4,000 koruna a person ([National Bank of Slovakia](https://www.nbs.sk/en/banknotes-and-coins/slovak-currency)). The common currency was meant to last six months but ended after five weeks, after *"large capital outflows from Slovak to Czech banks"* ([Brocek, Fraser of Allander](https://www.strath.ac.uk/media/1newwebsite/departmentsubject/economics/fraser/vol42no3/FAI_Economic_Perspectives-Monetary_and_fiscal.pdf)) | The Czech National Bank later called it an *"absolute 'non-event'"* for most people ([CNB](https://www.cnb.cz/en/cnb-news/press-releases/4f948865-3a7c-11e8-a804-5254004e4603)). The Slovak koruna was devalued 10% in July 1993 ([NBS](https://nbs.sk/en/monetary-policy/history-of-nbs-monetary-policy/1993-1999-independent-mp-of-the-nbs/)). Estimated dissolution costs: 2.1% of Czech and 5.7% of Slovak GDP (Brocek, citing Šujan and Šujanová) |
| **UK, 1992** (ERM exit) | Sterling fell about 15% | *"the pass-through of import prices at the retail level was limited"*; retail inflation averaged about 3% a year after 1993 (Haldane, [Bank of England, c.2000](https://www.bcb.gov.br/Pec/Depep/Seminarios/1999_ISemAnualMetasInflBCB/Arquivos/1999_SemMetasInfl_AndrewHaldane.pdf)) |
| **Argentina, 2001–02** | Partial deposit freeze (December 2001), end of convertibility (January 2002), *"forced conversion of dollar-denominated bank assets and liabilities into pesos at asymmetric rates"* | Peso from 1.00 to 3.32 per dollar by end-2002; GDP −11.0% in 2002; inflation 41% (December to December) ([IMF Independent Evaluation Office, 2004](https://www.imf.org/external/np/ieo/2003/arg/)) |

**Estimates for a euro exit** are old and vary widely. UBS (2011) put the cost for *"a weak Euro country leaving"* at *"40% to 50% of GDP in the first year"* ([UBS via Credit Writedowns](https://creditwritedowns.com/2011/09/eurozone-breakup-consequences.html)). Nomura (2011) estimated that the currencies of *"Ireland, Italy and Spain would all also fall by about 30%"* in a full break-up ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/b12kj4gt4z5xhh/new-drachma-to-plunge-new-deutschemark-only-currency-to-escape-euro-break-up-unscathed)). Neither fits today's Ireland well: the State now runs budget and external surpluses and is a TARGET creditor, which argues for a smaller fall; but leaving the EU at the same time removes tariff-free market access for the multinational sector, which argues for a larger one. **No current estimate of an Irish devaluation was found.** The economy section treats it as a range, not a number.

### 2.4 How a fall in the currency reaches prices

- Imports made up **33%** of Ireland's total supply of goods and services in 2022 (€505 billion), though this gross figure is inflated by multinational trade ([CSO Supply and Use Tables 2022](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-sauio/supplyanduseandinput-outputtablesforireland2022)). A household-level import share has not been sourced in this pass.
- The Central Bank estimates that pass-through from the euro–sterling rate to consumer goods prices *"is over 15 per cent after 12 months, settling at approximately 21 per cent in the long term"*, and that *"78 per cent of Food, Beverages and Tobacco imported from outside the EA come from the UK"* ([Reddan and Rice, Central Bank Economic Letter 2017/8](https://edit.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/publications/economic-letters/vol-2017-no-8-exchange-rate-pass-through-to-domestic-prices-(reddan-and-rice).pdf?sfvrsn=4)).
- **Energy is the exposed flank.** Ireland's energy import dependency was **78.2%** in 2025; it imports all its oil and coal and 82.3% of its gas (SEAI, seen via search results: [National Energy Balance](https://www.seai.ie/resources/seai-statistics/key-publications/national-energy-balance/)). A weaker currency makes every barrel and therm dearer at once. This is why the design's transition sequence puts domestic renewables and a priority list for energy, food and medicine imports before exit day (Art. 114).

## 3. Capital controls: how they have actually performed

| Case | Imposed | Lifted | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iceland** | 28 November 2008 ([Central Bank of Iceland rules No. 1082](https://cb.is/news-and-publications/article/2008-12-01-Rules-on-Foreign-Exchange--No--1082--November-28--2008), via search result) | For individuals and firms, March 2017 ([Government of Iceland](https://www.stjornarradid.is/frettir/grein/2017-03-12-Iceland-Lifts-Capital-Controls/)); in full only with the Foreign Exchange Act of 2021 (search result) | The króna fell 65% on a trade-weighted basis; inflation peaked at 18.6% in January 2009 ([IMF 2013](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2015/09/14/01/49/pr13300)). The IMF found the controls *"unavoidable"* and that they *"helped avoid a much deeper contraction that would have occurred with reliance on higher interest rates"* ([IMF ex post evaluation, 2012](https://www.sedlabanki.is/library/Skraarsafn/AGS/Iceland-Ex-Post-Evaluation.pdf)) |
| **Cyprus** | Banks shut 16–28 March 2013; €300 a day withdrawal limit, the first controls in the euro's history ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/446326)) | All remaining restrictions abolished April 2015 ([Neocleous](https://www.neocleous.com/articles/news/lifting-of-all-remaining-currency-controls), search result) | Two years, inside the euro |
| **Greece** | 29 June 2015; banks shut three weeks; €60 a day limit ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/banks-reopen-in-greece-after-three-week-shutdown-85648)) | 1 September 2019 ([VOA/AP](https://www.voanews.com/a/europe_greece-ends-crisis-era-capital-controls/6174987.html)) | Four years, inside the euro |

**What this tells the design.** Controls work as a brake in a crisis. Even the IMF, once their chief critic, accepted Iceland's. But **every one of these "temporary" controls lasted years**: about two in Cyprus, four in Greece, eight to thirteen in Iceland. That is the capital-control version of FM-15. Article 61 therefore makes controls ordinary published law that lapses unless renewed every six months, rather than an emergency power, and the stress tests show the renewal vote being harder than it looks.

**The exit-announcement problem.** Article 50 gives up to two years' public notice before the euro can be replaced. Every saver with euro deposits in an Irish bank has a reason to move them abroad before they are converted into a currency expected to fall. The Czech and Slovak separation saw exactly this in the weeks before the split (Brocek, above), and they shortened their planned six months to five weeks. **Some form of capital control during the notice period is close to unavoidable.** Cyprus and Greece show that a member state can impose controls inside the euro in a crisis, with the Commission's acquiescence; whether it would accept them from a state that has given notice to leave is untested.

## 4. Trade and the land border from outside

### 4.1 Who Ireland trades with

- **Goods, 2025.** Exports were a record **€260.3 billion**, imports **€144.0 billion**. The United States took **42.9%** of goods exports (€111.7 billion); the EU27 took €86.8 billion (about a third), Great Britain €15.1 billion and Northern Ireland €5.4 billion. **Medical and pharmaceutical products were 53.2%** of goods exports ([CSO, Goods Exports and Imports December 2025](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-gei/goodsexportsandimportsdecember2025/keyfindings); [RTÉ](https://rte.ie/news/business/2026/0217/1558929-cso-export-and-import-figures/)).
- **Services, 2024.** Exports were €483.0 billion, of which computer services €278.7 billion; exports to the UK were €52.7 billion and to the US €62.3 billion ([CSO, International Trade in Services 2024](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-its/internationaltradeinservices2024/)). No separate EU27 figure was found.
- **Agri-food, 2025.** Food, drink and horticulture exports reached **€19 billion**: **€7.1 billion (37%) to the EU** and **€6.7 billion (35%) to the UK**. Beef exports were over €3.4 billion and dairy €7.3 billion ([RTÉ on Bord Bia, January 2026](https://rte.ie/news/business/2026/0107/1551955-bordbia-irish-exports/)). Of dairy, about €3 billion (42%) went to the EU; of beef, about €1.6 billion each to the UK and the EU ([Irish Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/agribusiness/news/irish-food-and-drink-exports-reach-19bn-899358)).

**The pattern matters.** The multinational exports that dominate the totals go mainly to the US, and their fate turns on the companies' own decisions (the economy section models them as a range). **The trade shock from leaving the EU lands hardest on Irish-owned firms, above all food.**

### 4.2 The tariffs Irish food would face

On WTO most-favoured-nation terms, with no agreement:

| Product | EU tariff | UK tariff |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh boneless beef | 12.8% + €303.4 per 100 kg | 12% + £253 per 100 kg |
| Cheddar | €167.1 per 100 kg | £139 per 100 kg |
| Butter | €189.6 per 100 kg | £158 per 100 kg |

Sources: [AHDB beef](https://www.ahdb.org.uk/eu-and-uk-import-tariff-rates-for-selected-beef-products) and [dairy](https://ahdb.org.uk/eu-and-uk-import-tariff-rates-for-selected-dairy-products) tables; [UK Trade Tariff](https://www.trade-tariff.service.gov.uk/commodities/0201300090). A 2017 Oireachtas reply put the ad valorem equivalents at *over 60%* for beef and *over 50%* for dairy (seen in a search result only: [Oireachtas, 9 March 2017](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2017-03-09/272/)).

**The closest modelling is for Brexit, not for an Irish exit.** For the Irish government, Copenhagen Economics found that in a WTO scenario Irish GDP would be about **7% lower by 2030** than otherwise, with output down 23% in beef, 21% in processed food and 18% in dairy; beef exports would be 35% lower and dairy exports 40% lower, most of the dairy fall being due to regulatory divergence rather than tariffs ([Copenhagen Economics for DBEI, 2018](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/publications/publication-files/ireland-and-the-impacts-of-brexit.pdf)). An Irish exit would hit the same sectors in the EU market rather than the British one. The study is an analogy, not a forecast.

### 4.3 Every model of access has a price

| Model | What access it gives | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| **EEA** (Norway) | Single market, except agriculture and fisheries | The four freedoms, *including capital*; EU competition rules; it *"generally prohibits any measures that can be identified as state aid"* ([Commission](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/content/european-economic-area-eea-agreement)) |
| **Switzerland** | Sectoral access by bilateral agreements; new package signed 2 March 2026 ([Swiss government](https://www.europa.eda.admin.ch/en/package-switzerland-eu)) | Dynamic alignment in covered sectors, references to the Court of Justice, and *"the same State aid compatibility rules as those applicable in the EU"* (Commission Q&A, via a mirror) |
| **Customs union** (Turkey) | Industrial goods only, *"limited to products other than agricultural products"* | The EU's common external tariff and commercial policy ([Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/turkey-customs-unions-and-preferential-arrangements_en)) |
| **Trade agreement** (UK–EU TCA) | *"zero tariffs and zero quotas on all goods that comply with the appropriate rules of origin"* ([Commission](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/content/eu-uk-trade-and-cooperation-agreement)) | *"an effective system of subsidy control"* in which subsidies must remedy *"an identified market failure"* or *"an equity rationale"*, and can be met by "rebalancing" tariffs where divergence has a *"material impact"* on trade ([Senedd Research](https://research.senedd.wales/media/f4xdzbrp/22-35-tca-level-playing-field-en.pdf)) |

**None of these is compatible with Part V as written.** The least restrictive, a TCA-style agreement, is the obvious target. Its subsidy-control principles are looser than EU state-aid law, and "equity rationale" gives room for support to co-operatives, but the EU could still answer the republic's support for co-ops and the public bank with rebalancing tariffs. That is why Art. 104.4 makes any such commitment clear the bar for amending the core.

### 4.4 The Common Travel Area survives

The CTA *"dates back to 1922"* and *"is not specifically established in any bilateral treaty"* ([ByrneWallace](https://www.byrnewallace.com/news-and-recent-work/publications/brexit-cta-commitment-ire-uk.html)). The two governments' Memorandum of Understanding, *"Signed in duplicate at London on 8 May 2019"*, states that the CTA and its rights *"are separate from, and therefore not dependent on, EU citizenship or EU membership"*, covering residence, work, social security, healthcare, social housing, *"all levels of education"* and voting in *"local and national parliamentary"* elections. It is *"not of itself intended to create legally binding obligations"* ([MoU text](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5cd29d56ed915d50b5a206d1/CTA-MoU-UK.pdf)). **So people can keep crossing the land border and the Irish Sea without checks, provided both governments keep the arrangement.**

### 4.5 The land border becomes the EU's frontier

- Under the Windsor Framework (announced 27 February 2023), *"The Union's Customs Code … applies to all goods entering or exiting Northern Ireland"*, to protect *"the integrity of the EU's Single Market for goods"* ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/relations-united-kingdom/eu-uk-withdrawal-agreement/windsor-framework_en); [Institute for Government](https://instituteforgovernment.org.uk/explainer/windsor-framework)).
- EU law requires animals and animal products from outside the Union to be checked *"at the border control post of first arrival into the Union"* (search result: [Reg. 2019/1012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2019/1012)).
- **So goods crossing from the republic into Northern Ireland would be entering the EU's goods area from a third country.** The checks would fall on the northern side, run by UK authorities applying EU rules, whatever the republic itself does. **No published analysis of an Irish exit was found**; this is our reading of the rules, not an established finding.
- The scale: officials from both jurisdictions counted **208** crossing points ([Irish Examiner, 2018](https://www.irishexaminer.com/ireland/officials-had-nightmare-task-in-identifying-208-north-border-crossings-469931.html)); a Defence Forces and Garda exercise reportedly found almost 300 (search result). Cross-border trade in goods was **€5.5 billion in the first half of 2026** ([InterTradeIreland](https://intertradeireland.com/insights)).
- **The ways to soften it all cost something.** A customs union and full alignment on food standards with the EU would remove most checks, but would bind the republic to EU rules it left in order to escape. Trusted-trader schemes and checks away from the line reduce friction but do not remove it. The whole Brexit negotiation of 2017–2023 was about avoiding exactly this frontier, because of what border infrastructure meant during the Troubles.

### 4.6 Citizenship

- Under Article 20 TFEU, *"Every person holding the nationality of a Member State shall be a citizen of the Union."* An Irish exit ends EU citizenship for every Irish citizen, **including 614,300 people in Northern Ireland (32.3%) who held an Irish passport** in 2021, up from 375,800 in 2011 ([NISRA Census 2021](https://www.nisra.gov.uk/system/files/statistics/census-2021-main-statistics-for-northern-ireland-phase-1-statistical-bulletin-passports-held.pdf)). For many in the North, Irish citizenship has become their route to EU rights since Brexit. **An Irish exit takes that away from them without their having a vote in the State's referendum.** That bears directly on the Q3 answer.
- About **91,000 Irish citizens** live in the twenty EU states that report to Eurostat; six do not report ([Eurostat migr_pop1ctz, 1 January 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/migr_pop1ctz?citizen=IE&age=TOTAL&sex=T&unit=NR); our sum). Their right to stay would depend on their host states.
- **312,909 citizens of other EU countries** lived in the State in 2022 ([CSO Census 2022](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp5/censusofpopulation2022profile5-diversitymigrationethnicityirishtravellersreligion/citizenship/)). Article 104.5 guarantees their rights for life.

### 4.7 The North and the all-island offer

The owner's Q3 answer is the 26 counties first, with a standing offer of an all-island constitutional convention (Art. 3). Leaving the EU changes that answer's context in three ways:

1. **It makes unity harder to agree.** Northern Ireland voted 55.8% to remain in 2016 ([CAIN, Ulster University](https://cain.ulster.ac.uk/issues/politics/election/2016ref/2016-06-23_ref.htm)), and its Irish passport-holders now gain EU rights through the State. An all-island convention would have to decide whether a united Ireland is inside or outside the EU. That is a question the republic's own choice prejudges, and a reason for unionists and many nationalists alike to say no.
2. **It hands both nationalisms a grievance.** Border checks, even on the northern side, are what the peace process was built to avoid. FM-14 warns that the national question can swallow class politics, and a new frontier gives it something physical to gather around.
3. **The convention offer must stay genuinely open.** Article 3.2 already lets a convention propose *"any constitution for the island, including one that differs from this Constitution"*. That must include one inside the EU. The site says this plainly rather than pretending exit and unity point the same way.

## 5. What is gained

- **Monetary sovereignty.** The republic sets its own interest rate and exchange-rate policy, and has a central bank that can act as lender of last resort to its own banks and, within a limit voted by the Assembly, lend to the State (Art. 61).
- **Capital controls** as an ordinary, lawful instrument (no Art. 63 TFEU).
- **Freedom from state-aid and procurement rules.** Support for co-operatives, the public bank and municipal enterprise, and procurement that favours them, are matters for the republic's own law, subject to whatever a trade agreement trades away (§4.3).
- **No EU fiscal rules** (the net expenditure path; [08 §10](08-ireland-today.md)). The discipline that replaces them is domestic: hard budgets (Art. 54), open books (Art. 59), and the Central Bank's lending ceiling.
- **The budget contribution.** Ireland has been *"a net contributor to the EU budget"* since 2013, contributing around €3.6 billion in 2022 ([Comptroller and Auditor General](https://www.audit.gov.ie/media/pbxobe35/2-reporting-ireland-s-eu-transactions.pdf)); one MEP puts the 2024 net payment at about €800 million ([The Parliament Magazine](https://www.theparliamentmagazine.eu/news/article/oped-how-ireland-can-broker-agreement-on-the-next-eu-budget)). **But** nearly €7.5 billion of the €9.8 billion CAP plan for 2023–27 comes from the EU budget ([European Commission](https://ireland.representation.ec.europa.eu/strategy-and-priorities/key-eu-policies-ireland/agriculture-and-rural-development_en)). The republic must replace that from its own budget, and Article 67 promises farm incomes on top. **The net fiscal gain is small, and may be negative.**
- **Fisheries.** Ireland's waters within 200 nautical miles cover about 450,000 km² ([Marine Ireland](https://marine-ireland.ie/strengths/marine)). The Marine Institute estimates Irish vessels take 35.5% by weight and 38.8% by value of landings from the Irish exclusive economic zone (seen in a search result only: [Oireachtas, 2021](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2021-06-23/303)). Outside the Common Fisheries Policy, the republic controls access to its waters. That is a real gain. Using it would need a fleet and processing capacity, and would cost goodwill with the EU states whose boats fish there now.

## 6. What this means for the design

1. **The decision is driven by the party settlement**, and the site says so plainly. Under the Q1 alternatives the political reason weakens and only the economic one remains.
2. **The costs are large and front-loaded**: a currency that will fall, a run on deposits during the notice period, and import prices rising fastest for energy and food. The transition sequence (Art. 114) has to be built around them.
3. **Capital controls will be needed and will be hard to end.** The design treats them as law with a six-monthly sunset, not as an emergency power.
4. **The trade shock falls on Irish-owned firms, especially food**, and every model of market access costs part of Part V. The land border becomes the EU's frontier for goods.
5. **The ECHR problem remains** after leaving the EU.
6. **Legitimacy is the deepest risk.** Leaving goes against roughly four in five voters, and it strips EU citizenship from Irish citizens in the North who have no vote on it. The design can make the choice explicit on the ballot; it cannot make it popular.

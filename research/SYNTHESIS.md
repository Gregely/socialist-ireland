# SYNTHESIS — How socialist states fail, and what might stop it

This is the backbone of the project. There are 18 failure modes (FM-16 was expanded and FM-17 and FM-18 were added in the second pass). Each **failure mode** gets a stable ID (FM-xx). In Phase 2 every institution's *Safeguards* block will reference these IDs, and the site's "How it fails" section and institution map will key off them.

For each failure mode:

- **Cases**: where it happened, with links to the detailed research files.
- **Root causes**: the mechanism, not just the story.
- **What the literature suggests**: preventive mechanisms with a precedent, or a strong theoretical argument.
- **Residual tension**: why the fix is not free.

## Part A — Failure modes

### FM-01 · Bureaucratic caste / the "new class"

**Cases.**

- The USSR from the 1920s on: the bureaucracy as "policeman" over scarcity (Trotsky).
- Yugoslavia: the party-state as collective controller of property (Djilas).
- The Soviet nomenklatura: appointment lists and privileges in kind (Voslensky).
- The German SPD before 1914, showing this happens in legal democratic parties too (Michels).

→ [01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)

**Root causes.**

1. Scarcity gives whoever distributes goods discretionary power.
2. *Control* rights over social property are concentrated even when legal *ownership* is collective.
3. Appointment from above makes careers depend on loyalty upward.
4. Privileges paid in kind are invisible and add up over time.
5. Expertise, information and members' limited time push power toward full-time officials (Michels).

**What the literature suggests.**

- Officials paid at a skilled worker's wage. Precedents: the Paris Commune and Mondragon's 1:3 to 1:6 ratio ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Pay in money only; officials use the same public services as everyone else; public declarations of assets.
- Right of recall and short terms (Commune).
- Mandatory rotation (Athens; parecon's balanced job complexes).
- Selection by lot for oversight and some executive bodies. Sortition is the most direct answer to careerism (Athens, Ostbelgien, Ireland's assemblies).
- Election from below or open competition. No party-controlled appointment lists.
- Open books: public accounts, plan data and budgets (Cybersyn-style open data, [03](03-planning-and-calculation.md)).
- Universal provision of necessities, so there are fewer queues to police (Trotsky's own logic).

**Residual tension.** Rotation and lot cost expertise and continuity. Capped pay may drive skilled people to emigrate, which in Ireland's open labour market is a real risk. Removing visible privilege can push it into informal forms: access, favours, networks.

### FM-02 · Monopoly of power and suppression of pluralism

**Cases.**

- Russia 1918–21 ([02](02-suppression-of-pluralism.md)): the Constituent Assembly dissolved, the Mensheviks and SRs expelled from the soviets, factory committees subordinated, Kronstadt crushed, factions banned.
- Cuba's 2019 process: mass consultation, but no organised alternatives allowed ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Rojava: confederal councils alongside a dominant armed party that detained critics, according to HRW and Amnesty ([06](06-positive-models.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Real emergencies (civil war, intervention, sabotage) make suppression seem reasonable.
2. A theory that the party embodies the class makes opposition look like treason.
3. Each restriction removes a channel through which the next one could have been resisted.
4. "Temporary" measures have no expiry date.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Luxemburg: freedom for "the one who thinks differently" is the "life element" of socialist democracy, not a concession ([02](02-suppression-of-pluralism.md)).
- Entrenched political rights that a simple majority cannot suspend.
- Several channels of legitimacy (councils, assemblies, courts, referendums), so that capturing one does not mean capturing all.
- The right to form platforms or factions inside any governing organisation. The 1921 ban was the hinge.
- Self-management at work protected by the constitution, federated through *its own* structures.

**Residual tension.** Real pluralism includes parties that want to restore capitalism. Allowing them risks restoration by ballot. Banning them risks FM-02 itself. **This is the party question, and it is flagged for the user** (Part C).

### FM-03 · Personality cult and executive concentration

**Cases.** Stalin; Khrushchev's 1956 report blaming "the cult" while sparing the system ([01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)). Later examples in other states are not researched in this pass.

**Root causes.**

1. A single executive office with independent authority.
2. No rival centres of authority (a free press, independent courts, opposition) to puncture the myth.
3. The organisation's propaganda resources are aimed at one person.
4. Term limits are absent or can be changed by the person they apply to.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Collective, rotating chairs. The Commune was "executive and legislative at the same time"; Rojava uses co-chairs.
- Hard term limits that cannot be extended for the people currently in office.
- No honours for living people: no places or institutions named after them. *My proposal, untested.*
- A plural media system (FM-04 and the media section).

**Residual tension.** Collective leadership can be slow and indecisive in a crisis (see the stress tests). Charisma can't be legislated away; a popular figure *outside* office can still dominate.

### FM-04 · Security-state capture ("state within the state")

**Cases.**

- The Cheka (1917): set up to investigate and refer cases, it quickly took extrajudicial powers and was answerable only to the party ([01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)).
- The NKVD's quota-driven Great Terror: 681,692 executions recorded in official figures, broadly matching historians' estimates ([01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)).
- The Stasi: 91,015 staff and 173,081 registered informants in 1989 ([01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)).
- Rojava's Asayish: arbitrary detentions ([06](06-positive-models.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Emergency origins and mission creep.
2. Extrajudicial powers.
3. Secrecy that keeps out the people meant to oversee it.
4. Accountability to the leadership and not to plural institutions.
5. Files on the overseers themselves.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Separate policing, intelligence and counter-intelligence.
- No powers of arrest or detention for intelligence services.
- All coercive powers only under judicial warrant.
- Oversight by a committee that is led by the opposition and includes members chosen by lot, with full access.
- Automatic sunset of emergency powers, with supermajority renewal.
- A right to see one's own file. The post-1990 German Stasi Records Act is the precedent; it needs sourcing.
- The Commune's police as a "responsible, and at all times revocable, agent" ([06](06-positive-models.md)).

**Residual tension.** A democratic socialist Ireland would face real hostile intelligence activity (FM-10). A security service too weak to counter it is a vulnerability too. Oversight bodies can themselves be captured or co-opted.

### FM-05 · Planning breakdown (the knowledge problem)

**Cases.**

- The Soviet planning system's chronic mismatch of supply and demand ([03](03-planning-and-calculation.md), [04](04-stagnation-and-work.md)).
- The Aral Sea: centrally planned cotton irrigation, with large water losses from poorly built canals, as an ecological example of planners ignoring local knowledge. ([Wikipedia mirror](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/a/Aral_Sea.htm); [Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/environment/will-the-aral-sea-ever-come-back-28914). These are weak sources and need a scholarly one.)

**Root causes.**

1. Hayek: relevant knowledge is local, tacit and fleeting, and cannot be centralised.
2. Mises: without prices for producer goods, comparing costs is hard.
3. Planners have no feedback, or get falsified feedback when targets are tied to rewards.
4. One centre is a single point of failure.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Use **layered coordination**, not one mechanism:
  - market exchange for most consumer goods (Lange, Nove);
  - negotiated coordination for investment and capacity (Devine);
  - public planning with open data for networks and universal services (Cockshott & Cottrell, Cybersyn);
  - political caps for ecological limits.
- Real-time open data (Cybersyn).
- Decentralised participatory budgeting for local capital (Kerala, Porto Alegre).
- Iterative proposals, where they work at small scale (parecon).

**Residual tension.**

- Markets bring back inequality and some capitalist dynamics, as Yugoslavia showed. Parecon-style iteration is untested at scale. Computational planning answers Mises better than Hayek.
- **Honest summary:** the Austrians won the argument about Soviet-type central planning. The argument about democratic layered coordination is open, not won.

### FM-06 · Soft budget constraints and chronic shortage

**Cases.**

- Soviet-type economies (Kornai, *Economics of Shortage*, 1980) ([03](03-planning-and-calculation.md)).
- Yugoslav enterprises.
- Mondragon/Fagor 2013, which shows the *opposite* choice: the co-ops refused a further rescue ([06](06-positive-models.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Loss-making units are always rescued, so their demand for inputs is "almost insatiable" and they hoard.
2. Local democratic pressure to protect jobs makes the problem *worse*, not better.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Hard budget constraints by default.
- Any rescue must be an explicit, costed, public vote, never automatic.
- The job guarantee is held by **society, not the enterprise**: the worker is protected and the failing unit is not. Mondragon's redeployment of over 1,000 members is the precedent.
- Inter-cooperative solidarity funds with clear rules.

**Residual tension.** Hard budgets bring back insecurity and the disruption of firms closing. Communities built around one employer (a meat plant, a data centre) will resist, and democratic bodies will be tempted to give in.

### FM-07 · Stagnation, apathy and disengagement from work

**Cases.**

- The Brezhnev era: growth fell from about 5% to under 2%; "they pretend to pay us…", whose origin is unverified ([04](04-stagnation-and-work.md)).
- Stakhanovism as the *wrong* answer: staged records, norms raised, workers divided ([04](04-stagnation-and-work.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Wages buy little when goods are scarce (FM-06).
2. Workers have no voice over their work.
3. Recognition is used to intensify work.
4. Nomenklatura promotion rewards loyalty over competence.
5. Innovation has no channel.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Effort must buy something real: available goods, *time* (shorter hours as productivity grows), voice.
- Collective, peer-nominated recognition, capped and never linked to norms.
- Job rotation and balanced job complexes (parecon).
- Pay compression with *some* differential for effort (Mondragon 1:6; parecon pays for "effort and sacrifice").
- Peer accountability with due process.
- Freedom to start new cooperatives, and public R&D with open licences.

**Residual tension.** Without fear of destitution, some people will work less. The design should *accept* a lower work intensity as a legitimate choice, with fewer hours for less consumption, rather than pretend everyone will be enthusiastic. Innovation incentives are weaker than in capitalism; this is a real cost to admit.

### FM-08 · Participation fatigue and the hollowing-out of participatory institutions

**Cases.**

- Porto Alegre PB after 2004: kept in name, emptied of money ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Kerala after 2001: undercut by the next government ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Irish citizens' assemblies: advisory, with selective follow-through ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Michels: members' limited time hands power to full-time officials ([01](01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md)).
- The parecon critique: "too many meetings" ([03](03-planning-and-calculation.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Participation costs time, and the time cost falls unevenly (care burdens, shift work).
2. Bodies without binding power lose credibility.
3. Participatory budgets set by the executive can be quietly cut.
4. Professionals end up controlling the agenda.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Paid civic time: delegate and assembly duty compensated at full wage, like jury service.
- Sortition, so participation is not limited to the most motivated activists (which also answers Michels).
- A **constitutionally fixed share** of capital budgets for local assemblies (Kerala's 35–40%, but entrenched).
- Binding powers for some assemblies (agenda-setting, as in Ostbelgien; veto or referral).
- Delegation that can be recalled, so not everyone has to attend everything.
- Short working weeks, so people actually have time to take part.

**Residual tension.** Entrenching participatory budgets limits future majorities. Binding power for bodies chosen by lot raises questions about their legitimacy and accountability. Some people simply don't want to take part, and the design must not punish that.

### FM-09 · Insider capture of self-management

**Cases.**

- Yugoslavia: firms maximised income per member and avoided hiring. Unemployment reached about 15% by 1985, with Slovenia at 2.5% and Kosovo at 21% (1965), and regional divergence ([04](04-stagnation-and-work.md), [06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Mondragon: only about 40% of workers are member-owners. Subsidiary and contract workers were not protected when Fagor failed ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Irish co-operative creameries converted into PLCs (e.g. Kerry, Glanbia). *Needs sourcing; flagged in [07](07-irish-lineage.md).*

**Root causes.**

1. Enterprise-level democracy serves the people inside, not those outside.
2. Rules on surplus distribution reward a small membership.
3. Members can vote to demutualise and cash in.

**What the literature suggests.**

- An obligation to admit workers as members after a short probation, with no permanent second-class employees.
- **Indivisible reserves**: co-op assets that cannot be distributed to current members (an asset lock; Italian and Mondragon practice, *needs sourcing*).
- Society holds ultimate title to the means of production; enterprises hold *use rights*.
- Investment allocated partly by regional need, not just enterprise surplus.
- A job guarantee outside the enterprise.

**Residual tension.** Social title combined with enterprise use rights repeats the Djilas problem (FM-01) one level down. Mandatory membership weakens the sense of ownership members feel.

### FM-10 · Counter-revolution from outside or from the right

**Cases.**

- Chile 1970–73: "make the economy scream"; CIA funding of the opposition; the truck-owners' strike; the coup ([05](05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md)).
- The Irish soviets suppressed by the Free State army and by IRA units, 1920–23 ([07](07-irish-lineage.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. Domestic capital and foreign powers have strong motives and the means to act.
2. The military's loyalty is uncertain.
3. Economic crisis erodes support among the middle classes.
4. Popular organisations and government strategy split (the cordones vs Popular Unity).

**What the literature suggests.**

- Legitimacy through constitutional process and supermajorities. This makes the defenders the "constitutionalists".
- Civilian and democratic control of the armed forces. Citizen-soldier models; the Commune's armed people.
- Transparency on foreign funding, applied evenly to all sides.
- Contingency planning to keep production going (Cybersyn, cordones).
- Workers' councils already established *before* a crisis, coordinated with but not subordinate to government.

**Residual tension.** Every measure against counter-revolution can become FM-02 or FM-04. **"Counter-revolution" must never become a legal category usable against peaceful political activity.** Hungary 1956 and Czechoslovakia 1968 are the warning.

### FM-11 · External blockade, sanctions and capital flight

**Cases.**

- Cuba (the UN has voted against the US embargo every year since 1992; 187–2 in 2024).
- Nicaragua (ICJ 1986).
- Chile ([05](05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md)).
- Ireland-specific exposure: foreign multinationals pay 87% of corporation tax; the top 10 firms pay 56%; IDA clients employ 312,468 people ([08](08-ireland-today.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. A small open economy depends on outside capital, markets and inputs (energy, feed, fertiliser).
2. Booked profits can move instantly; real activity can move fairly quickly.
3. Single-market and EU legal constraints ([08](08-ireland-today.md)).

**What the literature suggests.**

- *Staged* transition, not a big bang.
- Ring-fence windfall revenues *before* any change.
- Build domestic capacity in parallel.
- Food and energy security as strategic priorities. Ireland's renewables potential is an asset here.
- Diversify trade partners and pursue regional alliances.
- Use the legal room the EU already allows (Art. 345 TFEU; SGEI rules).
- Plan explicitly which multinational activities to keep under negotiated terms.

**Residual tension.** The transition will likely make Ireland poorer in GDP terms, at least for a period, and probably in GNI\* terms as well. The design must say so and argue that the trade is worth it (security, housing, health, time). It must not promise otherwise.

### FM-12 · Restoration from inside

**Cases.** The USSR 1989–91, read as the party-state elite turning control into ownership (Kotz & Weir; Trotsky's 1936 prediction) ([05](05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md)).

**Root causes.** A class that controls but does not own has a motive to convert control into ownership when the chance comes (Djilas plus Kotz & Weir).

**What the literature suggests.**

- Spread-out, visible control rights, so that no group is positioned to take over.
- Privatisation of social property only by supermajority *and* referendum.
- Open registers of who controls what.
- Ban on officials holding interests in enterprises they oversee, during their term and for a cooling-off period after it.

**Residual tension.** Entrenching social ownership also entrenches it against legitimate democratic change. Where is the line between protecting socialism and stopping the people from changing their minds? **Flagged for the user** (Part C).

---

### FM-13 · Factional splits and sectarian fragmentation

**Cases.**

- The 1921 *ban* on factions as the wrong cure ([02](02-suppression-of-pluralism.md)).
- The Sino-Soviet split: ideology, national interest and personalities, ending in border clashes in 1969 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_relations_from_1969_to_1991); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/sino-soviet-tensions-mount-along-ussuri-river-border)).
- The Irish left's own history: Sinn Féin's split into Official and Provisional in 1970; the Workers' Party split into the WP and Democratic Left in 1992 ([Wikipedia, Democratic Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Left_(Ireland)); [Irish Left Archive](https://leftarchive.ie/calendar/on-this-day/02/22)).

**Root causes.**

1. Disagreements about strategy become questions of identity.
2. There are no institutional means of living with disagreement.
3. Winner-takes-all control of the organisation's resources.
4. The leadership treats dissent as disloyalty.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Legitimate, protected internal platforms. *Allowing* factions makes splits less likely than banning them.
- Proportional representation in internal bodies.
- Shared resources split proportionally if a split happens anyway.
- Decision rules that protect minorities (supermajorities on core questions).
- The state *not* tied to any single organisation, so that splits within a party do not become splits within the state.

**Residual tension.** Too much tolerance of factions can paralyse decisions. There is no evidence-based optimum; the design picks a point and says so.

### FM-14 · Nationalist capture

**Cases.**

- Yugoslavia: political monopoly combined with economic decentralisation by republic led to republic-level elites channelling national grievance; the SANU Memorandum (1986) and Milošević ([06](06-positive-models.md)).
- Ireland's own: independence without social change, as Connolly's 1897 warning foresaw; the Democratic Programme abandoned as "poetry" ([07](07-irish-lineage.md)).

**Root causes.**

1. When class politics fails to deliver, national identity takes over as the organising grievance.
2. Uneven regional development (FM-09).
3. Elites under threat look for a new basis of legitimacy.
4. The symbols of the movement and the nation run together.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Civic, not ethnic, definitions of membership.
- Regional equalisation built into investment allocation.
- Protection for minorities and multiple identities. The GFA principle that people may identify as British, Irish or both is directly relevant ([08](08-ireland-today.md)).
- Language rights grounded in *material* support, not symbolism.
- Avoid one-party structures in which national and class leadership merge.

**Residual tension.** The project is *named* in Irish and grounded in Irish republican history (1919, Connolly). That is a strength (legitimacy) and a danger (it can read as nationalism to unionists, and drift toward it). **The North is flagged for the user** (Part C).

### FM-15 · Entrenchment of emergency powers (cross-cutting)

**Cases.** Almost every case above: the Cheka, the 1918 one-man management decree, the 1921 ban on factions "temporarily", the Brezhnev Doctrine's "defence of socialism". Outside the socialist tradition, **Weimar's Article 48** is the canonical case. The framers expected it to be used rarely. From 1930 it became the routine way to govern without a parliamentary majority (one count gives 66 decrees against five laws in 1932). The Reichstag could revoke decrees on paper, but in practice doing so triggered dissolution. It was used to depose the Prussian government in 1932, and the Reichstag Fire Decree of February 1933 completed the legal path to dictatorship ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_48_of_the_Weimar_Constitution); [Rempel, johndclare.net](https://johndclare.net/Rempel_Weimar23.htm)). The lesson: an emergency power whose *revocation* carries a penalty is not checked at all.

**Root causes.** Emergencies are real. Emergency powers have no automatic end. Those who hold them define when the emergency is over.

**What the literature suggests.**

- Automatic sunset (e.g. 30–90 days).
- Renewal by supermajority, with a sortition panel's concurrence.
- Courts can review whether the emergency is real.
- Certain rights can never be suspended: elections on schedule, habeas corpus, freedom of the press.
- Any extension of terms during an emergency is forbidden.

**Residual tension.** A real existential emergency (sabotage, an energy collapse) may need fast, sustained action. Sunset rules can be gamed by back-to-back declarations. The design needs rules against "rolling" emergencies.

### FM-16 · Ecological overshoot (productivism)

**Cases.**

- Soviet productivism. The Aral Sea: cotton irrigation, leaky canals and catastrophic shrinkage; Feshbach & Friendly, *Ecocide in the USSR* (1992) ([09](09-ecology-and-growth.md)). Exact volume figures are still unverified.
- Cuba's Special Period: degrowth by necessity. Calories fell from about 2,900 to about 1,800 a day, and urban agroecology filled part of the gap ([09](09-ecology-and-growth.md)).
- Ireland today ([09 §3](09-ecology-and-growth.md)):
  - agriculture is 38% of greenhouse-gas emissions, from a 6.9 m cattle herd;
  - data centres use 23% of metered electricity;
  - Dublin Airport's passenger cap is being lifted (36.4 m passengers last year);
  - transport is 21.7% of emissions;
  - food waste is 835,000 t;
  - forestry planting runs at about a third of target.

**Root causes.**

1. Output growth used as the measure of legitimacy, in planned and market economies alike.
2. No institution represents ecological limits with real power. The Soviet press and civil society could not raise the alarm.
3. Local livelihoods are tied to extractive sectors (the herd).
4. A political offer of "more stuff" that sufficiency cannot match (Huber's challenge to degrowth).
5. Physical limits set as prices only, so whoever can pay can still overshoot.

**What the literature suggests.** The positions differ ([09 §1](09-ecology-and-growth.md)):

- **Degrowth.** Hickel and Saito: planned reduction of aggregate throughput in rich countries. Hickel & Kallis (2020) find no evidence of absolute decoupling at the necessary speed.
- **Eco-modernist socialism.** Huber: electrify everything under public ownership and win the working class with material gains.
- **Half-Earth.** Vettese & Pendergrass: rewild half the land, a vegan diet and per-person energy quotas; no nuclear, no geoengineering.

What they share, and what the design can adopt without picking a side:

- binding **physical budgets**, set politically and enforced as quantities;
- an **independent ecological institution** with a suspensive veto (the piece Soviet planning lacked);
- **electrification first**;
- "**private sufficiency, public luxury**" as the political offer (Monbiot; Saito's redefinition of wealth);
- a **generous just transition for farmers**: guaranteed income, payment for stewardship, re-mutualised processing, representation by right.

**Residual tension.**

- Caps on the herd and data centres hit rural incomes and public revenue. Those are exactly the constituencies whose loss drives FM-14 and FM-11.
- Sufficiency pulls against variety and consumer pleasure (FM-17).
- **The throughput question is flagged for the user** (Part C, item 9).

### FM-17 · Legitimacy decay / "grey socialism"

**Cases** ([10](10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md)):

- **Late-Soviet ritualised participation.** Yurchak's "performative shift": people kept performing official language while its meaning stopped mattering, until a system that "was forever" suddenly wasn't.
- **The GDR's two-tier consumption.** Trabant waiting lists of 13 or more years; Intershop goods for hard currency only; Czechoslovakia's Tuzex.
- **The Kitchen Debate (1959).** Socialism let capitalism set the terms of the contest (consumer goods) and lost on them.
- **Poland 1970 and 1980.** Price rises set by the state turned everyday grievance into revolt against the state, ending in Solidarność.
- **A counter-intuitive datum.** Kern & Hainmueller (2009): East Germans *with* West German TV were *more* satisfied with the regime. They watched for entertainment, and enjoyment stabilised the system.

**Root causes.**

1. Rituals of participation emptied of consequence (links to FM-08).
2. Scarcity and poor quality in *visible, desirable* goods (FM-06).
3. Access by connection or hard currency, which is worse than equal scarcity.
4. Comparison with richer neighbours.
5. Administered prices make the state the target of every grievance.
6. Neglect of beauty and design; Morris's warning against an "efficient drabness" socialism like Bellamy's.
7. A sacrificial political offer that leaves desire to capitalism (Fisher).
8. Target and audit culture that teaches performed compliance. Fisher calls the capitalist version "market Stalinism".

**What the literature suggests.**

- Morris: pleasure in work and *"the ornamental part of life"* as core goals, not luxuries for later.
- Fisher: *"disarticulate desire from capitalism"*. Offer free time, collective joy and novelty, not sacrifice.
- Public luxury that is actually luxurious, with design budgets protected.
- Open, rule-based rationing where scarcity remains. **Never** a privilege or hard-currency tier.
- Markets kept where variety matters (food, clothing, music, craft, hospitality).
- Full cultural openness.
- Few binding assemblies rather than many symbolic ones.
- Price changes by published formula with compensation built in.
- Few, rotating, democratically chosen indicators instead of target culture.

**Residual tension.**

- Variety and consumer pleasure pull against ecological sufficiency (FM-16).
- Whether *public* abundance can satisfy people raised on *private* consumption is plausible but unproven.
- A democratic system can lose legitimacy and be voted out. The design must accept that rather than prevent it (FM-12 vs FM-02).

### FM-18 · Social-reproduction failure / the "double burden"

**Cases** ([11](11-social-reproduction.md)):

- **The promise.** Lenin (1919): housework *"crushes, strangles, stultifies and degrades her"*. Kollontai (1920): communal kitchens and central laundries.
- **The reversal.** Abortion was legalised in 1920, charged for from 1930 and banned in 1936 as a pronatalist measure. Births rose, and so did maternal and infant mortality.
- **The double burden.**
  - Soviet time-budget surveys (1920s and 1960s–70s) and the 1970s *Soviet Studies* debate: housework still fell overwhelmingly on women.
  - The GDR: 78–91% of women employed, with an extensive crèche network, yet housework stayed women's work.
  - The "reprivatisation" of women's lives from the 1970s.
- **Ireland.**
  - The Constitution's Art. 41.2 ("woman in the home") survived the 2024 care referendum, rejected 73.9% No, partly because the replacement offered no material guarantee.
  - 299,128 unpaid carers (61% women).
  - Childcare costs among Europe's highest.
  - No national time-use survey since 2005.
  - The marriage bar lasted until 1973–74.

**Root causes.**

1. Socialised care was underfunded relative to "productive" investment, because care did not count in the plan.
2. The state changed women's role and not men's.
3. Reproduction was treated as a demographic resource of the state.
4. Care is not measured, so it is not planned.
5. Leaderships were male (needs sourcing).

**What the literature suggests.**

- Count care: a regular official time-use survey, and care investment planned alongside production.
- Socialise what people *want* socialised, and fund it properly: universal childcare; good canteens and food halls (which meets FM-17 too); home care; well-paid, unionised care workers.
- Change men's role: a shorter standard week; non-transferable, well-paid parental leave for each parent; care counted as civic service.
- Reproductive autonomy as an entrenched constitutional right.
- A care constituency in planning bodies.
- An enforceable right to receive and give care, worded *with* carers' and disability organisations.
- Lineage: Ghodsee on economic independence; Wages for Housework and Federici; social reproduction theory (Bhattacharya); Ireland's own Thompson & Wheeler, *Appeal* (1825).

**Residual tension.**

- Socialised care can slide into institutionalisation, a fear sharpened by Ireland's history of laundries and mother-and-baby homes. Many people want to care for their own family members.
- "Choice with support" (public services *and* an income for home carers) is expensive.
- Paying for care risks turning family relationships into wage relationships, Federici's own dilemma.

## Part B — Cross-cutting principles that emerge

1. **Plural channels of legitimacy.** No single body (party, council, assembly or court) can capture the rest. Councils, sortition chambers, referendums, courts and a free press all check each other. (FM-01–04, FM-12.)
2. **Officials at the median.** Pay at a skilled worker's wage, in money only, under a capped ratio, with the same services as everyone and open asset declarations. (FM-01, FM-12.)
3. **Rotation, recall and lot by default.** Election only where expertise or continuity truly requires it, as Athens did with its generals. (FM-01, FM-03, FM-08.)
4. **Open books as infrastructure.** Accounts, plans and budgets are public; citizens can see their own files. (FM-01, FM-04, FM-05, FM-12.)
5. **Layered economic coordination.** Markets for most consumption, negotiation for investment, planning for networks, political caps for ecology. (FM-05, FM-06, FM-16.)
6. **Hard budgets, soft landings.** Enterprises can fail; people cannot fall into destitution. (FM-06, FM-07, FM-09.)
7. **Time as the reward.** Productivity gains go partly to shorter hours, which also makes participation possible. (FM-07, FM-08.)
8. **Entrenchment with exits.** Core rights and social ownership are protected by supermajority and referendum, and remain *changeable* by the people. (FM-02, FM-12, FM-15.)
9. **Sunset everything coercive.** (FM-04, FM-15.)
10. **Irish roots without nationalist capture.** 1919, the soviets, Connolly and Thompson as lineage; civic membership and GFA-style protection of identity. (FM-14.)
11. **Legality as defence.** Transition through Art. 46 and referendum; the 1937 plebiscite as precedent. (FM-10.)
12. **Physical limits as quantities, set democratically and watched independently.** Carbon, nitrogen, land and electricity budgets, with an ecological body holding a suspensive veto. (FM-05, FM-16.)
13. **Public luxury, private sufficiency, and beauty.** Public goods that are genuinely good; design and culture budgets protected; no privilege tier; free time as the headline good. (FM-16, FM-17.)
14. **Care is counted, planned and shared.** Care appears in the national accounts and in the plan; universal services; non-transferable parental leave; reproductive autonomy entrenched. (FM-18, FM-07.)

## Part C — Open tensions I will NOT resolve silently (for your decision)

1. **The party question.** Options: a vanguard party with internal democracy; multi-party *socialist* pluralism; open multi-party pluralism including pro-capitalist parties; a no-party council system. FM-02, FM-10, FM-12 and FM-13 pull in different directions here. I will set out each option against each failure mode in Phase 2.
2. **Entrenchment vs reversibility.** How hard should it be for a future majority to undo social ownership? (FM-12 vs FM-02.)
3. **The North.** Options: 26 counties first; unity first; a combined all-island convention; a federal or confederal model ([08 §11](08-ireland-today.md)).
4. **The EU.** Options: work within; negotiated derogations; exit; selective non-compliance ([08 §10](08-ireland-today.md)).
5. **Binding sortition.** Should randomly selected bodies have binding powers (veto, agenda-setting) or stay advisory?
6. **Separation of powers vs the Commune's fusion** of legislature and executive. The literature leans toward keeping independent courts at least.
7. **Markets.** How much of the consumer economy stays market-based? There is a dial from Nove-heavy to parecon-heavy. FM-17 now pulls toward more markets for variety, and FM-16 toward less consumption.
8. **The name.** Is "Poblacht na nOibrithe" right?
9. **Ecological commitment** (new, from FM-16): should the constitution commit to falling throughput, to decarbonisation only, or to binding physical budgets with the mix left to democratic choice? *This was added to the Phase 2 outline as a ninth question because it emerged from the new research. It is the user's to drop or keep.*

All of these are set out as side-by-side options in [`design/PHASE2-OUTLINE.md`](../design/PHASE2-OUTLINE.md).

# 09 — Ecology: degrowth vs eco-modernist planning, and what planned reduction would mean in Ireland

This closes the FM-16 gap flagged in Phase 1. Section 1 sets out the four positions the brief named. Section 2 covers the historical record of socialist states. Section 3 applies the debate to Ireland sector by sector. Section 4 draws out the design implications. Section 4 also gives the honest point of disagreement that the user will need to settle (or leave open) in Phase 2.

---

## 1. The debate

The ecosocialist left agrees on more than the polemics suggest. It agrees that capitalism's growth imperative drives ecological breakdown, that the transition needs planning, and that fossil fuels must go fast. The real disagreements are over four questions:

- **(a)** whether *aggregate* material and energy throughput must fall in rich countries;
- **(b)** whether abundance or sufficiency is the political offer;
- **(c)** which technologies to back (nuclear, carbon capture, large-scale renewables);
- **(d)** who the agent of change is.

### 1.1 Degrowth — Jason Hickel
- *Less is More: How Degrowth Will Save the World* (Penguin/Heinemann, 2020). Hickel defines degrowth as *"a planned reduction of excess energy and resource use to bring the economy back into balance with the living world in a safe, just and equitable way"* ([Australian Institute of International Affairs review](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/book-review-less-is-more-how-degrowth-will-save-the-world/); [Thinking Faith review](https://www.thinkingfaith.org/node/2350)). The definition is quoted from reviews; I have not checked a page reference.
- **The proposals, as reviewers summarise them:**
  - end planned obsolescence;
  - cut advertising;
  - move from owning things to using them (sharing, libraries of things);
  - end food waste;
  - scale down ecologically destructive industries;
  - shorten the working week;
  - expand public goods

  ([AIIA](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/book-review-less-is-more-how-degrowth-will-save-the-world/); [Earth.fm](https://earth.fm/earth-stories/less-is-more-degrowth-book/)).
- **The empirical core.** Hickel & Kallis, "Is Green Growth Possible?", *New Political Economy* 25(4), 2020 (online 2019). It finds *no empirical evidence* that GDP can be *absolutely* decoupled from resource use globally while growth continues. It also finds decoupling from carbon *unlikely* to happen fast enough for 1.5°C or 2°C ([Goldsmiths repository](https://research.gold.ac.uk/26392); [UAB accepted manuscript](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/artpub/2019/205316/newpoleco_a2019m4ahickelkallispp.pdf)).
- **Left critique.** Tom Wetzel argues that Hickel moves between blaming an ideology ("growthism") and blaming the dynamics of capital, and that labour exploitation falls out of the picture ([Anarchist Library](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/tom-wetzel-review-of-jason-hickel-less-is-more.html)).

### 1.2 Degrowth communism — Kohei Saito
- *Marx in the Anthropocene: Towards the Idea of Degrowth Communism* (Cambridge University Press, 2023). Saito draws on newly published notebooks in the Marx–Engels *Gesamtausgabe* (MEGA). He argues that the late Marx moved away from productivism toward an ecological, "degrowth" communism, and that twentieth-century Marxism marginalised this ([LA Review of Books](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/red-and-green-make-degrowth-on-kohei-saitos-marx-in-the-anthropocene); [Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/economic-growth-is-fuelling-climate-change-a-new-book-proposes-degrowth-communism-as-the-solution-88047); [Işık University summary](https://research.isikun.edu.tr/items/7f2d4c28-cc75-4e73-a406-6c9c4da3f325/full)). His Japanese bestseller *Capital in the Anthropocene* (2020; in English as *Slow Down*) is the popular version ([Counterfire review of *Slow Down*](https://www.counterfire.org/article/slow-down-how-degrowth-communism-can-save-the-earth-book-review/)).
- **The key move for this project.** Saito redefines *wealth* as abundant public goods and free time, not as a growing stock of commodities. This converges with Monbiot's "private sufficiency, public luxury" (see §4).
- **Critiques.**
  - Matt Huber and Leigh Phillips (*Jacobin*) argue that Saito's textual case rests largely on the drafts of Marx's 1881 letter to Vera Zasulich and on notebook excerpts, and that it does not show Marx abandoning the development of productive forces ([Counterfire summary](https://www.counterfire.org/?p=35944)).
  - The *Monthly Review* editors (June 2024) reject *both* Saito's reading and Huber–Phillips's ([Climate & Capitalism](https://climateandcapitalism.com/2024/06/13/two-views-both-wrong-on-marx-degrowth-and-productivism)).
  - *Cosmonaut* also faults both camps ([Cosmonaut](https://cosmonautmag.com/2024/06/eco-leninism-a-much-needed-corrective/)).
  - **For this project, the exegetical question (what Marx "really" thought) matters less than the practical one: what to plan.**

### 1.3 Eco-modernist / "abundance" socialism — Matthew Huber
- *Climate Change as Class War: Building Socialism on a Warming Planet* (Verso, 2022). Huber argues:
  - the climate movement is weak because it is rooted in the **professional class** and fixated on individual consumption and a "politics of less";
  - a winning climate politics must be grounded in the **working class**, especially electricity-sector unions;
  - it must offer material gains through public ownership of power and rapid electrification

  ([Global Labour Journal review](https://mulpress.mcmaster.ca/globallabour/article/view/5242/4287); [Resilience](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2022-08-16/class-struggle-or-degrowth/)).
- **Critiques.**
  - Degrowth scholars say Huber does not answer the *empirical* claim that sustainability is incompatible with ever-rising aggregate throughput. They also say he confines class struggle to the Global North and hides global extraction ([FGCU, "Degrowth or Class Struggle? A Critique"](https://scholarscommons.fgcu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Degrowth-or-Class-Struggle-A-Critique/99384065443006570)).
  - *The Ecologist* criticises his relative leniency on aviation ([The Ecologist](https://theecologist.org/2023/aug/29/degrowth-new-dealers)).
- **The non-socialist eco-modernist pole.** *An Ecomodernist Manifesto* (Breakthrough Institute and others, April 2015) argues for decoupling human development from nature through intensification, nuclear power and carbon capture. It rejects "reconciliation with nature" as romantic ([KIT summary](https://www.km.kit.edu/kit_express_9848.php); [degrowth response, Resilience](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2015-05-06/a-degrowth-response-to-an-ecomodernist-manifesto/)). Huber's is the socialist variant: abundance through public power, not markets.

### 1.4 Half-Earth Socialism — Vettese & Pendergrass
- Troy Vettese and Drew Pendergrass, *Half-Earth Socialism: A Plan to Save the Future from Extinction, Climate Change and Pandemics* (Verso, 2022). It has three planks:
  1. **Rewild half the Earth.** This adapts E. O. Wilson's "Half-Earth" but rejects its colonial, fortress-conservation versions.
  2. **A global switch to veganism**, to free up land.
  3. **Per-person energy quotas** within a democratically planned economy run on renewables.

  It **rejects** nuclear power, geoengineering (its opening 2047 dystopia features solar radiation management) and carbon capture as techno-fixes ([LSE USAPP review](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/usappblog/2022/06/05/book-review-half-earth-socialism-a-plan-to-save-the-future-from-extinction-climate-change-and-pandemics-by-drew-pendergrass-and-troy-vettese); [Hastings Independent Press](https://www.hastingsindependentpress.co.uk/articles/culture/arts/bookbuster-review-52/); [Luke's Notes](https://lukesnotes.mataroa.blog/blog/on-half-earth-socialism/)). In an interview, Pendergrass favours moneyless, in-kind planning ([Socialist Standard](https://www.worldsocialism.org/spgb/?p=244732)).
- **Critique.** *Cosmonaut* argues the book does not make a convincing case for "half-earth" as a *viable* form of ecosocialist planning ([Cosmonaut](https://cosmonautmag.com/2022/10/who-holds-up-half-the-earth-a-review-of-half-earth-socialism)).
- **Relevance to Ireland.** This is the most radical position on *land use and diet*. In Ireland that means the herd. It is the position furthest from what rural Ireland would currently accept. It is also the one with the clearest answer to the question of where the land for rewilding and rewetting would come from.

### 1.5 The positions compared

| | Hickel / Saito (degrowth) | Huber (eco-modernist socialism) | Vettese & Pendergrass (Half-Earth) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rich-country aggregate throughput | Must fall | Can grow while decarbonising (electrification) | Must fall; energy quotas |
| Political offer | Public abundance plus free time; less private stuff | Material gains for workers; cheap clean power | Survival, biodiversity, planned sufficiency |
| Key agent | Broad coalition; Global South | Organised workers, especially in power | Planners plus the democratic public |
| Nuclear | Mostly sceptical | Open to it | Rejects |
| Diet and land | Less meat; agroecology | Not central | Global veganism; rewild half |
| Main weakness (per critics) | Politically hard sell ("less"); vague on mechanism | Dodges the throughput evidence; Northern focus | Viability; consent; diet compulsion |

## 2. The historical record of socialist states

- **Soviet productivism.** Soviet planners treated output targets as the measure of success. The Aral Sea is the emblematic case. Irrigation for cotton monoculture diverted the Amu Darya and Syr Darya. Canals were poorly built (from the Qaraqum Canal *perhaps 30–75%* of water was lost), and the sea shrank catastrophically ([Wikipedia mirror](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/a/Aral_Sea.htm)). Murray Feshbach and Alfred Friendly Jr., *Ecocide in the USSR: Health and Nature Under Siege* (BasicBooks, 1992), is the classic survey ([library record](https://theses.univ-nantes.fr/vufind/Record/PPN003376451/Description); [Laboratorium review](https://soclabo.org/index.php/laboratorium/article/view/1479)). **The exact volume loss is not verified to scholarly standard in this pass.** Sources range from "one third of its former volume" to higher figures. Use qualitative language on the site until it is checked against Micklin or similar.
- **The lesson.** Planning *without* an independent institution that represents ecological limits, and *with* output-based legitimacy, is no better for nature than markets. Sometimes it is worse, because there is no independent press or civil society to raise the alarm.
- **Degrowth by necessity: Cuba's Special Period (1990s).** After Soviet oil and inputs vanished, average calorie intake reportedly fell from about 2,900 (1989) to about 1,800 (1995). The state leased idle land for organic urban gardens (*organopónicos*), and claims of Havana's self-sufficiency in fresh produce range from about 80% to over 90%. The method was being trialled from 1987 ([AAIHS](https://www.aaihs.org/reaping-the-revolution-urban-agriculture-in-havana-cuba/); [IIED](https://www.iied.org/node/75654); [SourceWatch](https://www.sourcewatch.org/index.php/Organic_Agriculture_in_Cuba_During_the_Special_Period)). The figures vary and some sources are advocacy. **Lesson in both directions:** decentralised agroecology can feed cities under severe constraint. But an *unplanned*, imposed contraction is a humanitarian crisis, not a model. Degrowth has to be chosen and designed.

**What grows** (both camps agree here):

- **Renewables.** The target is 80% of electricity by 2030, with 9 GW onshore, 8 GW solar and at least 5 GW offshore; offshore wind was 25 MW in 2023 ([William Fry on CAP25](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/government-approves-climate-action-plan-2025/)).
- **Forestry.** Cover is 11.6% against an 18% target. Only 2,527 ha was planted in 2025 against a target of 8,000 ha a year ([Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/more/forestry/tree-planting-up-61-but-falls-well-short-of-climate-plan-targets-929568); [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41908156.html)).
- **Retrofitting.** Residential emissions *rose* 4.9% in 2024 ([EPA infographic](https://www.epa.ie/publications/monitoring--assessment/climate-change/air-emissions/Proxy-Infographic-2024-formatted_v3.pdf)).
- **Public transport and rail.**
- **Housing built to passive standards.**
- **Care work**, which is low-carbon (see file 11).
- **Repair.**
- **Horticulture.** Ireland imports most of its vegetables; not sourced in this pass.

**This is where the two camps meet in Ireland.** Growth *in public goods and low-carbon labour* (care, repair, retrofit, transit, rewilding) can absorb workers released from shrinking sectors. Whether the *total* is "degrowth" or "green growth" depends on how GDP counts it. That matters much less in Ireland than elsewhere, because Irish GDP is already a fiction (GNI\*, file 08).

## 4. Design implications (Phase 2 input)

1. **An independent ecological institution with teeth.** This is the missing piece in Soviet planning. Options:
   - a constitutionally established **Ecological Inspectorate (working Irish name *Coimisiún an Dúlra*; to be checked by a fluent speaker)** with a suspensive veto over plan items that breach physical budgets; or
   - a standing **sortition assembly on ecology** (Ireland's 2022–23 Citizens' Assembly on Biodiversity Loss is the precedent, [file 06](06-positive-models.md)).
2. **Physical budgets, set politically, enforced as quantities.** Carbon, nitrogen, water, land use and electricity allocation are set as caps (Half-Earth's quota logic), not only as prices. Within the caps, allocation can use plans, negotiation or markets.
3. **The wealth question is the political offer.** Adopt Saito's and Monbiot's framing: wealth is public luxury plus free time. "Private sufficiency, public luxury" is associated with George Monbiot from about 2017, and he gave a Schumacher Lecture under that title ([Monbiot 2017](https://www.monbiot.com/2017/06/05/%ef%bb%bf-space-savers/); [The Ecologist 2020](https://theecologist.org/2020/jun/11/george-monbiot-coronavirus-public-luxury); [The Ecologist 2024](https://theecologist.org/2024/jul/09/private-sufficiency-and-public-luxury)). It answers Huber's charge that degrowth offers only "less", **and** it answers FM-17 (greyness), *provided the public luxury is actually luxurious* (see file 10).
4. **A just transition for farmers as a first-order constitutional commitment**, not an afterthought:
   - guaranteed income through the transition;
   - payment for land stewardship, rewetting and forestry;
   - cooperative ownership of processing (reversing the demutualisation of the creameries, [file 07](07-irish-lineage.md));
   - farmers represented by right on the planning bodies that decide land use.
5. **Electrification of everything is common ground and goes first.** Both camps agree on it. It wins Huber's constituency (power-sector workers) and is a precondition for anything Hickel wants.
6. **The disagreement to put to the user.** Should the constitution commit to *falling aggregate throughput* (degrowth), to *decarbonisation with no commitment on throughput* (eco-modernist), or only to *binding physical budgets* (agnostic: let the democratic process find the mix)? I recommend presenting all three. The third is the least ideological and the most robust to evidence changing, but it is also the easiest to hollow out.

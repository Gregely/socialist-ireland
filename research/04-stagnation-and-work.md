# 04 — Stagnation, work discipline and incentives

## 1. The problem

A socialist economy that guarantees employment and removes the threat of destitution also removes capitalism's main (brutal) discipline on work. If nothing *positive* replaces it, the likely results are disengagement, hoarding of labour, low productivity growth and cynicism. Soviet history tried two answers and both failed: **coercion and exhortation** (Stakhanovism, labour discipline laws) and **tacit tolerance** (the Brezhnev-era "social contract" of low effort for low pay).

## 2. Brezhnev-era stagnation (1964–82)

- Growth slowed from roughly 5% a year in the 1960s to under 2% by the late 1970s and early 1980s. The figures vary by source and measure, and historians dispute the dates and causes of "stagnation" ([Wikipedia, Era of Stagnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Stagnation); [Wikipedia, History of the USSR 1964–82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_(1964%E2%80%931982))).
- **Proposed causes** in the sources:
  - rigid central planning that could not cope with a more complex economy;
  - a nomenklatura that rewarded loyalty over competence;
  - heavy military spending;
  - oil revenues after 1973 that allowed reform to be put off (the USSR became a large grain importer in the 1970s).

  (Sources: the [Wikipedia articles above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Stagnation); [GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/cccp-history-period-of-stagnation.htm); [Moscow Times commentary](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2011/11/07/stagnation-is-never-good-a10650/pdf).)
- **"They pretend to pay us and we pretend to work."** It is widely described as a Soviet joke. Its origin is **unverified**. The earliest dated print appearance I found is a May 1965 *San Diego Union* item attributing it to Hungary ([Barry Popik](https://barrypopik.com/blog/they_pretend_to_pay_us_and_we_pretend_to_work)). Alexandra Oberländer's scholarly article on late-Soviet work ethics calls it the most famous Soviet joke about work ([Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/666018)). The site should use it as a *folk saying* and say its origin is uncertain.
- **Reading.** The joke describes a *mutual* withdrawal. The state's wages bought little because consumer goods were scarce (see Kornai, file 03). Workers withheld effort in return. Effort follows what effort can buy, and what voice a worker has.

## 3. Stakhanovism (1935 onward)

- In August 1935 the miner Aleksei Stakhanov cut about fourteen times his quota in one shift. The shift was staged with favourable conditions. *Pravda* hailed it as a world record, and the party built a movement around it ([Siegelbaum, Seventeen Moments](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1936/year-of-the-stakhanovite/)).
- **Consequences.** Stakhanovites were often resented, and some were verbally or physically abused by co-workers who did not get the same favourable conditions. Norms were raised in 1936, which in practice meant pay cuts for many ([Siegelbaum](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1936/year-of-the-stakhanovite/); [Berneri, a partisan anarchist pamphlet of the period](https://libcom.org/article/stakhanovism-and-british-workers)). Lewis Siegelbaum's *Stakhanovism and the Politics of Productivity in the USSR* (1988) is the standard scholarly treatment. I have not consulted it directly.
- **Lesson.** Rewarding individual "heroes" with privileges and then raising everyone's norms *turns recognition into a tool for intensifying work*. It divides workers and breeds cynicism.

## 4. The Yugoslav variant: self-management and unemployment

- Self-managed firms had an incentive to maximise income *per member*. That gave them reasons not to take on new members (Woodward's analysis, below). Yugoslavia's unemployment reached about 15% by 1985. It was concentrated in the less developed regions: in 1965 Slovenia had 2.5% and Kosovo 21%. Regional income gaps widened ([Princeton UP: Woodward, *Socialist Unemployment*](https://press.princeton.edu/node/43750); [Carl Beck Papers](https://carlbeckpapers.pitt.edu/ojs/cbp/article/view/13/12)).
- After 1980 output fell, foreign debt rose, inflation became hyperinflation, and regional inequality worsened ([MIT Press chapter](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/9930/014.pdf?dl=1)).
- **Lesson.** Self-management by insiders can shut out outsiders. Hiring obligations, job guarantees *outside* the enterprise, and investment steered by regional need are all needed (see file 06).

## 5. What does the literature suggest instead?

These are claims from the wider literature that I have *not* yet sourced here. They need sources in Phase 2:

- Intrinsic motivation, autonomy and recognition from peers. This is the self-determination theory literature (Deci & Ryan). Workplace democracy studies, including research on Mondragon productivity, are relevant.
- Shorter working hours as a share of productivity gains (Keynes's "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren"; the 4-day-week trials in Ireland and the UK).
- Job rotation and balanced job complexes (parecon, file 03).

Sourced so far:

- Mondragon's pay ratio is capped at 1:6. It began at 1:3, became 1:4.5 in the 1970s and 1:6 in 1988 ([Co-operative News](https://www.thenews.coop/co-operative-approaches-to-pay/)). This shows that compressed pay differentials are compatible with an industrial enterprise surviving for decades. Its limits are in file 06.

## 6. Design implications

1. **No Stakhanovism.** Recognition should be collective, peer-nominated and non-monetary, or modestly monetary with a cap. It must never be tied to raising norms.
2. **Effort must buy something real.** That means consumer goods actually available, more free time (hours reduced as productivity grows), and a voice in the workplace.
3. **Hard budget constraints on enterprises** (Kornai) combined with a **job guarantee from society, not the firm**. Workers are protected; inefficient units are not.
4. **Shirking is handled by peers first.** Workplace councils handle it with due process, then redeployment and retraining. Expulsion from the enterprise is possible, but expulsion from the means of life is not.
5. **Stagnation in innovation** needs its own channel: public R&D, open licences, a right for enterprises to experiment, and the freedom to start new cooperatives.

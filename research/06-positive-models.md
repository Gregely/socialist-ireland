# 06 — Positive models and experiments

Each entry covers **what it was**, **what worked**, **how it failed or was limited**, and **what it lends this design**. None is a blueprint. Most were small, short-lived, under siege, or all three.

---

## 1. The Paris Commune (March–May 1871)

**What.** The Paris municipal insurrection after the Franco-Prussian War. It was crushed in late May 1871.

**Mechanisms**, from Marx's *The Civil War in France* (1871), ch. 5, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm):

- Councillors were *"chosen by universal suffrage in the various wards of the town, responsible and revocable at short terms."*
- *"The Commune was to be a working, not a parliamentary body, executive and legislative at the same time."*
- *"From the members of the Commune downwards, the public service had to be done at workman's wage."*
- The police were *"turned into the responsible, and at all times revocable, agent of the Commune."*
- *"The first decree of the Commune, therefore, was the suppression of the standing army,"* which was replaced by the armed people (the National Guard).

**Caveats.** It lasted about 72 days. Its membership mixed Jacobins, Blanquists, Proudhonists and members of the International. Its decrees were more municipal and republican than communist ([Wikipedia, *The Civil War in France*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Civil_War_in_France); [Irish Marxist Review](https://irishmarxistreview.net/index.php/imr/article/view/405)). Marx's account is partly a political argument and not a neutral history.

**Lends.** Recall. Officials paid a worker's wage. Fusing deliberation with execution. Making the police accountable. Note that "executive and legislative at the same time" is itself a risk: it removes separation of powers. The design must weigh this.

## 3. Yugoslav workers' self-management (1950–1990)

**What.** The Basic Law on the Management of State Economic Enterprises (26 June 1950) put enterprises under workers' collectives acting through elected **workers' councils** (one-year terms, recallable) and management boards ([marxists.org document collection](https://www.marxists.org/subject/yugoslavia/self-management/1950/06/x01.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_self-management)).

**Limits.**

- Directors appointed by the government kept a veto at first, and the state kept control over investment ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/serbia/yu-02.htm)).
- The 1965 market reform gave firms more autonomy. It is credited with good results up to that point and then followed by growing problems ([SciELO](https://scielo.br/j/his/a/LVh33QKPqXs68Sdz5TTVqvr/abstract/?lang=en)).

**Failures.**

- Unemployment about 15% by 1985, with a sharp regional gradient (Slovenia 2.5% vs Kosovo 21% in 1965). Firms maximising income per member had reasons not to hire ([Woodward, Princeton UP](https://press.princeton.edu/node/43750); [Carl Beck Papers](https://carlbeckpapers.pitt.edu/ojs/cbp/article/view/13/12)).
- Foreign debt and 1980s hyperinflation ([MIT Press chapter](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/9930/014.pdf?dl=1)).
- Self-management *in the enterprise* sat alongside a one-party state, and economic decentralisation turned into decentralisation by republic. Republic-level party elites became the channels for national resentment (SANU Memorandum, 1986; Milošević's rise) ([Wikipedia, SANU Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SANU_Memorandum); [De Gruyter article](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/soeu-2021-0044/html)).

**Lends.** Workplace councils are workable at national scale for decades.

**Warns.**

- Insider-outsider unemployment.
- Soft budgets.
- Regional divergence without strong equalisation.
- **Political monopoly plus economic decentralisation leads to nationalist capture of regional elites.**

## 5. Kerala's People's Plan Campaign (1996 onward)

**What.** The Left Democratic Front government devolved **35–40% of the state's plan budget** to local governments for 1997–2002. The headline figure is 35%. Village and ward assemblies (grama sabhas) identified needs, task forces drafted projects, and elected local councils chose priorities ([Franke, Montclair](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~franker/isaacbook.htm); [Franke, MacArthur paper](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~franker/MacArthurforweb.htm)).

**Scale.** Its publisher says over 3 million of Kerala's roughly 30 million people took part, across 1,052 villages and urban areas. Franke elsewhere gives 990 villages ([LeftWord](https://mayday.leftword.com/catalog/product/view/id/22319); [Franke](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~franker/KeralaExperiment.pdf)). T. M. Thomas Isaac, who led it from the State Planning Board, and Richard Franke wrote the main account, *Local Democracy and Development* (2002).

**Limits.** After the LDF lost the 2001 election, the Congress-led ministry undercut many features of the campaign, but local planning survived ([Franke](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~franker/KeralaExperiment.pdf)).

**Lends.** A fixed, *constitutionally guaranteed* share of investment for local assemblies. A training campaign so that people can actually use the power. Volunteer technical experts. **Warns:** reforms that depend on one party's term in office are open to being reversed.

## 6. Rojava / the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (2012 onward)

**What.**

- **Communes** of about 30–400 households elect boards with mandatory **male and female co-chairs**. Representatives can be recalled.
- Communes federate into neighbourhood and village councils, then districts ([TRISE report](https://trise.org/2018/08/27/democratic-revolution-in-rojava/)).
- The 2014 Charter of the Social Contract (Art. 65) requires at least 40% of either sex in all institutions ([Heinrich Böll Foundation paper](https://lb.boell.org/sites/default/files/women_rights_in_rojava-_english_-_online_paper.pdf)).
- Women's councils handle gender-based violence ([academic delegation statement](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/ramazan-mendanlioglu-rojava-s-everyday-democracy)).

**Criticisms.**

- The Böll paper reports that the quota was at times used to promote women close to the PYD. Influence went to women with PKK military training or family ties to martyrs, and Arab women took part only partially ([Böll](https://lb.boell.org/sites/default/files/women_rights_in_rojava-_english_-_online_paper.pdf)).
- Human Rights Watch (2014) documented arbitrary arrests of PYD political opponents, abuse in detention, and unsolved killings ([HRW](https://www.hrw.org/node/126064/section/12)).
- Amnesty International (2015) documented arbitrary detention of critics and "blatantly unfair" trials ([Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/press-releases/2015/09/syria-abuses-mar-pyd-fight-against-terrorism/)).
- The PYD denied holding political prisoners ([Rudaw on HRW](https://www.rudaw.net/english/middleeast/syria/20062014)).

**Lends.** Co-chairs. Gender parity built into institutions. Commune-level federation. **Warns:** confederal councils can sit alongside a dominant party with armed power behind it, the "dual power" problem. The site should not romanticise Rojava, and also should not dismiss a wartime experiment by peacetime standards. The current status after events in Syria from 2024 on was not researched in this pass and should not be described without a fresh check.

## 7. Cuba's 2019 constitutional consultation

**What.** A draft constitution was put to nationwide public consultation in 2018. Almost **9 million** people are reported to have taken part. The drafting commission then made **760 changes**, affecting about 60% of articles ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/2/24/cubans-vote-on-new-constitution-to-replace-cold-war-era-charter); [EFE via El Día](https://eldia.com.do/los-cubanos-comienzan-a-debatir-su-constitucion-entre-homenajes-a-fidel/)). The figure of "133,000 meetings" that is sometimes cited could not be verified from a reliable source. The referendum on 24 February 2019 approved it with 86.8% yes and 9% no. Turnout figures vary by source ([People's Dispatch](https://peoplesdispatch.org/2019/02/26/cubans-overwhelmingly-vote-in-favor-of-the-new-constitution/)).

**Limits.** More than 11,000 proposals for direct election of the president were not adopted. Critics said it was unclear how proposals were weighed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/2/24/cubans-vote-on-new-constitution-to-replace-cold-war-era-charter)). The constitution keeps the Communist Party as the "superior leading force", so there was no organised opposition campaign. That is my statement and needs a source to the constitutional text for the site.

**Lends.** Mass consultation *before* a referendum. **Warns:** consultation without open competition between alternatives, and without transparent rules for weighing input, can be steered from above.

## 8. Mondragon (Basque Country, 1956 onward)

**What.** A federation of worker cooperatives. Pay ratios are set by each co-op's general assembly: they started at 1:3, rose to 1:4.5 in the 1970s and to **1:6** in 1988 ([Co-operative News](https://www.thenews.coop/co-operative-approaches-to-pay/)).

**Failure of Fagor Electrodomésticos (2013).**

- Its flagship appliance co-op went bankrupt after the 2008 housing crash. The other co-ops refused further rescues ([ISA Global Dialogue](https://globaldialogue.isa-sociology.org/articles/the-mondragon-cooperatives-successes-and-challenges); [Mondragon 2013 annual report](https://www.mondragon-corporation.com/urtekotxostena/dist/docs/en/informe-anual-2013-en.pdf)).
- More than 1,000 member-workers were relocated to other co-ops. But contract workers and about 3,500 employees of Fagor's *subsidiaries* were not protected in the same way ([Global Dialogue](https://globaldialogue.isa-sociology.org/articles/the-mondragon-cooperatives-successes-and-challenges)).
- Only about 40% of group workers are full owner-members ([GEO](https://geo.coop/node/5145)).
- Pay and conditions in overseas subsidiaries were poor ([Global Dialogue](https://globaldialogue.isa-sociology.org/articles/the-mondragon-cooperatives-successes-and-challenges)).

**Lends.** Pay compression that works. Inter-cooperative solidarity funds and redeployment of workers. Hard budget constraints *combined with* worker protection. **Warns:** cooperatives in capitalist markets drift into two-tier workforces. Solidarity among co-ops has limits when losses are large.

## 9. Participatory budgeting — Porto Alegre (1989 onward)

**What.** The first full participatory budgeting process, started by the Workers' Party (PT) city government in 1989. Some sources say 1990 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_budgeting)).

**Decline.** After the PT lost the mayoralty in 2004, successor administrations kept PB in name while taking away its institutional, political and financial support ([Cambridge Core article; I have not confirmed the authorship (sometimes attributed to Baiocchi & Ganuza)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/44EC7210668F4E4CC82853961C5133E9); [SciELO, "Why has PB declined in Brazil?"](https://www.scielo.br/j/bpsr/a/Tx6VMmgTLphDZXvCRsccLMq/?lang=en)). The often-cited "World Bank evaluation" was **not** located in this pass.

**Lends.** Neighbourhood assemblies that allocate real capital budgets. **Warns:** without legal entrenchment, participation can be quietly drained of money and kept on as a shell.

## 10. Citizens' assemblies — including Ireland's own

**Ireland's record.** Ireland is one of the world's most-cited users of randomly selected assemblies.

- **Convention on the Constitution (2012–14).** Its recommendations led to the 2015 marriage-equality referendum ([Electoral Reform Society](https://www.electoral-reform.org.uk/the-irish-abortion-referendum-how-a-citizens-assembly-helped-to-break-years-of-political-deadlock/)).
- **Citizens' Assembly (2016–18).** 99 randomly selected citizens plus a chair. It deliberated on the Eighth Amendment over five weekends. The referendum of 25 May 2018 passed 66.4% to 33.6% ([Citizens' Assembly terms of reference](https://citizensassembly.ie/?p=7160); [ERS](https://www.electoral-reform.org.uk/the-irish-abortion-referendum-how-a-citizens-assembly-helped-to-break-years-of-political-deadlock/)). It also considered an ageing population, fixed-term parliaments, how referendums are held, and climate change.
- **Later assemblies.** Gender equality; biodiversity loss (2022–23, which voted for a constitutional amendment to protect biodiversity); a directly elected mayor for Dublin (2022, report January 2023); drugs use (2023) ([citizensassembly.ie, previous assemblies](https://citizensassembly.ie/previous-assemblies/); [Oireachtas](https://www.drugsandalcohol.ie/38089)).

**Limits.** Assemblies are **advisory**. Governments choose which recommendations to act on, and many have not been implemented. That point is general and needs a specific source for the site. The government also sets the agenda.

**Lends.** A proven, legitimate *Irish* mechanism for deliberation by lot. **Design question:** should assemblies be binding, agenda-setting, or both?

## 11. Sortition

- **Athens.** The Council of 500 (*boulē*) and most magistrates were chosen by lot, in a way that balanced the tribes and demes. The *kleroterion* was a stone allotment machine. Generals (*stratēgoi*) and some financial officers were **elected**, a deliberate exception for posts needing expertise ([Britannica, sortition](https://www.britannica.com/topic/sortition)).
- **Ostbelgien (German-speaking Community of Belgium), 2019.** A permanent **Citizens' Council** (*Bürgerrat*) of 24 people chosen by lot, serving 18 months, sets the agenda for ad hoc **citizens' assemblies** chosen by lot with quotas ([Equality by Lot](https://equalitybylot.com/2019/03/01/new-permanent-sortition-assembly-in-belgium/)).
- David Van Reybrouck's *Against Elections* (2013) revived the case for sortition as a complement or alternative to elections ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/sortition)). I have not consulted it directly.

**Lends.** Sortition resists capture by careerists and parties. That makes it the best direct answer to Michels and Voslensky. **Warns:** members chosen by lot depend on staff and experts, which risks capture by the secretariat. Accountability is weak because there is no electorate to answer to.

## 13. A framework for judging all of these

Erik Olin Wright, *Envisioning Real Utopias* (Verso, 2010), proposes three tests for any alternative: **desirability, viability and achievability**. He argues that viability (whether it works without perverse effects and lasts) is the most pressing question ([Crooked Timber](https://crookedtimber.org/2007/05/08/rules-for-radical-thinking/); [P2P Foundation](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Envisioning_Real_Utopias)). The site can use these three tests as a lens for every institution.

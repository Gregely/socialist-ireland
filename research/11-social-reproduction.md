# 11 — Social reproduction and the "double burden" (FM-18)

## 1. The problem

Every economy rests on work that is mostly unpaid and mostly done by women:

- bearing and raising children;
- cooking, cleaning and laundry;
- caring for sick, disabled and older people;
- the emotional and organisational work that keeps households and communities going.

Socialist states promised to *socialise* this work. Instead they mostly drew women into paid employment while leaving domestic work where it was. The result was the **double burden** (or "double shift"). Any socialist design that counts only "production" will repeat this.

## 2. The promise

- **Lenin, "A Great Beginning" (28 June 1919).** Housework *"crushes, strangles, stultifies and degrades her"*. Lenin named *"public catering establishments, nurseries, kindergartens"* as "shoots of communism" that could free women. He added that these means were themselves created by large-scale capitalism ([marxists.org, "Women and Marxism" extract](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/subject/women/abstract/19_06_28.htm)).
- **Alexandra Kollontai, "Communism and the Family" (*Komunistka*, 1920).** *"under communism everyone will be able to eat in the communal kitchens and dining-rooms"*. The working woman *"will simply take these things to the central laundries each week"*. And: *"Communism liberates wom[e]n from her domestic slavery and makes her life richer and happier"* ([marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollonta/1920/communism-family.htm); the archive text reads "worm", an apparent typo for "women"). The 1919–20 programme was explicit: housework was to be *abolished* as private labour.
- **Reproductive rights.** In November 1920 the Soviet Union became the first state to legalise abortion broadly: free, and on non-medical grounds as well as medical ones ([VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/beneath-ban-abortion-evidence-ussr); [Nakachi interview](https://newbooksnetwork.com/replacing-the-dead)).

## 3. What happened

- **Reversal.** Abortion became a paid service from 1930. In **1936** it was banned, alongside benefits for large and single-parent families, as a pronatalist measure after the collectivisation famine. The VoxEU study finds the ban raised births *and* infant and maternal mortality ([VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/beneath-ban-abortion-evidence-ussr)). Trotsky called the 1936 family legislation a "Thermidor in the family" ([American Reformer, citing Trotsky](https://americanreformer.org/?p=14297); see also *The Revolution Betrayed*, ch. 7, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1936/revbet/ch07.htm)). **Lesson:** reproductive rights that are granted for reasons of state can be withdrawn for reasons of state.
- **The double burden, measured.**
  - Soviet family time-budget surveys from the early 1920s and from the late 1960s to early 1970s allow official claims of gender equality to be compared with what actually happened in households (Martine Mespoulet, 2015, [Rhuthmos PDF](https://www.rhuthmos.eu/IMG/article_PDF/Un-article-de-Martine-Mespoulet_a2016.pdf); [Isidore](https://isidore.science/document/10670/1.0ea969a2738df42e9601b164551e90484b16fb91)).
  - A 1970s exchange in *Soviet Studies* found housework had not fallen substantially and still fell overwhelmingly on women. This probably held them back from senior positions, even as they entered most occupations ([*Soviet Studies*, Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/42B4860F98D442E654998F8F56536B44/S0037677900083194a.pdf/no_title.pdf)).
  - Interwar research (Kessler) documents the double burden from the start ([*Continuity and Change*](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/continuity-and-change/article/abs/work-and-the-household-in-the-interwar-soviet-union/BAFBB40787F8F2897E1469D12B308E10)).
  - **I did not find exact hours-per-week figures in this pass.** The Mespoulet article is the place to get them.
- **The GDR**, the strongest Eastern Bloc case. Women's employment was 78.1% of working-age women in 1989, or 91.2% if apprentices and students are included (Winkler, *Frauenreport '90*). There was an extensive network of crèches, kindergartens and after-school care, usually open 6:00–18:00, often run by the workplace ([Böll Foundation](https://www.boell.de/de/2016/11/09/familienpolitik-ost-und-westdeutschland-und-ihre-langfristigen-auswirkungen); [GenderOpen](https://www.genderopen.de/bitstreams/1e0a641c-cd9a-432f-9e6f-05a9b1f0eb6a/download)). Even so, housework remained mainly women's work ([ES Think Tank, a secondary source](https://esthinktank.com/2025/03/20/double-burden-double-shift-womens-care-work-in-east-germany-pre-and-post-reunification/)). No time-use figures were found in this pass.
- **The broader pattern.** Soviet women entered the workforce on a large scale in the 1960s–70s, but were concentrated in lower-paid, lower-status jobs. Historians describe a "reprivatisation" of women's lives from the 1970s ([RUDN Journal of Russian History](https://journals.rudn.ru/russian-history/article/view/17465)).

**Root causes.**

1. Socialised services (canteens, laundries, crèches) were chronically underfunded relative to heavy industry. Investment priorities followed output targets, which links to FM-05 and FM-16's productivism.
2. Men's share of domestic work barely changed. The state changed *women's* role and not *men's*.
3. Reproduction was treated as a *demographic* resource for the state, not a matter of autonomy.
4. Care work was not counted as "production" and so did not appear in the plan.
5. Party leaderships were overwhelmingly male (needs sourcing).

## 4. The positive case and the theory

- **Kristen Ghodsee, *Why Women Have Better Sex Under Socialism: And Other Arguments for Economic Independence* (2018).** She argues that state-socialist provision (employment, childcare, parental leave) gave women economic independence from men. The book's best evidence compares East and West German women ([THE review by Lynne Segal](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/663938); [In These Times](https://inthesetimes.com/article/why-women-have-better-sex-under-socialism-Kristen-Ghodsee-review)). **Critiques:** *Reason* argues the thesis doesn't hold up and that Ghodsee blurs Eastern Bloc socialism with Nordic social democracy ([Reason](https://reason.com/2019/03/01/no-sex-wasnt-better-for-women/)). Segal notes the sexological data's limits ([THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/663938)).
- **Wages for Housework (1972 onward); Silvia Federici.** Founded by the International Feminist Collective (Mariarosa Dalla Costa, Silvia Federici, Brigitte Galtier, Selma James). It argues that unpaid domestic labour produces labour-power itself, so capital extracts it unpaid *outside* the wage relation ([Spheres journal](https://spheres-journal.org/contribution/every-moment-of-our-reproduction-as-a-moment-of-struggle-the-new-york-wages-for-housework-archive/); [Federici, *Radical Philosophy*](https://www.radicalphilosophy.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/radical_philosophy_2_04_federici-social-reproduction-theory.pdf)). **Critique:** the demand for a wage sits awkwardly with opposition to wage labour ([Spheres](https://spheres-journal.org/contribution/every-moment-of-our-reproduction-as-a-moment-of-struggle-the-new-york-wages-for-housework-archive/)).
- **Social reproduction theory** (Tithi Bhattacharya, ed., *Social Reproduction Theory: Remapping Class, Recentering Oppression*, Pluto, 2017). The economy depends on the "production of people", so class politics must include the household, schools, hospitals and communities. *Not directly checked in this pass; cited by title only.*
- **Ireland's own lineage.** Thompson and Wheeler's 1825 *Appeal* argued that women's emancipation had to be *economic*, through cooperative association ([file 07](07-irish-lineage.md)). This is a pre-Marxian Irish statement of exactly this problem.

## 5. Ireland today

- **The Constitution.** Article 41.2 still says that the State recognises that by her life within the home, woman gives the State support without which the common good cannot be achieved, and that mothers shall not be obliged by economic necessity to work outside the home ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland)). The **March 2024 care referendum** would have replaced it with gender-neutral recognition of family care. It was defeated **73.9% No**, the highest No vote in the State's history, on 44.4% turnout. Disability and carers' groups opposed the *wording* as too weak, not the goal ([Electoral Commission](https://electoralcommission.ie/referendum/referendums-on-family-and-care); [Agriland](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/final-count-confirms-family-and-care-referendums-defeated/); [GCN](https://gcn.ie/?p=183763)). **Lesson for the design:** Irish voters rejected a symbolic recognition of care that came with no material guarantee. A design should offer *enforceable* care rights, not warm words.
- **Unpaid carers.**
  - **299,128** people gave regular unpaid care in 2022, up more than 50% since 2016. **61%** were women.
  - 29% of carers gave 43 or more hours a week (31% of women vs 26% of men) ([CSO, Census 2022 Profile 4](https://cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp4/censusofpopulation2022profile4-disabilityhealthandcarers/carers)).
  - In Q2 2020, 268,200 women vs about 22,700 men were mainly engaged in unpaid home duties ([CSO SDG 5](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-sdg5/irelandsunsdgs-goal5genderequality2024/equality)).
- **Time use.** The last full national time-use survey, by the ESRI in 2005, found that women worked about 40 minutes a day longer than men once paid and unpaid work are combined, and that parenthood widens the gap ([ESRI](https://esri.ie/publications/gender-inequalities-in-time-use-the-distribution-of-caring-housework-and-employment); [IHREC](https://www.ihrec.ie/?p=16672)). **The absence of a recent national time-use survey is itself telling: the State does not measure this work.**
- **Childcare.** Irish net childcare costs for an average-earning couple are among the highest in Europe, joint fourth on 2023 OECD data, and nearly double the OECD average as a share of income ([The Journal / Investigate Europe](https://www.thejournal.ie/article.php?id=6831936)). The National Childcare Scheme's universal subsidy is €2.14 per hour, up to 45 hours ([gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/press-release/ca5d3-record-numbers-of-families-and-early-learning-and-childcare-providers-engaging-with-the-national-childcare-scheme); [ESRI](https://www.esri.ie/publications/childcare-in-ireland-usage-affordability-and-incentives-to-work-0)).
- **History.** The marriage bar forced women out of civil-service jobs on marriage until **31 July 1973** (the Civil Service (Employment of Married Women) Act), and out of the wider public service until 1974 ([ICTU](https://ictu.ie/news/marriage-bar-ban-employing-married-women); [1973 in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_in_Ireland)). The Magdalene laundries and the mother-and-baby homes are the darkest Irish chapter of institutionalised, unpaid "care" labour. They are not researched in this pass, and the site should treat them with care, with sources.

## 6. What the evidence suggests

1. **Count it.** Care and domestic work appear in the plan and in national accounts. That means a regular official time-use survey every five years, and care investment planned alongside "productive" investment. What isn't measured can't be planned.
2. **Socialise what people want socialised, and resource it properly.**
   - free universal childcare (GDR coverage, without GDR underfunding);
   - public canteens and food halls that are actually good (Kollontai's canteens meet FM-17's public luxury);
   - home-care services;
   - community laundries and repair;
   - well-paid, unionised care workers.
3. **Change men's role, not just women's.**
   - Shorter standard working week for everyone (FM-07, FM-08).
   - Non-transferable, well-paid parental leave for each parent. The Nordic "daddy quota" is the precedent; not sourced in this pass.
   - Care work counts as civic service for rotation and delegate eligibility.
4. **Reproductive autonomy as a constitutional right, not a policy.** It must be entrenched so it cannot be reversed for demographic or "national" reasons (the USSR in 1936).
5. **Carers have a voice in planning.** A recognised care constituency in planning bodies and assemblies. Unpaid carers get paid civic time, not exclusion from participation.
6. **Replace Article 41.2 with an enforceable right to receive care and to give care with support.** This should be worded *with* carers' and disability organisations, the lesson of 2024.
7. **Residual tension.**
   - Socialising care can mean institutionalisation. Ireland's own history makes that a serious fear, and many people want to care for their own family members.
   - The design must offer *choice with support*: good public services *and* a real income for people who care at home. That is costly.
   - Paying for care risks turning family relationships into wage relationships, which is Federici's own dilemma.

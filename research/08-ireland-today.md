# 08 — Ireland today: the legal, economic and political ground

Latest available data as of **October 2026**. Fast-moving figures should carry "as of" dates on the site.

## 1. Bunreacht na hÉireann and how a transition would interact with it

- **The amendment route.** *"Any provision of this Constitution may be amended, whether by way of variation, addition, or repeal"* (Art. 46.1). Amendments must start in the Dáil as a Bill, pass both Houses and then be approved by referendum by simple majority ([Wikisource consolidated text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland); [Citizens' Assembly explainer](https://citizensassembly.ie/previous-assemblies/2013-2014-convention-on-the-constitution/our-constitution)). The 1937 Constitution was itself adopted by plebiscite (1 July 1937) and replaced the Free State Constitution ([IDEA](https://www.idea.int/answer/ans92581099000087)). **A wholly new constitution adopted by referendum therefore has a direct precedent.**
- **Property (Art. 43).** It recognises a natural right to private ownership and bars laws that *abolish* private property in general. But the State *"may as occasion requires delimit by law the exercise of the said rights"* to reconcile them with *"the exigencies of the common good"* ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland); [Irish Judicial Studies Journal](https://irishjudicialstudiesjournal.ie/assets/uploads/images/2023%20edition3/2.%20Irish%20Constitutional%20Property%20Rights%20and%20Normative%20Theories%20of%20Property.pdf)). Article 43 and Article 40.3.2 are read together. Compulsory purchase with compensation is routine; abolishing private ownership of the means of production is not possible without amendment.
- **Directive Principles (Art. 45).** *"The principles of social policy set forth in this Article are intended for the general guidance of the Oireachtas"*. The courts cannot enforce them, though they have occasionally used them as an aid to interpretation ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ireland); [Cassandra Voices](https://cassandravoices.com/society/law/the-key-change-to-fix-the-irish-constitution/)). The Democratic Programme's ideals already have a non-binding echo in the current text. A transition could make them **justiciable**.
- **The EU and the Constitution.** *Crotty v An Taoiseach* (Supreme Court, 9 April 1987) held that Title III of the Single European Act (foreign-policy cooperation) needed a referendum. Since then every major EU treaty has been put to a referendum ([CVCE judgment](https://www.cvce.eu/en/obj/judgment_of_the_supreme_court_of_ireland_crotty_v_an_taoiseach_9_april_1987-en-187e7d4f-aa3e-43da-a1e2-bb3fc41d2fbd.html); [IACL blog](https://blog-iacl-aidc.org/2023-posts/2023/11/7/the-crotty-case-and-eu-treaty-referendums-in-ireland)). Art. 29.4 protects EU law from constitutional challenge. I have not checked the exact wording in this pass.
- **Art. 3 (since 1998).** A united Ireland is to be brought about by peaceful means *"with the consent of a majority of the people, democratically expressed, in both jurisdictions in the island"* (Nineteenth Amendment, approved 22 May 1998) ([Wikipedia, Arts. 2 and 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_2_and_3_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland)).

**Implications.** Four legal paths exist:

- **(a)** A staged series of amendments, each by referendum.
- **(b)** A constitutional convention or citizens' assembly that drafts a new constitution, then a referendum. This follows the 1937 and Cuba 2019 precedents, but with competing drafts on the ballot.
- **(c)** Ordinary legislation within the current Constitution: public enterprise, municipal housing, co-op law, compulsory purchase for the common good.
- **(d)** Some combination of these.

Paths (a)–(c) all depend on winning majorities repeatedly. That is a *feature*, because it builds legitimacy, and a *risk*, because the process can be reversed in the middle.

## 2. Local government

- There are 31 local authorities: 26 county, 3 city and 2 city-and-county councils. They have 949 councillors and most are divided into municipal districts. The 2014 reform cut the number of councils from 114 to 31 and abolished 80 town councils ([LGMA](https://lgma.ie/en/irish-local-government); [LGIU](https://lgiu.org/local-government-facts-and-figures-ireland/); [gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/organisation-information/26251-local-government-structure-and-functions)).
- Power is divided between **reserved functions** (the elected council) and **executive functions** (an appointed chief executive) ([localgov.ie](https://localgov.ie/about-local-government/the-elected-council)).
- The average population per council is very high, about 161,000–166,000. By comparison, France's average municipality has about 2,000 people and the Czech Republic's about 1,500 ([LGIU](https://lgiu.org/local-government-facts-and-figures-ireland/); [OECD/SNG-WOFI](https://sng-wofi.org/country_profiles/ireland.html)).
- **Implication.** Ireland has almost no tier of government at the scale of a parish or town. A council system built from below would have to *create* one, which Kerala's grama sabhas and Rojava's communes suggest is possible. A head-to-head comparison of local autonomy (e.g. the EU Local Autonomy Index) has not yet been sourced.

## 3. The FDI and corporation-tax model

- **Corporation tax (CT) in 2025** was €34.7 bn in total, or €32.9 bn excluding one-off receipts from the CJEU Apple ruling. **Foreign-owned multinationals paid 87%** of net receipts. **The ten largest payers paid 56%** ([Revenue, *CT 2025 payments and 2024 returns*](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/documents/research/ct-analysis-2026.pdf)).
- The Irish Fiscal Advisory Council has repeatedly warned about dependence on a few firms and about "windfall" receipts. A 2022 estimate put windfall CT at €1.5–4.5 bn ([Fiscal Council, "More than corporation tax"](https://fiscalcouncil.substack.com/p/more-than-corporation-tax-irelands); [IFAC, US tariffs paper Nov 2025](https://www.fiscalcouncil.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/how-might-us-tariffs-and-other-policy-changes-affect-Irelands-corporation-tax-receipts.pdf)).
- **Jobs.** IDA client companies employed **312,468** people in 2025, 54% of them outside Dublin. Across Enterprise Ireland, IDA and Údarás client companies the total is about 554,600 ([IDA annual report 2025](https://www.idaireland.com/annual-reports/annual-report-2025); [Dept. of Enterprise](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/news-and-events/department-news/2026/february/202602124.html)).
- **Distorted statistics.** Multinational accounting inflates GDP. In 2015 nominal GDP jumped about 35% while GNI* rose about 9% (my calculation from CSO figures). The CSO created **GNI\*** (modified GNI) to strip out globalisation effects ([CSO, Measuring Ireland's Progress 2019](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-mip/measuringirelandsprogress2019/economy/)).
- **Implications for transition.** This is the single biggest economic vulnerability:
  - a large share of public revenue,
  - a substantial number of well-paid jobs,
  - much of the capital stock,

  all depend on a small number of mostly US firms that can move booked profits, and to a lesser extent real activity, with little warning. Any socialist programme must have a *staged* plan:

  - **Ring-fence** windfall CT now. The current state is already doing this through the Future Ireland Fund (not sourced in this pass).
  - **Build domestic capacity** in parallel.
  - Decide which multinational activities to **keep under negotiated terms**, which to **socialise**, and which to **let go**.
  - Plan for **capital flight**.

## 4. Housing

- A record **17,885** people were in emergency accommodation in August 2026 (12,055 adults and 5,830 children). Dublin accounts for about 68.5% of adults. These figures exclude the "hidden homeless" ([RTÉ, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0925/1592984-homeless-figures-august/)).
- Social housing is about **9%** of the housing stock, compared with about 20% in countries Ireland is often compared with. This is an advocacy estimate from Simon Communities that should be checked against census tables ([Social Justice Ireland / Simon briefing](https://socialjustice.ie/system/files/file-uploads/2024-11/2024-11-18-%20Election%202024%20Briefing%20Housing_0.pdf)).
- **The state relies on private landlords through HAP.** In 2022 there were 68,180 HAP households. €2.3 bn went on HAP in 2019–23 and €525 m was budgeted for 2024. Landlords with 50 or more HAP tenancies (under 1% of landlords) held 19% of HAP properties ([CSO, HAP 2022](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-hhwl/socialhousinginireland2022-analysisofhousingassistancepaymenthapscheme/keyfindings); [Social Justice Ireland](https://socialjustice.ie/system/files/file-uploads/2024-11/2024-11-18-%20Election%202024%20Briefing%20Housing_0.pdf)).
- **Implication.** Housing is the most obvious first sector for a public-luxury programme. A Vienna-style model of municipal and cost-rental housing at scale is the comparison point (not yet sourced). It is also the area where the cost of the current system to the public is easiest to show.

## 5. Health

- In December 2025, **894,369** people were on outpatient or inpatient/day-case waiting lists, up from 808,061 a year earlier. Sláintecare targets are 10 weeks for outpatients and 12 weeks for procedures ([RTÉ, Jan 2026](https://rte.ie/news/health/2026/0112/1552566-hospital-waiting-lists/)).
- About 45% of people have private health insurance, according to older ESRI and HIA estimates. Sláintecare's stated aim is to end the two-tier system, and implementation has been slow ([LaingBuisson](https://www.laingbuissonnews.com/imtj/ireland-grapples-with-public-vs-private-healthcare/); [WLR](https://wlrfm.com/news/at-least-five-years-before-private-health-insurance-not-needed-says-health-minister-251524)).
- **Implication.** The cross-party 2017 Sláintecare report already endorses universal single-tier care. A socialist design can present itself as *carrying out an existing Irish consensus*. Phase 2 needs a source for the Sláintecare report itself.

## 6. Agriculture and the national herd

- **Cattle:** **6,904,800** in June 2025, down 3.9% on 2024. Dairy cows numbered about 1.59 m (provisional). Cork alone has over 1 m cattle ([CSO, Crops & Livestock June 2025 final](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-clsjf/cropsandlivestocksurveyfinalresultsjune2025)).
- **Emissions:** agriculture was **37.9%** of national greenhouse-gas emissions in 2024, mainly methane from livestock and nitrous oxide from fertiliser and manure. Enteric fermentation is 62.1% of agricultural emissions. Agricultural emissions fell 1.3–1.7% in 2024 (provisional vs final) because cattle numbers fell, partly offset by a 10.6% rise in fertiliser nitrogen ([EPA, agriculture](https://www.epa.ie/our-services/monitoring--assessment/climate-change/ghg/agriculture/); [Agriland](https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/epa-agriculture-ghg-emissions-down-1-7-in-2024)).
- **Implication.** This is the hardest political-economic problem in the design. The herd is central to rural livelihoods, to farm identity and to the export economy, and it is the largest source of emissions. A plan that does not offer farmers a *better* life (guaranteed income, land-use diversification, rewilding payments, cooperative processing, a just transition) will lose rural Ireland. Losing rural Ireland is a recognised route to nationalist capture.

## 7. Data centres and energy

- **Data centres:** 22% of metered electricity in 2024 (6,969 GWh, later revised to 6,973 GWh), up from 5% in 2015. **In 2025 the share was 23%** (7,663 GWh), more than all urban households combined. A small number of facilities account for most of this ([CSO via Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/business/companies/arid-41875214.html); [Silicon Republic](https://siliconrepublic.com/?p=1087546)).
- **Renewables:** 40.7% of electricity in 2023. The target is 80% by 2030, with 9 GW onshore wind, 8 GW solar and 5 GW offshore wind. Offshore wind was only 25 MW in 2023 ([William Fry on CAP25](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/government-approves-climate-action-plan-2025/); [Cornwall Insight](https://cornwall-insight.com/thought-leadership/blog/energy-policy-progress-in-ireland--northern-ireland-looking-back-at-2025); [EEA](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/europe-environment-2025/countries/ireland/renewable-energy-sources)). In January 2026 the Minister indicated Ireland would achieve only about half its emissions-reduction target ([Cornwall Insight](https://cornwall-insight.com/thought-leadership/blog/energy-policy-progress-in-ireland--northern-ireland-looking-back-at-2025)).
- **Implication.** Data centres are where the FDI model and the ecological limit meet. A planned economy has to decide *politically* how much electricity goes to which uses. This is a clear case for allocating by quantity (caps) rather than by price alone.

## 8. The Irish language

- **Census 2022:** 1,873,997 people (40% of those aged 3 and over) said they could speak Irish, but only **71,968** spoke it daily outside education, down 2%. In the Gaeltacht, about 20,000 spoke it daily, 31% of Gaeltacht residents. Other figures circulate (e.g. 15,360) under different definitions ([CSO, Census 2022 Profile 8](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp8/censusofpopulation2022profile8-theirishlanguageandeducation/keyfindings/); [Údarás](https://udaras.ie/en/news/2022-census); [CNaG](https://cnag.ie/en/news/1713-census-results-for-irish-and-the-gaeltacht-very-much-a-%E2%80%98mixed-bag%E2%80%99-showing-positive-and-negative-trends.html)).
- **Implication.** The gap between symbolic status (the "first official language") and daily use is very large. Linguistic rights in the design need *material* support: Gaeltacht housing, employment and Irish-medium services. Status on paper is not enough. The decline in the Gaeltacht is partly a *housing and jobs* problem.

## 9. Neutrality

- The **triple lock** requires a UN mandate, a Government decision and Dáil approval for deployments of more than 12 troops overseas. The Government's **Defence (Amendment) Bill 2026** would remove the UN-mandate requirement. As of September 2026 it was still before the Oireachtas, with the Government aiming to pass it by the end of the year ([Irish Times, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/17/bills-on-ending-triple-lock-e-scooters-and-citizenship-will-come-before-dail-this-term/); [RTÉ, Jun 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/politics/2026/0601/1576150-triple-lock-politics/)).
- **President Catherine Connolly** has said the triple lock is "at the core" of neutrality and has called for a referendum. There is dissent inside Fianna Fáil ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/17/bills-on-ending-triple-lock-e-scooters-and-citizenship-will-come-before-dail-this-term/)).
- **Public opinion.** 68% support the current neutrality model (Irish Times/Ipsos, Sep 2026). In June 2025, 61% backed neutrality and 55% backed "significantly increasing" military capacity ([Irish Times, 26 Sep 2026](https://irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/26/triple-lock-may-prove-tough-to-remove-as-poll-shows-many-voters-aligning-with-opposition/); [Westmeath Independent](https://westmeathindependent.ie/?p=83019)).
- **Implication.** Neutrality is popular. A socialist republic would almost certainly *entrench* it, which means it has to answer the defence question seriously: territorial defence, protection of undersea cables, airspace. Neutrality is not the same as being undefended.

## 10. EU membership constraints

- **Ownership is formally neutral.** Art. 345 TFEU: the Treaties *"shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership."* Nationalisation as such is lawful ([legislation.gov.uk TFEU text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teec/article/345/data.htm?view=plain)). **However**, the CJEU's *Essent* judgment (C-105/12 to C-107/12, 2013) held that a national ban on privatising energy network operators *restricts* free movement of capital (Art. 63), though it can be justified ([Maastricht University](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/node/36860); [Oxford Business Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2025/07/privatizations-golden-shares-investment-screening-and-bailout-agreements)).
- **State aid (Art. 107) and public service compensation.** Under *Altmark* (2003) and Art. 106(2), compensation for services of general economic interest escapes state-aid rules if four criteria are met. Member States rarely manage to show compliance ([Lexxion State Aid Post](https://www.lexxion.eu/stateaidpost/the-perennial-altmark-questions/)).
- **Fiscal rules.** The reformed EU framework (2024) sets a multi-year **net expenditure path** that the Council must endorse. The Irish Fiscal Advisory Council's remit now covers compliance with EU rules ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/economic-governance-framework); [IFAC Box F 2024](https://fiscalcouncil.ie/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Box-F-Substantial-EU-reforms-come-into-place.pdf)).
- **Free movement of capital** (Art. 63) limits capital controls. Treaty-level exceptions exist (e.g. Arts. 65–66) but are narrow. Not yet sourced beyond Essent.

- **Public support for EU membership** is high and stable. It was 82% in the European Movement Ireland poll (Amárach, March 2026; 76% in Northern Ireland), the same as 2025 and down from 88% (Red C) in 2022. Only 45% think the EU is heading in the right direction, down from 58% in 2023 ([RTÉ, May 2026](https://rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0506/1571883-eu-attitudes-ireland/); [gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-foreign-affairs/press-releases/strong-public-support-for-eu-membership-ahead-of-irelands-eu-presidency/)).

**The options**, to be stated, not resolved:

1. **Socialism within the EU**, using the room Art. 345, SGEI and public procurement allow. The constraint is most acute on capital controls, state aid to cooperatives and fiscal rules.
2. **Negotiated opt-outs or derogations.** There is precedent for opt-outs (Denmark, Ireland itself on justice and home affairs), but not for an economic-system opt-out.
3. **Exit.** This carries huge costs for an economy built around single-market access, and complicates the North and the border.
4. **"Disobedience"**: stay in and defy specific rules, accepting infringement proceedings.

Each has a credible case and serious costs. This is for the user to decide.

## 11. The North

- **The legal mechanism.** Under Schedule 1 of the Northern Ireland Act 1998, the Secretary of State *must* call a border poll if it appears likely to them that a majority would vote for a united Ireland. They *may* call one at any time, with at least seven years between polls. In *McCord* (NI Court of Appeal, 2020) the court held there is no duty to publish a policy on when a poll would be called ([Court of Appeal summary](https://www.judiciaryni.uk/files/judiciaryni/decisions/Summary%20of%20judgment%20-%20In%20re%20Raymond%20McCord%20%28Border%20Poll%29.pdf); [Irish Legal News](https://irishlegal.com/article/court-of-appeal-constitutional-value-of-flexibility-not-maintained-by-publication-of-border-poll-policy)).
- **Opinion in the North.** Polls conflict.
  - NILT (June 2025): 42% for the status quo, 36% for unification. 48% of 18–24-year-olds back unity.
  - ARINS/Irish Times (Feb 2025): 48% against unity, 34% for.
  - University of Liverpool (June 2026): unity support at 35.8%, a ten-year low.

  ([ARK/NILT](https://www.ark.ac.uk/ARK/sites/default/files/2025-09/RU165.pdf); [Irish News](https://www.irishnews.com/news/politics/gap-between-irish-unity-supporters-and-unionists-halves-within-12-months-NFE2RP34OJBDTG5QYN3H5D4Z74/); [Irish Times / ARINS](https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2025/02/07/support-for-irish-unification-grows-but-unity-vote-would-be-soundly-defeated-in-north-poll-shows/); [Irish Politics summary](https://www.irishpolitics.ie/united-ireland/))
- **The tension for this project.** The prompt says "a united socialist Ireland". But:
  - **(a)** unity requires consent North and South (Art. 3; the GFA);
  - **(b)** a *socialist* united Ireland would have to win a majority that includes people for whom both unity and socialism are unwelcome;
  - **(c)** unionists and others in the North have rights under the GFA that any successor constitution would have to respect: parity of esteem, the right to identify as British, Irish or both, and citizenship;
  - **(d)** much of the Northern left (and the labour movement as a whole) is cross-community or unionist in background, and "socialism" cannot be allowed to read as "nationalism by another name". Connolly's own record is contested on exactly this point.
- **The options**, to be stated, not resolved:
  1. **Socialist republic in the 26 counties first**, with a door open to unity by consent. The risk is a two-state socialist/capitalist border on the island.
  2. **Unity first, socialism by later democratic decision** in a new all-island constitutional process. Lower stakes for each vote, but socialism is not guaranteed.
  3. **A combined all-island constitutional convention** where both questions are on the table and the North is guaranteed a veto on certain provisions (consociational protections).
  4. **A confederal or federal model** with significant Northern autonomy.

  Each needs careful treatment on the site. No option should be presented as obvious.

## 12. What this means for the design

1. **Legality first.** The transition can be fully constitutional, using Art. 46 and the 1937 precedent. It should be, because the legitimacy it gains is protection against counter-revolution.
2. **The FDI cliff** is the transition's biggest economic risk and needs a staged plan with fallbacks.
3. **Housing and health** are the domains where the public case is strongest and opponents' arguments are weakest. These are the natural first wins.
4. **The herd** is where the design is most likely to fail politically. It needs the most generous transition.
5. **Data centres** are the clearest case for allocating physical resources democratically rather than by price.
6. **The EU and the North** are genuine choices that belong to the user and, inside the fiction, to the people. They should be shown as options, not settled.

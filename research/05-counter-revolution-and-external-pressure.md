# 05 — Counter-revolution, external pressure and collapse

Two very different things are often grouped under "counter-revolution":

1. **Restoration of capitalism from outside or from the right** against a democratic socialist government (Chile 1973).
2. **Suppression by a "socialist" state of democratic socialist reform** (Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968), which the suppressors *called* counter-revolution.

A third pattern, **restoration from inside** by the ruling stratum (USSR 1991), is arguably the most important for design. The site should keep the three apart.

## 1. Chile 1970–73

- **US policy.** At a 15 September 1970 White House meeting, CIA Director Richard Helms's handwritten notes record Nixon's instructions: "1 in 10 chance perhaps, but save Chile!", "$10,000,000 available, more if necessary", *"make the economy scream"* ([FRUS 1969–76 vol. XXI, doc. 93](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v21/d93); [National Security Archive](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/chile/2020-09-15/extreme-option-overthrow-allende)).
- **Covert action.** The US Senate Church Committee staff report *Covert Action in Chile 1963–1973* (December 1975) documents CIA funding of opposition parties and media. On the October 1972 truck-owners' strike, it found that the 40 Committee did not approve direct funding of strikers. However, private-sector groups that received CIA money supported the strike, and one passed money to strikers ([report scan, AARC](https://www.aarclibrary.org/publib/church/reports/vol7/pdf/ChurchV7_13_Appendix.pdf); [summary, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_truckers%27_strike)). The dollar figure is reported second-hand and should be checked against the report before the site uses it.
- **Capital strike and lockouts.** The October 1972 employers' lockout and truck-owners' strike were met by Cybersyn's telex network (file 03) and by the **cordones industriales**. These were territorial councils of workers from neighbouring factories, independent of both the official union body (CUT) and the Popular Unity parties. They kept production going and pushed for factories to be taken over ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cord%C3%B3n_industrial); [biblat oral history](https://biblat.unam.mx/pt/revista/historia-oral/articulo/el-paro-patronal-y-la-formacion-de-los-cordones-industriales-desde-la-memoria-de-sus-protagonistas-chile-1972-1973)).
- **The tension.** The cordones' independence put them at odds with the government's constitutional strategy. Scholars argue that the absence of unified leadership of popular mobilisation helped the opposition in 1973 ([Kondolf, UNCA](https://editorial.unca.edu.ar/Publicacione%20on%20line/CD%20INTERACTIVOS/INTERESCUELAS/CD/PDF/Mesa%2025/KONDOLF.pdf)).
- **The coup** of 11 September 1973 ended it ([Equal Times](https://www.equaltimes.org/chile-forty-years-after-the-coup)).

**Lessons.**

1. External powers will use economic sabotage against a democratic socialist government, and the evidence for this is documented, not speculative.
2. The military's loyalty to the constitution cannot be assumed. Allende's commander-in-chief was Pinochet. That point is general knowledge and needs a specific source for the site.
3. Popular organisations can defend production in a crisis, but if they are not coordinated with the government, a split opens.
4. Economic crisis eroded the government's support among the middle classes, and that was what the coup exploited.

## 2. Hungary 1956

- After the Soviet army's second intervention on 4 November 1956, workers' councils kept resisting. A **Central Workers' Council of Greater Budapest** was formed around 14 November. Its programme was democratic socialist, not restorationist. It negotiated with the Kádár government and the Soviet command, and on 21 November claimed to hold more real power than any other body in Hungary. Its leaders were arrested. Its last appeal was issued, illegally, on 15 January 1957 ([Nagy, *Budapest 1956: The Central Workers' Council*, marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/nagy-balazs/budapest.pdf); [Érudit](https://releve.erudit.org/fr/revues/llt/1989-v24-llt_24/llt24re03.pdf); [IPN journal](https://czasopisma.ipn.gov.pl/index.php/pis/article/view/590)).
- **Lesson.** The workers' councils that Marxist theory names as the organ of workers' power were crushed by a state calling itself socialist, *as* counter-revolution. "Counter-revolution" is a label that power can attach to anything.

## 3. Czechoslovakia 1968

- The Action Programme of April 1968 eased censorship, decentralised the economy and gave Slovakia more autonomy. Dubček's "socialism with a human face" stayed formally Marxist-Leninist ([Britannica](https://britannica.com/topic/Czechoslovak-history/The-Prague-Spring-of-1968); [Seventeen Moments](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1968/crisis-in-czechoslovakia/)).
- Warsaw Pact forces (USSR, Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, GDR) invaded on the night of 20–21 August 1968, citing a fabricated request for help. The **Brezhnev Doctrine**, set out in late 1968 (dated September or November depending on the source), claimed a right to intervene wherever "socialism" was threatened. Husák replaced Dubček in April 1969, beginning "normalisation" ([Seventeen Moments](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1968/crisis-in-czechoslovakia/); [Britannica](https://britannica.com/topic/Czechoslovak-history/The-Prague-Spring-of-1968)).
- **Lesson.** The same as Hungary. Also: membership of an alliance can be used to suppress internal democratic change. That is relevant to any Irish alliance choices.

## 4. 1989–91: restoration from inside

- Kotz and Weir, *Revolution from Above: The Demise of the Soviet System* (Routledge, 1997), based on interviews with more than 50 former officials and others. They argue that the Soviet system was dismantled *by its own party-state elite*, which concluded it would be better off materially as owners in a market economy. It was not simply a collapse under stagnation or popular revolt ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/products/9780203350928); [ProQuest review](https://www.proquest.com/docview/228666933)).
- This fits Trotsky's 1936 prediction that the bureaucracy might itself restore capitalism (file 01). It also fits Djilas: a class that *controls* without *owning* has a motive to turn control into ownership.
- **Lesson.** The surest internal threat to socialised property is the people who manage it. Control rights must be spread out and visible, so that no group is able to turn control into ownership when the chance comes.

## 5. Blockade and external economic warfare

- **Cuba.** The UN General Assembly has called every year since 1992 for the US embargo to end. On 30 October 2024 the vote was 187 for, 2 against (the US and Israel) and 1 abstention. The resolution is non-binding ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2024/10/1156316)).
- **Nicaragua.** In *Nicaragua v. United States* (1986), the International Court of Justice ruled against the US over support for the contras and the mining of Nicaraguan harbours ([ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/70)). I have not checked the judgment text in this pass. The holdings need to be verified before the site quotes them.
- **Lesson for Ireland.** A small, very open economy is extremely exposed to capital flight, to withdrawal of multinationals, to trade sanctions and to financial-market pressure (see file 08). The design must assume this from day one. It cannot rely on great-power goodwill.

## 6. Synthesis

| Threat | Mechanisms seen | What the record suggests |
|---|---|---|
| External covert action | Funding opposition, media, strikes (Chile) | Transparency laws on foreign funding of political actors, applied evenly to all sides; public media pluralism |
| Capital strike / lockout | Chile 1972 | Contingency plans to keep production going (Cybersyn-style); a legal basis to take over abandoned enterprises; workers' councils already in place |
| Military coup | Chile 1973 | Civilian and democratic control of the military; citizen-soldier models; officers recallable; no internal security role |
| Suppression by a "socialist" power | Hungary 1956, CSSR 1968 | Never let "counter-revolution" be a legal category used against peaceful political activity; independent courts |
| Restoration from inside | USSR 1991 | Spread-out, visible control rights; strong legal entrenchment of social ownership (supermajority plus referendum to privatise) |
| Blockade / sanctions | Cuba, Nicaragua | Diversified trade; food and energy security; regional alliances; legal challenges; careful sequencing of the transition |

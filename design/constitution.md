# Bunreacht na hÉireann (Leasaithe) — The Constitution of Ireland (Amended)

*A working draft for a democratic socialist republic. This is a design document, not a legal text: it has not been reviewed by lawyers, and the Irish-language headings are provisional.*

## Preamble

*Réamhrá*

We, the people of Ireland,

Remembering the Democratic Programme of the First Dáil, which declared on the 21st day of January 1919 that the Nation's sovereignty extends to all its material possessions, that all right to private property must be subordinated to the public right and welfare, and that the first duty of the Republic is to the welfare of its children;

Remembering the workers of Limerick, Knocklong and the other soviets of 1919 to 1923, who showed that working people can run the affairs of a town and a trade; William Thompson of Cork and Anna Doyle Wheeler, who joined the cause of co-operation to the emancipation of women; and James Connolly, who warned that a change of flag without a change in the ownership of the country's wealth would leave the people ruled as before;

Remembering also the lesson of every revolution that devoured its own: that power held without check, in the name of the people, becomes power over the people;

Resolved that the wealth of this country shall serve all who live here; that no one shall be without a home, care, health, education or a voice in the decisions that govern their work and their place; that the living world shall be held in trust for those who come after us; that the care of one another shall be counted as the work it is; and that every power created by this Constitution shall be answerable, removable and open to view;

Do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution, amending the Constitution of 1937 in the manner that Constitution provides.

> **Why.** The preamble places the design in an Irish lineage rather than importing a foreign template, and says plainly why every institution below is built to be checked. The 1937 preamble's religious invocation is replaced by a civic one, while the State's name and legal continuity are kept (Q8). Note that the Democratic Programme's own authors may not have meant it to be implemented ([07](research/07-irish-lineage.md)). **Guards against:** FM-14 (civic, not ethnic, lineage), FM-17 (a founding story that isn't ritual). **Sources:** [Democratic Programme (Wikisource)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Democratic_Programme_of_the_First_D%C3%A1il); [Connolly 1897](https://www.marxists.org/archive/connolly/1897/01/socnat.htm); [Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thompson_(philosopher)).

## Part I — The State and its Foundations

*Cuid I — An Stát agus a Bhunsraitheanna*

### Article 1 — Name {core}

1. The name of the State is Éire, or in the English language, Ireland.
2. This Constitution amends the Constitution of 1937 under its Article 46. The State, its laws and its obligations continue except as this Constitution provides.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q8: continuity is part of the legitimacy argument for a lawful transition, and a rename would look like the ritual politics FM-17 warns about. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-10 (a lawful transition gives its opponents no "usurpation" argument). **Source:** [08 §1](research/08-ireland-today.md).

### Article 2 — Sovereignty of the people {eternal}

1. All powers of government derive from the people. They are exercised through the councils, assemblies, elections and referendums established by this Constitution, and through no other means.
2. No person, party, association or organ of State may claim to embody the will of the people or of any class beyond the mandate given to it under this Constitution.

> **Why.** Clause 2 is aimed squarely at substitutionism: the claim, from 1918 onward, that a party *is* the class and so may overrule the class's own organs. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-03. **Source:** [Luxemburg, *The Russian Revolution*, ch. 6](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch06.htm); [02](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md).

### Article 3 — The island and consent {core}

1. It is the firm will of the people of this State that the island of Ireland be united in harmony and friendship, but only by peaceful means and with the consent of a majority of the people, democratically expressed, in both jurisdictions in the island.
2. The State makes a standing offer of an all-island constitutional convention, to be convened whenever a majority in each jurisdiction so consents. The convention shall be free to propose any constitution for the island, including one that differs from this Constitution.
3. Every person born on the island of Ireland is entitled to be part of the Irish nation and to hold Irish citizenship, and has the right to identify as Irish, British or both.
4. Nothing in this Constitution asserts any jurisdiction over Northern Ireland.

> **Why.** Owner's answer to Q3: the 26 counties first, no unity by imposition, and the constitution states the offer and the principle of consent, not the outcome. Clause 1 adapts the existing Article 3, approved by referendum in 1998. Clause 3 carries over the Good Friday Agreement's protection of identity. **Guards against:** FM-14 (socialism subordinated to the national question, or the national question turned into a weapon), FM-13. **Residual risk:** a socialist South next to a capitalist North means two economic systems across one border, and since the republic is outside the EU (Art. 104) that border is also the EU's external frontier for goods (Art. 107). Clause 2 lets a convention propose any constitution, including one for an island inside the EU; the republic's own choice does not bind it. See [13 §4.7](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md). **Sources:** [08 §11](research/08-ireland-today.md); [Arts. 2 and 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_2_and_3_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland).

### Article 4 — Languages {standard}

1. The Irish language, as the national language, is the first official language. The English language is recognised as a second official language. Irish Sign Language is recognised as an official language of the deaf community.
2. The State shall respect and support Ulster Scots and the other languages spoken in Ireland.
3. Linguistic rights are material, not symbolic. The State shall secure housing, employment and public services through Irish in the Gaeltacht, and shall provide education and public services through Irish to any community that seeks them.

> **Why.** The gap between the symbolic status of Irish and its daily use is very large. In 2022 about 72,000 people spoke Irish daily outside education, against 1.87 million who said they could speak it. Decline in the Gaeltacht is partly a housing and jobs problem, so clause 3 binds the State to material measures. **Guards against:** FM-17 (ritual status), FM-14 (language as an identity weapon rather than a living right). **Source:** [CSO Census 2022](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp8/censusofpopulation2022profile8-theirishlanguageandeducation/keyfindings/); [08 §8](research/08-ireland-today.md).

### Article 5 — Social ownership {core}

1. The economic foundation of the State is the social ownership of the principal means of producing wealth, held in the forms set out in Part V.
2. The following are held in national public ownership and shall not be transferred to private ownership except by amendment under Article 109: the public housing stock; the electricity transmission and distribution grid; public water supply and treatment; the public health service; the railways.
3. Personal property, including homes, savings, personal belongings and family farms, is protected. No family farm shall be collectivised without the free consent of those who work it.

> **Why.** Owner's answer to Q2: core public ownership is hard to undo but not impossible. There is no eternity clause for economic arrangements, because that would contradict the commitment to revisability. Clause 3 is there because forced collectivisation was among the worst failures of the Soviet model, and because Ireland's Land Acts already created a nation of smallholders. Winning farmers is the single largest test of the design (FM-14, FM-16). **Guards against:** FM-12 (quiet privatisation by insiders: Kotz & Weir), FM-14. **Residual risk:** entrenchment is a secondary defence; organised popular strength is the real guarantee. **Sources:** [05 §4](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md); [Kotz & Weir (Routledge)](https://www.routledge.com/products/9780203350928).

### Article 6 — Equality and membership {core}

1. All persons are equal before the law. No person shall be discriminated against on grounds of sex, gender, sexual orientation, race, colour, ethnic or national origin, membership of the Traveller community, language, religion or belief, political opinion, disability, age, family status, class or property.
2. Membership of the political community is civic. It rests on residence and citizenship, never on ancestry, religion or ethnicity.
3. The State shall take positive measures to overcome inequality where it has been produced by law, history or the organisation of work and care.

> **Why.** A civic definition of membership is the main constitutional defence against nationalist capture. The Traveller community is named because Irish law recognised Traveller ethnicity only in 2017 (to be sourced) and discrimination persists. **Guards against:** FM-14, FM-18.

### Article 7 — Neutrality {core}

1. Ireland is a neutral state. It shall not join any military alliance or mutual-defence pact, nor permit foreign military bases on its territory.
2. Neutrality does not mean being undefended. The State shall maintain the capacity to defend its territory, airspace, seas and undersea infrastructure (Article 87).
3. The State shall pursue peace, disarmament and the peaceful settlement of disputes, and shall support the United Nations.

> **Why.** Neutrality is popular: 68% backed the current model in September 2026. A socialist republic would entrench it, so it must answer the defence question seriously and not treat neutrality as pacifism. **Guards against:** FM-10 (alliance membership used to suppress internal change, as in Czechoslovakia in 1968), FM-11. **Sources:** [08 §9](research/08-ireland-today.md); [05 §3](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md).

## Part II — Rights

*Cuid II — Cearta*

### Article 8 — Elections {eternal}

1. Every citizen of the age of sixteen years or over, and every person lawfully resident in the State for five years or more, has the right to vote by secret ballot in elections and referendums.
2. Elections to every council and assembly established by this Constitution shall be held at the intervals it fixes. No law, emergency or decision of any body may postpone an election or extend the term of any office.
3. Every citizen eligible to vote may stand for election to any council or assembly as an individual, whether or not they are a member of any party or association.

> **Why.** Fixed elections that no one can postpone, and a guaranteed right to stand as an individual, are the conditions under which the people can change their minds. They are eternal (owner's answer to Q2). Clause 3 holds under every Q1 option and is how the leading party "has to earn its role in practice" (owner's answer to Q1). Votes at sixteen and for long-term residents widen the political community civically (FM-14). **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-15. **Source:** [Luxemburg: "without general elections… public life gradually falls asleep"](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch06.htm).

### Article 9 — Recall {eternal}

1. Every person holding elected or delegated office under this Constitution may be recalled by those who elected or delegated them, in the manner and by the thresholds that law prescribes. No law may make recall unattainable in practice.
2. A recall petition signed by one fifth of the electors of a constituency, ward or workplace shall bring about a recall vote within forty-five days.

> **Why.** Councillors *"responsible and revocable at short terms"* (Marx on the Paris Commune). A fixed petition threshold stops a hostile majority from setting thresholds so high that recall is a dead letter. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-03, FM-08. **Residual risk:** recall can be used for harassment or by organised minorities; the 20% threshold is a judgement call. **Source:** [Marx, *The Civil War in France*, ch. 5](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm).

### Article 10 — Expression and a free press {eternal}

1. Every person has the right to seek, receive and impart information and opinions of every kind, including opinions hostile to this Constitution and to socialism.
2. The press and every other medium are free. There shall be no prior censorship.
3. No law may make the holding or expression of an opinion an offence. The law may restrict expression only to protect individuals from direct incitement to violence, from harassment and from defamation, by measures that are narrowly defined, necessary and proportionate, and subject to the courts.
4. The State shall not operate, fund or direct any covert media or influence operation aimed at people in Ireland.

> **Why.** *"Freedom is always and exclusively freedom for the one who thinks differently"* (Luxemburg). Clause 1 explicitly protects anti-socialist opinion: under the default Q1 answer, pro-capitalist citizens have no electoral party, so their freedom of speech must be beyond doubt. Clause 4 closes the door on a Ministry of Truth. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-04, FM-17 (Kern & Hainmueller found that openness *stabilised* the GDR). **Sources:** [Luxemburg](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch06.htm); [Kern & Hainmueller 2009](https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/OVM2009.pdf).

### Article 11 — Assembly and association {core}

1. Every person has the right to assemble peaceably and to form and join associations, trade unions, co-operatives, campaigns and societies of every kind.
2. Trade unions are independent of the State and of every party. Workers have the right to strike, including against public and socially owned enterprises and against the State itself.

3. The right to form an association to contest elections under a party label is governed by Part III, Chapter 4. Associations of every other kind, including those that advocate private ownership or oppose this Constitution, are free to organise, publish, assemble and campaign.

3. Any association may register as a political party and contest elections, subject only to Article 30.

3. Associations of every kind are free to organise, publish, assemble and campaign. No association may appear on a ballot paper or receive public election funding (Article 30).

> **Why.** Independent unions with the right to strike *against the State* are the Kronstadt and Solidarność lesson: a workers' state that forbids workers' independent organisation has stopped being one. Clause 3 changes with the Q1 setting. Under the default, it is where the design **restricts political association**, and the text says so openly rather than hiding it. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-13. **Residual risk (default Q1):** a large minority with speech but no electoral vehicle (FM-17, FM-10). See the honesty notes on the Choices page. **Sources:** [02](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md); [10 §2.4](research/10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md).

### Article 12 — Independent courts and due process {eternal}

1. Judicial power is exercised only by courts established under Part XI, by judges who are independent and subject only to this Constitution and the law.
2. No person shall be deprived of liberty save in accordance with law. Every person detained has the right to be brought promptly before a court, to know the charge, to legal assistance and to a public trial.
3. No tribunal, commission or other body outside the courts shall impose any criminal penalty or deprivation of liberty.
4. No act is an offence because of its political character. There shall be no offence of "counter-revolution", "anti-State activity", "sabotage of socialism" or any like category defined by opinion or allegiance rather than by a specific act.

> **Why.** The Cheka's slide from investigating to punishing outside the courts is the archetype of security-state capture. Clause 4 bars the legal category used to crush the Hungarian workers' councils in 1956 and Kronstadt in 1921. Real sabotage (arson, violence, theft) is already a crime as a specific act. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-10's twin danger, FM-15. **Sources:** [01 §7](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md); [05 §2](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md).

### Article 13 — The sunset on emergency powers {eternal}

1. Every power exercised under a declaration of emergency lapses automatically at the end of the period fixed by Article 90. No law and no amendment may abolish, suspend or extend that limit.
2. The rights in Articles 8, 9, 10, 12 and 15 may not be suspended in any emergency.

> **Why.** Weimar's Article 48 was meant for rare use and became the routine means of governing without parliament, and then the legal path to dictatorship. The Cheka, the 1921 "temporary" ban on factions and the Brezhnev Doctrine's "defence of socialism" are the socialist equivalents. Owner's answer to Q2: the sunset is eternal. **Guards against:** FM-15, FM-04. **Source:** [Article 48 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_48_of_the_Weimar_Constitution); [SYNTHESIS FM-15](research/SYNTHESIS.md).

### Article 14 — The democratic core {eternal}

1. Articles 2, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13 and this Article, and the independence of the courts under Article 92, form the democratic core of this Constitution. They may not be amended, suspended or repealed by any procedure.
2. Any measure purporting to do so is void, and every person and organ of State is released from any duty to obey it.

> **Why.** Owner's answer to Q2: the eternity clause covers *democratic rights only*. These are the conditions under which everything else, including social ownership, remains revisable by the people. Economic arrangements are deliberately left out. **Precedent:** German Basic Law Art. 79(3). **Residual risk:** an eternity clause does not stop a revolution; it only denies it legality. **Source:** [Basic Law (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany).

### Article 15 — Life, dignity and bodily integrity {core}

1. Every person has the right to life, dignity and bodily integrity. No person shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. There shall be no death penalty.
2. No person shall be held in any institution against their will except by order of a court under law, reviewed at short intervals.

> **Why.** Clause 2 speaks to Ireland's own history of coercive confinement in "care" institutions as much as to the Gulag. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-18.

### Article 16 — Privacy and one's own file {core}

1. Every person has the right to privacy in their home, correspondence and communications, and to the protection of their personal data.
2. Every person has the right to see every record any organ of State holds about them, including records of the intelligence service, subject only to delay of not more than five years where a court finds that disclosure would endanger a current operation or another person.

> **Why.** After 1990 Germany opened the Stasi files to the people they were kept on; the Stasi Records Act still needs sourcing. A file you can read is a file that is harder to abuse. **Guards against:** FM-04. **Residual risk:** this burdens legitimate counter-espionage, so the five-year delay must be strictly policed by courts.

### Article 17 — Conscience and belief {standard}

1. Freedom of conscience, religion and belief, including the freedom to hold no religion, is guaranteed.
2. The State is neutral between religions and beliefs, and shall endow none. Publicly funded schools, hospitals and care services shall be under public or democratic co-operative control and open to all without religious test.

> **Why.** Patronage of schools and hospitals is a long-standing Irish question. Clause 2 completes a separation that is already under way. **Guards against:** FM-18 (care institutions run outside democratic control), FM-02.

### Article 18 — Reproductive autonomy {core}

1. Every person has the right to decide freely whether and when to have children, and to the information and health care necessary to do so, including contraception and the termination of pregnancy, as law provides.
2. No law shall restrict this right for demographic, economic or national ends.

> **Why.** The USSR legalised abortion in 1920 and banned it in 1936 for pronatalist reasons; births rose, and so did maternal and infant deaths. Rights granted for reasons of state are withdrawn for reasons of state, so this is entrenched at the core tier. **Guards against:** FM-18. **Source:** [VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/beneath-ban-abortion-evidence-ussr); [11 §3](research/11-social-reproduction.md).

### Article 19 — Housing {core}

1. Every person has the right to a secure, warm, accessible and affordable home.
2. The State shall secure this right chiefly through public and co-operative housing available to all at cost rent, and shall ensure that no household pays more than one fifth of its income in rent for a public or co-operative home.
3. No person shall be evicted into homelessness.

> **Why.** In August 2026 a record 17,885 people were in emergency accommodation. Housing is where the public case is strongest. Cost rent for everyone, not just for the poor, makes public housing a universal service rather than a residual one (FM-17: public luxury). **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-01 (Trotsky's "policeman over the queue" grows where housing is scarce). **Source:** [08 §4](research/08-ireland-today.md); [Trotsky, ch. 5](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1936/revbet/ch05.htm).

### Article 20 — Health {core}

1. Every person has the right to the highest attainable standard of health and to health care free at the point of use, through a single-tier public health service.
2. No person may obtain faster or better access to any publicly owned health facility by payment.

> **Why.** 894,369 people were on waiting lists in December 2025. The cross-party Sláintecare consensus already aims at single-tier care. Private practice outside public facilities remains lawful as small private enterprise, which is an honest concession with an FM-17 trade-off. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-01 (no special hospitals for officials). **Source:** [08 §5](research/08-ireland-today.md).

### Article 21 — Care {core}

1. Every person has the right to receive the care they need, and every person who gives care has the right to support in doing so.
2. The State recognises that the care of children, of the sick, of disabled people and of older people is work on which the whole of society depends, whoever performs it, and shall count, plan and resource it as such.
3. The State shall provide universal childcare, home care and residential care through the National Care Service. It shall pay an income to those who give substantial unpaid care in the home, and shall respect each person's choice between public care and care by family, with support for either.
4. Care work shall be shared between women and men. Law shall reserve for each parent a period of well-paid parental leave that cannot be transferred to the other.

> **Why.** This replaces Article 41.2 of the 1937 text. The 2024 care referendum, which offered symbolic recognition with no material guarantee, was rejected 73.9% No. Clause 3 is enforceable, and it respects the choice to care at home because socialised care can slide into institutionalisation (Ireland's own history). Clause 4 changes *men's* role as well as women's, which no Eastern Bloc state did. **Guards against:** FM-18. **Sources:** [11](research/11-social-reproduction.md); [Electoral Commission](https://electoralcommission.ie/referendum/referendums-on-family-and-care).

### Article 22 — Education {standard}

1. Every person has the right to free education at every level, and to return to education at any time in life.
2. Education shall develop the whole person, including practical, artistic and civic capacities, and the capacity to take part in self-government.

### Article 23 — Work, income and time {core}

1. Every person able and wishing to work has the right to socially useful work at a living wage. The duty to secure this falls on society, through the Job Guarantee Service, not on any single enterprise.
2. Every person has the right to an income sufficient to live with dignity, whether or not they are in paid work.
3. Every person has the right to free time. The standard working week shall be reduced by law as the productivity of labour rises (Article 72).

> **Why.** If the job guarantee sits with society rather than with the firm, enterprises can face hard budgets (Kornai) without workers being thrown into destitution. Free time is the headline good that answers Fisher's challenge to offer something people actually desire. **Guards against:** FM-06, FM-07, FM-17. **Sources:** [03 §3](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md); [10 §3.2](research/10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md).

### Article 24 — Transport, energy and connection {standard}

1. Every person has the right to affordable public transport, a basic allowance of household energy free of charge, and connection to communications networks.
2. The State shall provide public transport to every community, including rural communities, at a standard that makes a private car unnecessary for the ordinary business of life.

> **Why.** Cutting car dependence without first building rural transit would hit the countryside hardest (FM-14). The right comes before the restriction. **Source:** [09 §3](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md).

### Article 25 — Culture {standard}

1. Every person has the right to take part in cultural life, to enjoy the arts, and to the beauty and amenity of their surroundings.
2. Public buildings, spaces and services shall be designed for beauty and pleasure as well as use.

> **Why.** Morris: *"We must begin to build up the ornamental part of life"*. Design and beauty are built in from the start, not treated as luxuries for later. **Guards against:** FM-17. **Source:** [Morris, "Useful Work versus Useless Toil"](https://libcom.org/article/useful-work-versus-useless-toil-william-morris).

### Article 26 — A living world {core}

1. Every person has the right to a healthy environment, and future generations have a claim on the living world that the State shall hold in trust.
2. Water, the seabed, the foreshore, peatlands and the atmosphere are common goods. They shall not be owned privately except as law permits for use that respects their common character.

### Article 27 — Enforcement of social rights {core}

1. The rights in Articles 19 to 26 are enforceable in the courts. A court that finds the State in breach may order it to remedy the breach within a period the court fixes, and to report to the court on its progress.
2. Where a right can be realised only progressively, the State shall publish a timetable, and the courts may enforce it.

> **Why.** Article 45 of the 1937 Constitution made social policy *"for the general guidance of the Oireachtas"* only, and unenforceable. This Article makes the Democratic Programme's ideals justiciable. **Residual risk (a left critique):** this gives unelected judges power over budgets. The remedy is structural orders with timetables, not judges writing budgets. **Source:** [08 §1](research/08-ireland-today.md).

## Part III — Democratic Structure

*Cuid III — Struchtúr Daonlathach*

### Article 28 — Principles of office {core}

1. Every person who holds elected, delegated or appointed office under this Constitution, or senior office in any party or socially owned enterprise, shall:
   1. be paid no more than the wage of a skilled worker, as fixed by the Pay and Privileges Panel under Article 100, and receive no benefit in kind that is not available to every resident;
   2. use the same public housing, health, education and transport services as every other resident, and hold no right of priority in them;
   3. publish each year a declaration of their income, assets, gifts and the interests of their household;
   4. serve no more than two consecutive terms in the same office, and no more than ten years in any one office in a lifetime.
2. Every council, assembly and executive body shall be chaired by two co-chairs, of whom at least one shall be a woman.
3. A person who has held senior office shall not, for three years afterwards, take any paid position with a body they regulated, funded or oversaw.
4. No amendment or law that lengthens a term of office or relaxes a term limit shall apply to any person who holds that office, or has held it in the preceding ten years, when the change is first proposed.

> **Why.** This one article carries most of the anti-nomenklatura design. Clause 1.1 is the Commune's *"workman's wage"*. Clause 1.2 abolishes the special shops, clinics and dachas Voslensky describes. Clause 1.4 is rotation against Michels's iron law, and clause 4 means no one can change the term limits for their own benefit. Clause 2 adapts Rojava's co-chair rule. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-03, FM-12, FM-18. **Residual risk:** capped pay may drive skilled people abroad in Ireland's open labour market, and privilege can move into informal networks, which is why Article 103 looks at networks and not only posts. **Sources:** [01](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md); [Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm); [06 §6](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 29 — Civic time {standard}

1. Every person elected, delegated or chosen by lot to serve on any council, assembly or panel is entitled to leave from work on full pay for the time that service requires, and their employment is protected.
2. Every person with caring responsibilities who is so chosen is entitled to substitute care at public expense for the duration of that service.

> **Why.** Michels: members' lack of time hands power to full-time officials. Clause 2 means carers, mostly women, are not shut out of participation. **Guards against:** FM-08, FM-18, FM-01.

### Chapter 1 — Parties and elections

:::variant Q1=A
### Article 30 — The party {core}

1. One socialist party, organised under this Chapter, may nominate candidates under a party label and receive public funding as an electoral organisation. No other association may nominate candidates, run slates of candidates, or fund election campaigns.
2. The party holds no reserved seat, veto, power of appointment, or right of command over any organ of State, council, enterprise or union. Its leading role is political and must be won at each election against independent candidates.
3. Any citizen may join or leave the party freely. Membership confers no advantage in public employment, housing, education or any public service, and to give or seek such advantage is an offence.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q1: a leading socialist party with guaranteed internal democracy; not open multi-party competition; pro-capitalist parties are not part of the political order after the transition. **This is the riskiest choice in the design** against FM-02 and FM-17, and this Article is where political association is restricted. Clause 2 means the party's leading role is never a matter of law; clause 3 abolishes the nomenklatura's career ladder. **Guards against (partially):** FM-01, FM-02, FM-13. **Thinnest points:** an organised party competing against unorganised individuals wins most contests, so this can become a monopoly in practice; and a pro-market minority with speech but no electoral vehicle may lose faith in the system or go underground. See the Choices page. **This Article is also the main reason the republic is outside the European Union** (Art. 104): it cannot be reconciled with the pluralism required by Article 2 TEU, and it is likely to fail the European Court of Human Rights' test for restrictions on parties (Art. 105).

### Article 31 — Democracy within the party {core}

1. Members of the party have the right to form platforms and factions, to publish their views inside and outside the party, and to contest every internal election on a platform. No member shall be disciplined or expelled for their views or for organising a platform.
2. Every office in the party is filled by secret ballot of the members or of their elected delegates. Internal bodies shall be elected by proportional representation, so that every significant platform is represented.
3. Officials of the party are subject to Article 28: a skilled worker's wage, declarations of assets, and term limits with rotation.
4. The party's accounts, membership numbers and internal election results are published each year.

> **Why.** This directly reverses the 1921 resolution "On Party Unity", which banned factions. Lenin called it temporary; Stalin used it against every rival. Disagreement that is legal and organised does not need to go underground or end in a split or a purge. **Guards against:** FM-13, FM-02, FM-03. **Residual risk:** factions protected on paper are not the same as factions safe in practice, because careers still depend on the leadership's favour. **Source:** [02 §2](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md); [Wikipedia, Ban on factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_on_factions_in_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union).

### Article 32 — Separation of party and State {core}

1. No person shall at the same time hold senior office in the party and senior office in the State. Senior office in the State means membership of the Executive Council, the chair of any assembly, judicial office, the headship of any independent body under Part XII, the command of any police, intelligence or defence body, or the headship of a national public enterprise.
2. The party, its organs and officials shall not instruct, direct or receive reports from any police, intelligence or defence body, any court, the Audit Office, or any other independent body. Any such instruction is void, and to give or obey it is an offence.
3. A panel of the Citizens' Chamber, chosen by lot under Article 39, shall audit the party's internal elections and finances each year, with full access to its records, and shall publish its findings.

> **Why.** The Cheka worked as a *"direct agency of the Party"*. Here, the party is barred from commanding any coercive or checking body. The audit by lot answers the question of who watches the party. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-01, FM-12. **Thinnest point:** loyal non-office-holders can be appointed to state posts, so the audit panel must examine networks as well as titles. **Source:** [01 §7](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md).

### Article 33 — Renewal {core}

1. In the tenth year after this Constitution comes into force, and every tenth year after that, a referendum shall ask whether the party shall keep the status given by Article 30.
2. If the majority vote No, Article 30 ceases to have effect and Chapter 1 is read with the provisions that would apply under the rules for several socialist parties, as set out in Schedule A.

> **Why.** *Drafter's addition (DECISIONS D-30); the owner may remove it.* The owner wants the party's authority to come from trust, not a clause. A periodic renewal makes that claim testable at the ballot box and gives a lawful path to plurality that does not need a rupture. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-02. **Residual risk:** a party that has governed for ten years campaigns with every advantage of incumbency.

### Article 30 — Political parties {core}

1. Any association may register as a political party and contest elections, provided that its constitution and programme accept the democratic core of this Constitution (Article 14) and the social ownership of the sectors listed in Article 5.
2. Only the Constitutional Court may refuse or cancel a registration, on application by the Electoral Commission, and only on the ground that the party's own published programme or constitution rejects what clause 1 requires. A party's campaign to amend this Constitution by the lawful procedure is not such a rejection, except as regards the democratic core.
3. No party holds any reserved seat, veto, power of appointment or right of command over any organ of State.

> **Why.** Several socialist parties: the multi-party soviets of 1917, and the Kronstadt sailors' demand for freedom for "left socialist parties and anarchists". The line is drawn by a court on narrow, published grounds, never by the governing party. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-13. **Residual risk:** the June 1918 expulsions were justified as defence against counter-revolution, and whoever controls the court controls what counts as "socialist". **Sources:** [02](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md).

### Article 31 — Democracy within parties {standard}

1. Every registered party shall fill its offices by secret ballot of its members, permit internal platforms, and publish its accounts each year.
2. Officials of every party are subject to Article 28.

### Article 32 — Separation of party and State {core}

1. No person shall at the same time hold senior office in a party and senior office in the State, as defined by law.
2. No party, organ or official of a party shall instruct any police, intelligence or defence body, court or independent body. Any such instruction is void, and to give or obey it is an offence.
3. A panel of the Citizens' Chamber shall audit the finances of every party each year.

### Article 33 — Public funding {standard}

1. Public funding for parties and candidates shall be distributed by a formula fixed by law that favours small and new parties, and no private donation to any party or candidate shall exceed an amount fixed by law.

### Article 30 — Elections without parties {core}

1. Candidates for every council and assembly stand as individuals. No party label or name of any association shall appear on a ballot paper, and no association shall nominate candidates or run slates.
2. Associations of every kind may publish, campaign and endorse ideas. Public election funding is paid equally to individual candidates.

> **Why.** Councils with no parties remove the party machine that Michels and Voslensky identify as the engine of a new class. **Residual risk:** in Uganda (1986–2005) and Rojava, "no parties" meant one hidden party. Informal slates form with less transparency than parties have. **Sources:** [FES on Uganda](https://collections.fes.de/publikationen/download/ftpack/plain/447697); [06 §6](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 31 — Transparency of associations {standard}

1. Every association that spends money on public campaigning shall publish its accounts, donors and officers.

### Article 32 — No command over the State {core}

1. No association, organ or official of an association shall instruct any police, intelligence or defence body, court or independent body. Any such instruction is void, and to give or obey it is an offence.

### Article 33 — Strengthened sortition {standard}

1. In addition to its powers under Article 39, the Citizens' Chamber may initiate legislation, which the National Assembly shall debate and vote on within ninety days.

### Chapter 2 — Councils and assemblies

### Article 34 — Workplace councils {core}

1. In every socially owned enterprise and every public body, and in every private enterprise employing twenty or more people, the workers shall elect a workplace council by secret ballot each year. Its members may be recalled at any time.
2. The workplace council of a socially owned enterprise appoints and may dismiss its management, approves its plan and budget, and decides questions of work organisation, safety and job design. Article 53 protects the rights of users and the community.
3. Workplace councils may federate by sector and by county. The Federation of Workplace Councils is independent of the State and of every party.

> **Why.** The factory committees of 1917 were subordinated to the State and then replaced by one-man management in 1918. Here, self-management has a constitutional floor and its own federation. Councils elect individuals, not party lists (owner's answer to Q1). **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-02, FM-07 (voice at work). **Residual risk:** insider capture, which is addressed by Articles 50 and 54. **Source:** [02 §5](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md); [06 §3](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 35 — Local assemblies {core}

1. Every parish or urban ward of between two thousand and ten thousand residents shall have a local assembly open to every resident aged sixteen or over. It shall meet at least four times a year and whenever one hundred residents request it.
2. The local assembly decides the use of its share of the participatory budget (Article 57), elects delegates to the county council, and may summon any public official serving its area to answer questions.
3. Each local assembly elects a standing committee by lot from among willing residents, renewed each year, to prepare its business.

> **Why.** Ireland has almost no tier of government at the scale of a parish or town: its 31 councils average about 160,000 people each. Kerala's ward assemblies show the tier can be created, and Porto Alegre shows it can be drained of money if its budget is not entrenched. **Guards against:** FM-08, FM-01. **Sources:** [08 §2](research/08-ireland-today.md); [06 §5, §9](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 36 — County councils {standard}

1. Each county and city has a council. Two thirds of its members are elected by proportional representation by single transferable vote in local electoral areas, and one third are delegated by its local assemblies. All serve four-year terms and may be recalled.
2. County councils govern local services, planning, housing allocation and policing priorities, and elect the county's delegates to the Congress of Councils.
3. The county council appoints its chief executive by open competition and may dismiss them. Executive functions are exercised under the council's direction.

> **Why.** Since 2014, power in Irish local government has been divided between councillors and an appointed chief executive. This reverses that. Mixing STV election with delegation keeps the Irish electoral tradition while tying the county to its localities. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-08. **Source:** [localgov.ie](https://localgov.ie/about-local-government/the-elected-council).

### Article 37 — The National Assembly {core}

1. The National Assembly (Tionól Náisiúnta) is the legislature. It has one hundred and sixty members elected for four years by proportional representation by single transferable vote in constituencies of between three and five seats.
2. Members may be recalled by their constituency under Article 9. A recalled member is replaced by a by-election, in which they may not stand.
3. The Assembly makes laws, elects and may recall the Executive Council, approves the national plan and the budget, and declares or ends an emergency under Part X.

### Article 38 — The Congress of Councils {standard}

1. The Congress of Councils (Comhdháil na gComhairlí) consists of delegates of the county councils and of the Federation of Workplace Councils in equal numbers, as law provides. It meets at least twice a year.
2. The Congress may propose laws to the National Assembly, which must debate and vote on them within ninety days. Its approval is required, together with the Assembly's, for the national investment plan (Article 56).
3. Delegates to the Congress are mandated and recallable by the bodies that send them.

> **Why.** This is the council channel: representation through workplaces and localities, alongside the Assembly elected by territory. Unlike the multi-tier indirect elections of Soviet practice, it is a *second* channel, never the only one. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-08. **Source:** [02](research/02-suppression-of-pluralism.md).

### Article 39 — The Citizens' Chamber {core}

1. The Citizens' Chamber (Seomra na Saoránach) consists of one hundred and twenty residents chosen by lot, stratified by county, sex, age and class. Half are replaced each year. Members serve two years, on full pay with civic leave, and may not serve twice.
2. The Chamber, and panels it chooses by lot from among its members, have the following binding powers and no others:
   1. to fix the pay and permitted benefits of every office under Article 28, and to audit compliance (Article 100);
   2. to approve or refuse any extension of a declaration of emergency (Article 90);
   3. to oversee the police, intelligence and defence services through the Security Oversight Committee (Article 85);
   4. by a two-thirds vote, within sixty days of the passing of any Act, to require that the Act be put to the people in a referendum before it comes into force;
   5. to audit internal party elections and finances as Chapter 1 provides;
   6. to set the topics of citizens' assemblies under Article 40.
3. The Chamber appoints and may dismiss its own secretariat. No member of the secretariat may serve more than six years, and the Chamber may call on any expert it chooses.
4. Members may be removed only by the Chamber itself, by a two-thirds vote, for misconduct.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q5: binding, but bounded. The Chamber gets power exactly where elected politicians have a conflict of interest (their own pay, emergency powers, security oversight) plus the right to force a referendum, and ordinary law-making stays with elected, recallable delegates. Ireland's citizens' assemblies show the method works; FM-08 shows advisory bodies fade. **Precedents:** Athens's *boulē*; Ostbelgien's permanent *Bürgerrat* (2019); Ireland 2012–2023. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-04, FM-15, FM-12, FM-08. **Residual risk:** secretariat capture, since members chosen by lot depend on staff. Hence clause 3: the Chamber hires its own staff and rotates them. **Sources:** [06 §10–11](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 40 — Citizens' assemblies {standard}

1. Citizens' assemblies of between ninety-nine and one hundred and fifty members chosen by lot shall be convened on topics set by the Citizens' Chamber, by the National Assembly, or by a citizens' initiative supported by fifty thousand electors.
2. The National Assembly shall respond to every recommendation of a citizens' assembly within six months, by adopting it or by publishing its reasons for not doing so. A recommendation that has not been adopted within two years shall be put to referendum if the Citizens' Chamber so decides.

> **Why.** This keeps Ireland's own proven method and fixes its weakness: governments currently choose which recommendations to act on, and many lapse. **Guards against:** FM-08, FM-17 (rituals of participation that change nothing breed cynicism, as Yurchak shows). **Source:** [06 §10](research/06-positive-models.md); [10 §2.1](research/10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md).

### Article 41 — Referendums and citizens' initiative {core}

1. A proposal for a law or an amendment to this Constitution supported by the signatures of one hundred thousand electors shall be put to the National Assembly. If the Assembly does not enact it unchanged within six months, it shall be put to the people in a referendum.
2. Referendum campaigns shall be governed by the Electoral Commission, with equal public funding for each side and limits on spending.

### Article 42 — Making laws {standard}

1. A Bill becomes law when passed by the National Assembly, subject to Articles 38, 39 and 46.
2. Every Bill shall be published with an assessment of its effects on the physical budgets under Article 63, on care and unpaid work, and on equality.

> **Why.** Clause 2 makes the ecological and care effects of every law visible. Care that is not measured is not planned (FM-18), and physical limits that are not checked are not kept (FM-16).

## Part IV — The Executive and the President

*Cuid IV — An Feidhmeannas agus an tUachtarán*

### Article 43 — The Executive Council {core}

1. Executive power is exercised by the Executive Council (Comhairle Feidhmiúcháin) of seven members, elected by the National Assembly by proportional representation from among its members or from outside it.
2. The Council decides collectively. Each member directs one or more departments. The chair rotates each year among its members, and no member may chair in two consecutive years. The chair is first among equals and has no power that the Council does not grant.
3. Members of the Council are paid a skilled worker's wage and are subject to Article 28.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q6: the executive is elected by, and recallable at any time by, the assembly, as in the Paris Commune. The collective, rotating form follows the Swiss Federal Council (DECISIONS D-28). There is no single executive office for a cult to grow around. **Guards against:** FM-03, FM-01. **Residual risk:** collective leadership can be slow in a crisis; Article 89 lets the Council act quickly but only for a short time. **Sources:** [Swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-oddities/why-switzerland-has-no-head-of-state/90704688); [Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm).

### Article 44 — Recall of the Executive {core}

1. The National Assembly may at any time, by a majority of its members, recall the Executive Council or any member of it, and elect a replacement in the same sitting.
2. A recall does not dissolve the Assembly or trigger an election.

> **Why.** Recall is cheap and carries no penalty for the body that uses it. Under Weimar, revoking an emergency decree triggered dissolution, which made the check worthless in practice. **Guards against:** FM-03, FM-15. **Source:** [Rempel](https://johndclare.net/Rempel_Weimar23.htm).

### Article 45 — The public service {standard}

1. Appointments to the public service are made on merit by open competition administered by an independent Public Appointments Commission. No appointment below the Executive Council may be made on political grounds, and no list of posts reserved for approval by any party or body shall exist.
2. Public servants have the right and the duty to refuse an unlawful instruction and to report it under Article 96.

> **Why.** "Nomenklatura" literally meant a list of posts requiring party approval. Clause 1 abolishes the list. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-12. **Source:** [01 §4](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md).

### Article 46 — The President {standard}

1. The President (Uachtarán na hÉireann) is elected directly by the people for a single term of seven years and is paid a skilled worker's wage.
2. The President may refer any Bill to the Constitutional Court before signing it, and shall convene the National Assembly if the Executive Council fails to do so.
3. The President has no other executive power.

> **Why.** This keeps the familiar Irish office as a ceremonial guardian with the Article 26 reference power, limited to a single term. It provides continuity without creating a seat of power. **Guards against:** FM-03.

## Part V — The Economy

*Cuid V — An Geilleagar*

### Article 47 — Forms of ownership {core}

1. The means of production are held in the following forms:
   1. **national public ownership**, for the sectors in Article 5 and those designated by law;
   2. **municipal ownership** by county councils and local assemblies;
   3. **co-operative ownership** by workers, consumers, producers or a combination of them;
   4. **small private enterprise**, owned and worked by individuals and families;
   5. **foreign enterprise** operating under a charter (Article 52).
2. A private enterprise that employs more than fifty people shall, within the period law provides, be converted into a co-operative or another socially owned form, with compensation under Article 62. Its workers have a right of first offer whenever such an enterprise is sold or its owner retires.

> **Why.** Nove's *feasible socialism*: several forms, each suited to a different scale, instead of one form for everything. The threshold for conversion is a judgement call (the owner may wish to revisit it). **Guards against:** FM-05 (one centre cannot know everything), FM-17 (variety). **Source:** [03 §4](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md).

### Article 48 — National public enterprises {standard}

1. Each national public enterprise is governed by a board of which one third is elected by its workers, one third represents its users and the communities it serves, and one third is appointed by the Executive Council with the consent of the National Assembly.
2. Its accounts, plans and performance are published in full (Article 59).

> **Why.** This follows Devine's stakeholder governance: interests are assumed to differ and are made to negotiate in the open. ESB's planned end of coal at Moneypoint shows that a public utility can carry out a planned phase-out. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-09, FM-16. **Source:** [03 §7](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md); [ESB](https://esb.ie/news---insights/press-releases/article/2025/06/20/after-40-years--esb-announces-that-coal-generation-has-ended-at-moneypoint-power-station).

### Article 49 — Municipal enterprise {standard}

1. County councils and local assemblies may own and run enterprises, including housing, energy co-operatives, food halls, repair workshops and local transport.

### Article 50 — Co-operatives {core}

1. Every co-operative shall admit as a full member, with equal vote, every worker who has completed a probationary period of no more than one year.
2. Workers who are not members shall not exceed one tenth of the workforce of any co-operative, save for seasonal work as law provides.
3. A co-operative's reserves are indivisible. They may not be distributed to members. On dissolution or conversion, its net assets pass to the Co-operative Development Fund.
4. No co-operative may be converted into a company owned by shareholders.

> **Why.** This is the owner's FM-09 rule. Yugoslav self-managed firms had reasons not to hire, and Mondragon's member-owners were only about 40% of its group workforce, with contract staff unprotected when Fagor failed. Clause 4 answers Ireland's own history of converting creameries into public limited companies (still to be sourced). **Guards against:** FM-09, FM-12. **Residual risk:** compulsory membership may dilute the sense of ownership; indivisible reserves may make members less willing to invest. **Sources:** [06 §3, §8](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 51 — Small private enterprise {standard}

1. Individuals and families may own and run enterprises employing up to fifty people, and family farms of any size that law permits. Shops, cafés, crafts, trades and services in private hands are part of the economy of this State, not a concession to be tolerated.

> **Why.** Variety, responsiveness and pleasure in everyday consumption are where planning is weakest and drabness most visible: the Trabant, the Intershop. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-05. **Residual risk:** private enterprise brings market inequality and a constituency for restoration (FM-12).

### Article 52 — Foreign enterprise {standard}

1. A foreign-owned enterprise may operate in the State under a charter agreed with the Executive Council and approved by the National Assembly. The charter fixes its taxation, the rights of its workplace council, its share of the physical budgets, and its obligations to train workers and share technology.
2. No foreign enterprise shall be expropriated except with compensation under Article 62.

> **Why.** In 2025, foreign multinationals paid 87% of corporation tax, the ten largest payers paid 56%, and IDA client firms employed 312,468 people. Ending the FDI model in one stroke would be the largest self-inflicted shock available. Charters let the State decide openly what to keep, on what terms, and what to let go. **Guards against:** FM-11. **Residual risk:** charter firms keep a powerful lobby inside the republic. **Source:** [08 §3](research/08-ireland-today.md).

### Article 53 — Users and communities {standard}

1. Every socially owned enterprise that provides an essential service shall have a users' council with the right to be consulted on its plan, prices and standards, and to bring complaints to the Ombudsman.

### Article 54 — Hard budgets and public rescue {core}

1. No public body, socially owned enterprise or co-operative has a right to have its losses covered by public funds.
2. Public funds may be used to rescue an enterprise only by a specific vote of the National Assembly or of the relevant county council, published with its cost and its reasons.
3. Where an enterprise closes, every worker has the right to work through the Job Guarantee Service and to retraining on full pay.

> **Why.** Kornai: the soft budget constraint made Soviet-type firms' demand for inputs "almost insatiable", which caused chronic shortage. Democratic bodies are under even more pressure to rescue local employers. The owner's design protects workers through society, not through the failing unit. **Guards against:** FM-06, FM-07, FM-09. **Residual risk:** communities built around one employer will resist closures, and the vote in clause 2 will be tempting. **Source:** [03 §3](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md).

### Article 55 — Pay {standard}

1. In every socially owned enterprise and co-operative, the highest rate of pay shall not exceed four times the lowest.
2. Law shall set a national floor for pay and income that is sufficient for dignity, and shall tax incomes progressively.

> **Why.** Mondragon has run a 1:6 ratio for decades. A tighter 1:4 is a judgement call that the owner may wish to revisit (DECISIONS D-31). **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-17. **Residual risk:** skilled people may leave for work abroad. **Source:** [06 §8](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 56 — Planning {core}

1. Investment in the major sectors is planned through the National Investment Council (Comhairle Infheistíochta Náisiúnta). Its members are delegates of the Federation of Workplace Councils, the users' councils, the county councils and the Executive Council. The Ecological Authority sits on it without a vote but with the powers of Article 64.
2. The Council prepares a five-year investment plan by negotiation among those it represents. The plan takes effect when approved by both the National Assembly and the Congress of Councils.
3. Regional planning boards, constituted in the same way, plan investment within their regions.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q7: democratically negotiated planning for investment and the major sectors. This is Devine's *negotiated coordination*: market exchange for current output, negotiation for capacity. **Guards against:** FM-05, FM-16. **Residual risk:** negotiation bodies can become a new coordinator class (FM-01), and Devine's model is thin on operational detail. **Source:** [03 §7](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md).

### Article 57 — Participatory budgets {core}

1. Not less than one third of the public capital budget of each county shall be allocated by its local assemblies.
2. Law shall provide technical help, training and published costings to every local assembly.

> **Why.** Kerala devolved 35–40% of its plan budget. Porto Alegre's participatory budget was kept in name after 2004 and starved of money. Entrenching the share means a future government cannot drain it quietly. **Guards against:** FM-08, FM-01. **Sources:** [06 §5, §9](research/06-positive-models.md).

### Article 58 — Markets and prices {standard}

1. Goods and services other than those provided under Part VII are bought and sold freely, at prices set by the enterprises that make them, subject to law on competition, safety and consumer protection.
2. Where law fixes or controls a price, it shall do so by a published formula, revised on a published timetable, with compensation for households on low incomes built in. No price shall be fixed or changed by announcement.
3. Where any good is scarce, it shall be rationed openly and equally, by published rule, waiting list or lot. No person shall obtain priority through office, connection or foreign currency.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q7: markets, mostly co-ops and small private firms, for most consumer goods and everyday services. Clause 2 follows Poland in 1970 and 1980, where price rises announced by the state turned everyday grievance into revolt against the state. Clause 3 rules out the Intershop and Tuzex: open scarcity is tolerable, but scarcity with a privilege tier is not. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-05, FM-01. **Sources:** [10 §2.2, §2.4](research/10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md).

### Article 59 — Open books {core}

1. The accounts, plans, contracts and performance data of every public body and socially owned enterprise, and the input–output data of the national plan, are public, published in open formats, and free to use.
2. Information may be withheld only to protect personal data, or for not more than two years where a court finds disclosure would cause serious commercial harm.

> **Why.** Open data is an answer to both Djilas and Hayek. It stops planners from monopolising knowledge, and it lets anyone check the plan against reality. Cybersyn showed that real-time economic data is possible. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-05, FM-12. **Source:** [03 §8](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md).

### Article 60 — The Social Wealth Fund {standard}

1. The Social Wealth Fund (Ciste an Rachmais Shóisialta) holds the State's investments for the long term. It receives every public revenue that law classes as windfall, including corporation tax above its trend.
2. The Fund finances the transition (Part XV), the Co-operative Development Fund and long-term public investment. It may not finance current spending, except as law provides in an emergency declared under Part X.

> **Why.** Windfall corporation tax depends on a handful of US firms. It must be ring-fenced *before* any change, so that its loss is survivable. **Guards against:** FM-11. **Source:** [08 §3](research/08-ireland-today.md); [Fiscal Council](https://fiscalcouncil.substack.com/p/more-than-corporation-tax-irelands).

### Article 61 — Money, the Central Bank and public banking {core}

1. The currency of the State is issued by the Central Bank (An Banc Ceannais). Its name is fixed by law.
2. The Central Bank shall pursue stable prices, full employment and a sound financial system, within a mandate and a target for inflation fixed by the National Assembly by law for periods of not less than five years. Within that mandate it decides interest rates and supervises banks and credit unions, and no member of the Executive Council may direct it in any rate decision or individual case.
3. The Central Bank is governed by a board of seven, appointed by the National Assembly from candidates nominated by the Executive Council after open competition, for single terms of seven years, staggered. A member may be removed only by a two-thirds vote of the Assembly for misconduct or incapacity. The Governor shall answer to a committee of the Assembly every quarter. The minutes of the board and the votes of its members are published within four weeks.
4. The Central Bank may lend to the State, or buy its debt when newly issued, only within a ceiling voted each year by the National Assembly as part of the budget and published with it.
5. Law may regulate the movement of capital into and out of the State. Every such rule shall be published before it takes effect, shall be administered by the Central Bank, shall lapse after six months unless renewed by the Assembly, and shall not restrict payments for ordinary imports, travel, remittances to family, or the transfer of personal savings below a threshold fixed by law.
6. The State maintains a public bank with branches in every county, and a network of credit unions and co-operative banks. Credit for socially owned enterprises, co-operatives, housing and the ecological transition is provided chiefly through them.

> **Why.** Outside the euro (Art. 104) the republic needs its own money and a central bank. Clause 2 splits the difference that the literature argues over: the Assembly sets the *goal* in public, and the Bank is insulated in *operation*, so neither the Executive nor an unelected board decides alone (Tucker's test for legitimate delegated power; before 1997, rate changes at the Bank of England *"had to be approved by the Chancellor"*). Clause 4 makes any monetary financing of the State a visible, voted decision with a number on it, not a quiet habit. Clause 5 makes capital controls lawful but temporary by default: Iceland's "temporary" controls lasted more than eight years. Leaving out ordinary payments protects everyday life from the controls. The currency's working name in this design is **the punt (IR£)**, after the Irish pound of 1928–2001; the owner may choose another. **Guards against:** FM-11, FM-06, FM-15, FM-01. **Residual risk:** a new currency has no record to draw on, and the six-monthly renewal of controls will usually pass. **Sources:** [13 §2–3](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md).

### Article 62 — Compensation {core}

1. Property taken into social ownership shall be compensated under law, at a value that takes account of the public interest and of any public support the property has received.
2. Personal savings, pensions and homes of ordinary value shall be compensated in full.

> **Why.** A lawful transition needs lawful compensation. Article 1 of Protocol 1 to the ECHR protects property subject to the public interest (still to be sourced in detail). Protecting small savers and pensions removes the most effective scare that opponents of a transition could run. **Guards against:** FM-10, FM-11, FM-17.

## Part VI — Ecology

*Cuid VI — Éiceolaíocht*

### Article 63 — Physical budgets {core}

1. Every five years the National Assembly shall fix by law binding budgets for the State's greenhouse-gas emissions, nitrogen surplus, land use, water abstraction and allocation of electricity, on a path consistent with the State's international obligations and the best available science.
2. The budgets bind every plan, enterprise, public body and council. Within them, allocation may be made by plan, negotiation or market.
3. No growth target, positive or negative, shall be set for the economy as a whole.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q9: the common-ground position. Physical limits are set politically and enforced as quantities, not only as prices. Clause 3 takes no side on whether total throughput must fall; it leaves that to democratic choice inside the limits. **Guards against:** FM-16. **Residual risk:** this is the easiest position to hollow out, by setting the budgets loosely. **Source:** [09](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md).

### Article 64 — The Ecological Authority {core}

1. The Ecological Authority (Údarás Éiceolaíochta) is independent. Its board of nine is appointed by a panel of three judges and six members of the Citizens' Chamber, from among scientists, farmers, workers in affected sectors and members of the public, for single seven-year terms.
2. The Authority advises on the budgets under Article 63 and monitors compliance with them.
3. The Authority may veto any item of a national, regional or county plan, or any charter under Article 52, that would breach a budget. A veto may be overturned only by the Constitutional Court on the ground that the Authority has misapplied the law. If the budgets themselves are to change, the National Assembly must amend them by law.

> **Why.** Soviet planning had no independent body with power to represent ecological limits, and no free press to raise the alarm; the Aral Sea is the result. The owner's answer to Q6 keeps the Authority independent. Democracy sets the limits; the Authority enforces them. **Guards against:** FM-16, FM-05. **Residual risk:** an unelected body with a veto over the plan will be resented; its legitimacy depends on its lay members and on the budgets being set democratically. **Source:** [09 §2](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md).

### Article 65 — Reducing waste {standard}

1. The State shall plan a reduction in material and energy use in sectors whose ecological cost exceeds their social value, as designated by law after a citizens' assembly. The first designation is made by Article 114.
2. Advertising in public space, and the promotion of products by planned obsolescence, shall be restricted by law. Durable goods shall be repairable, and their makers shall supply parts and information for repair.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q9: a planned fall in material use in named wasteful sectors. Hickel's proposals (ending planned obsolescence, curbing advertising) and Morris's distinction between *"wealth"* and *"waste"*. **Guards against:** FM-16. **Residual risk:** restrictions on advertising and products can make streets and shops feel drabber (FM-17); public luxury has to fill that space. **Sources:** [09 §1.1](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md); [Morris](https://libcom.org/article/useful-work-versus-useless-toil-william-morris).

### Article 66 — No austerity {core}

1. No measure taken under this Part shall reduce the rights in Part II or the income floor in Article 55 for any person. The costs of the ecological transition shall be borne progressively, according to means.

> **Why.** The owner's words: degrowth never means austerity for working people. Cuba's Special Period shows that an imposed, unplanned contraction is a humanitarian crisis, not a model. **Guards against:** FM-16, FM-17, FM-14. **Source:** [09 §2](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md).

### Article 67 — The land and farm transition {core}

1. Any change in the national herd or in agricultural land use shall be made under a land-use plan negotiated with farmers through the Land and Farm Transition Board, of which at least half the members are delegates elected by farmers.
2. Every farm household affected is guaranteed an income no lower than its average of the preceding five years for the period of the transition, and payment for land stewardship, rewetting, forestry and alternative production.
3. Processing of farm produce shall be returned to co-operative ownership by farmers and workers.

> **Why.** The herd is 6.9 million cattle, agriculture is 38% of emissions, and beef and dairy exports were worth €10.7 billion in 2025. It is the hardest constituency and the most likely point of political failure. The owner's answer: planned and paid for, negotiated rather than imposed. **Guards against:** FM-14, FM-16. **Residual risk:** cattle prices have doubled in five years, so many farmers are currently doing well out of the herd, and a guarantee pegged to recent averages is expensive. **Sources:** [09 §3](research/09-ecology-and-growth.md); [CSO](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-clsjf/cropsandlivestocksurveyfinalresultsjune2025).

## Part VII — Public Systems

*Cuid VII — Córais Phoiblí*

### Article 68 — Public luxury {standard}

1. Housing, health, care, childcare, education, public transport and a basic allowance of household energy are provided free or at cost, as universal public services, not as relief for the poor.
2. Not less than one hundredth of the capital budget of every public service shall be spent on design, art and amenity.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q7: *public luxury, private sufficiency*. Clause 2 protects beauty from being the first thing cut. Public goods must be genuinely good, or the design loses the argument with consumer capitalism. **Guards against:** FM-17, FM-16. **Sources:** [Monbiot](https://theecologist.org/2024/jul/09/private-sufficiency-and-public-luxury); [10 §5](research/10-legitimacy-and-everyday-life.md).

### Article 69 — The Housing Authority {standard}

1. The Housing Authority, with the county councils, builds, owns and maintains the public housing stock to the highest standards of warmth, accessibility and design. It allocates homes by published rules that give no priority to office or connection.
2. Tenants of public housing elect the tenants' council of each estate, which shares in management.

### Article 70 — The health service {standard}

1. The public health service is a single national service, delivered regionally and accountable to county councils and users' councils.

### Article 71 — The National Care Service and counting care {core}

1. The National Care Service provides childcare, home care and residential care as Article 21 requires. It employs care workers on terms no worse than those of comparable public employees.
2. The Central Statistics Office shall conduct a national survey of time use, paid and unpaid, at least every five years. Its results shall be laid before the National Assembly and used in every plan.

> **Why.** The last national time-use survey in Ireland was in 2005. The State does not measure the work it depends on. What is not counted is not planned, and Eastern Bloc care services were underfunded because care did not appear in the output plan. **Guards against:** FM-18. **Source:** [11 §5](research/11-social-reproduction.md).

## Part VIII — Work

*Cuid VIII — Obair*

### Article 72 — Working time {core}

1. The standard working week is fixed by law. Every five years the National Assembly shall reduce it in proportion to the rise in the productivity of labour, unless it decides by a two-thirds majority to use that rise otherwise.
2. Every worker has the right to paid annual leave of not less than five weeks.

> **Why.** Time is the reward. Effort has to buy something real (FM-07), and shorter hours make participation possible (FM-08) and care shareable (FM-18). **Guards against:** FM-07, FM-08, FM-18, FM-17. **Source:** [04 §6](research/04-stagnation-and-work.md).

### Article 73 — Job design {standard}

1. Workplace councils shall organise work so that unpleasant, dangerous and routine tasks are shared by rotation, and every worker has a share of skilled and decision-making work, so far as practicable.

> **Why.** This adapts parecon's balanced job complexes, against a "coordinator class" monopolising empowering work. It applies "so far as practicable" because full balancing is untested at scale. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-07. **Source:** [03 §6](research/03-planning-and-calculation.md).

### Article 74 — Recognition {standard}

1. Recognition for exceptional work shall be collective or by election of one's peers, and any monetary award is capped by law.
2. No recognition, record or example shall be used to raise the work norms, targets or quotas of other workers.

> **Why.** Stakhanov's 1935 record was staged, and the norm increases that followed in 1936 meant pay cuts for many and resentment of the "heroes". Recognition must never become a tool to intensify work. **Guards against:** FM-07, FM-17. **Source:** [04 §3](research/04-stagnation-and-work.md).

### Article 75 — Accountability at work {standard}

1. Persistent failure to do one's share of work shall be dealt with first by one's peers through the workplace council, with fair procedures, then by redeployment and retraining. A worker may be dismissed from an enterprise by its workplace council after fair procedures, but no person may be deprived of the means of life.

> **Why.** Laziness and shirking are real, and the design handles them without coercion or destitution. Peers have the knowledge to judge; the Job Guarantee Service catches anyone dismissed. **Guards against:** FM-07, FM-06.

### Article 76 — The Job Guarantee Service {standard}

1. The Job Guarantee Service offers socially useful work at a living wage, in care, repair, retrofitting, environmental restoration, culture and public works, to every person who seeks it, in or near their own community.

## Part IX — Culture and Media

*Cuid IX — Cultúr agus na Meáin*

### Article 77 — The Public Media Trust {core}

1. Public broadcasting and public digital media are held by the Public Media Trust. It is independent of the Executive Council, the National Assembly and every party.
2. Its funding is fixed by law as a share of national income for periods of ten years and may not be reduced during a period.
3. Its board consists of one third elected by its staff, one third chosen by lot from among residents, and one third appointed by the Media Regulator. No member of any party's executive may serve on it.
4. No organ of State may direct its editorial decisions.

> **Why.** This prevents a state monopoly on truth. Funding fixed for a decade removes the budget as a lever of pressure. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-03, FM-17. **Residual risk:** staff and lot-chosen members can still form a cosy consensus; plural media funding (Article 79) is the counterweight.

### Article 78 — The Media Regulator {core}

1. The Media Regulator is independent. It licenses broadcasting, enforces rules on ownership concentration and accuracy in news, and appoints one third of the Trust's board. It may not regulate opinion.

### Article 79 — Plural media {standard}

1. Each year every adult resident may direct a sum fixed by law, from public funds, to the news organisations of their choice that meet published standards of independence and transparency.
2. No person or organisation may own more than a share of the news market fixed by law.

> **Why.** Citizens direct the funding, so neither the State nor a single trust decides which voices are heard. Similar "media voucher" schemes have been proposed elsewhere (to be sourced). **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-17.

### Article 80 — The Arts and Design Council {standard}

1. The Arts and Design Council funds the arts, crafts, design and public amenity at arm's length from government, under a budget that may not fall below a share of national income fixed by law.

### Article 81 — The Language Commissioner {standard}

1. An Coimisinéir Teanga protects the language rights in Article 4 and may bring proceedings to enforce them.

## Part X — Security, Defence and Emergencies

*Cuid X — Slándáil, Cosaint agus Éigeandálaí*

### Article 82 — The police {core}

1. An Garda Síochána is a civilian police service. It has no political function and shall not gather information on any person for their lawful political, union, religious or cultural activity.
2. Each county council sets local policing priorities. National policing policy is set by law and overseen by the independent Policing Authority.
3. Police officers have the right and the duty to refuse an unlawful order, including an order to act against a peaceful assembly or strike.

> **Why.** The Commune's police were "the responsible, and at all times revocable, agent" of the people. Clause 1 bars the political policing that produced the Stasi's files on a large share of the population. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-10. **Sources:** [Marx](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm); [01 §7](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md).

### Article 83 — The Police Ombudsman {standard}

1. The Police Ombudsman investigates every complaint against the police and every death or serious injury involving the police, independently of the police and of government.

### Article 84 — The intelligence service {core}

1. The State may maintain an intelligence service to protect against espionage, sabotage, terrorism and attacks on infrastructure by foreign states and armed groups. It is separate from the police and the Defence Forces.
2. The intelligence service has no power to arrest, detain, interrogate under compulsion or prosecute any person.
3. It may intercept communications or carry out surveillance only under a warrant issued by a judge of the High Court for a specific person or place, for a specific period, on evidence of a specific threat. Warrants and their outcomes are reviewed by the Security Oversight Committee.
4. Its budget is published by programme each year. It shall not investigate any person for lawful political activity.

> **Why.** The Cheka began as a body to investigate and refer cases and took the power to punish within months. This service gets intelligence powers but none of punishment. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-15. **Residual risk:** a democratic socialist Ireland would face real hostile intelligence activity, as the CIA in Chile shows. A service that is too weak is also a danger (FM-10). **Sources:** [01 §7](research/01-bureaucratic-degeneration.md); [05 §1](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md).

### Article 85 — The Security Oversight Committee {core}

1. The Security Oversight Committee consists of twelve members chosen by lot from the Citizens' Chamber, four members of the National Assembly chosen by lot from among those who are not members of the Executive Council or of the largest grouping in the Assembly, and an Inspector who is a serving or retired judge.
2. The Committee has full access to the records, premises and personnel of the police, the intelligence service and the Defence Forces. It may publish any finding, redacting only what a judge finds would endanger a person or a current lawful operation.
3. The Committee may suspend any operation it finds unlawful, pending a ruling of the High Court.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q5: binding oversight by lot, where elected politicians have a conflict of interest. Nobody can capture a body chosen by lot by winning an election. **Guards against:** FM-04, FM-01. **Residual risk:** members chosen by lot may defer to security professionals, so the Inspector and full access matter.

### Article 86 — No command but the law {core}

1. The police, intelligence service and Defence Forces act only under law and on the lawful direction of the Executive Council as a whole. No party, association or individual member of the Council may direct them outside that authority.

### Article 87 — The Defence Forces {core}

1. The Defence Forces defend the State's territory, airspace, seas and undersea infrastructure, and serve in peacekeeping under Article 88.
2. The Defence Forces have no role in any political or industrial dispute, and shall not be deployed against any assembly or strike.
3. Members of the Defence Forces have the right to form representative associations and the duty to refuse an unlawful order.
4. The Chief of Staff and senior commanders are appointed by the Executive Council with the consent of two thirds of the National Assembly, for fixed terms.

> **Why.** Allende's armed forces command turned on the constitutional government in 1973 (the details of Pinochet's appointment still need sourcing). Civilian control requires the consent of a broad majority to appointments, and a constitutional ban on any domestic political role. **Guards against:** FM-10, FM-04. **Source:** [05 §1](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md).

### Article 88 — Deployment abroad {core}

1. No member of the Defence Forces shall be deployed outside the State on any operation other than for rescue or humanitarian relief, except under a mandate of the United Nations, with the approval of the Executive Council and of the National Assembly.

> **Why.** This entrenches the "triple lock" that the 2026 Defence (Amendment) Bill would remove. **Guards against:** FM-10, FM-14. **Source:** [08 §9](research/08-ireland-today.md).

### Article 89 — Declaring an emergency {core}

1. Where the life of the community is threatened by armed attack, sabotage of essential services, natural disaster or a sudden failure of supply, the Executive Council may declare an emergency. The declaration lapses after seventy-two hours unless the National Assembly approves it by a majority of its members.
2. During an emergency the Executive Council may make orders that are strictly necessary to meet the threat, and that law permits. Every order is laid before the Assembly at once and may be annulled by it, or by any court, at any time.
3. No emergency order may suspend the rights in Article 13(2), postpone any election, extend any term of office, dissolve or suspend any council or assembly, or create any offence defined by opinion or allegiance.

### Article 90 — Duration and sunset of emergencies {eternal}

1. A declaration of emergency lasts no more than thirty days.
2. It may be extended for further periods of no more than thirty days each, only with the approval of two thirds of the National Assembly and of a majority of the Citizens' Chamber, voting separately.
3. No emergency may last more than ninety days in any period of twelve months. A further extension requires the approval of the people in a referendum. A new declaration that relies on substantially the same facts counts as an extension of the old one.
4. The courts may at any time rule that an emergency or an emergency order lacks a factual basis, and the ruling has immediate effect.

> **Why.** Owner's answers to Q2 and Q5: the sunset is eternal, and any extension needs the approval of a body chosen by lot. Clause 3's last sentence closes the "rolling emergency" loophole. **Guards against:** FM-15, FM-04. **Sources:** [SYNTHESIS FM-15](research/SYNTHESIS.md); [Article 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_48_of_the_Weimar_Constitution).

### Article 91 — No penalty for refusing an emergency {core}

1. The refusal, annulment or non-extension of any declaration or order of emergency by any body shall not dissolve that body, trigger any election, or have any other penalty.

> **Why.** Under Weimar the Reichstag could revoke presidential decrees, but doing so triggered dissolution, so the check was real on paper and inoperative in practice. **Guards against:** FM-15. **Source:** [Rempel](https://johndclare.net/Rempel_Weimar23.htm).

## Part XI — Justice

*Cuid XI — Ceartas*

### Article 92 — Independence of the courts {eternal}

1. Judges are independent in the exercise of their functions and subject only to this Constitution and the law. No person or body may instruct a judge.
2. A judge may be removed only for stated misbehaviour or incapacity, found after a hearing by a tribunal of judges, and by resolution of two thirds of the National Assembly.
3. The pay of a judge shall not be reduced during their term of office.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q6: Commune-style accountability for the executive, but the courts stay independent. In the Commune, judges were elected and revocable by the same majority, and that part is not adopted, because fusing the judiciary into the governing body is how emergency powers became permanent. Owner's answer to Q2: independent courts belong to the eternal core. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-04, FM-15.

### Article 93 — Appointment of judges {core}

1. Judges are appointed by the President on the nomination of the Judicial Appointments Commission, which consists of judges, practising lawyers and members of the public chosen by lot, in equal numbers. No member of the Executive Council, the Assembly or any party's executive may serve on it.

> **Why.** Taking appointment out of the hands of the executive is the main defence against a captured bench. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-01. **Residual risk (a left critique):** judges recruited from the legal profession may be hostile to social ownership. Lay members chosen by lot and the published grounds for nomination are the counterweights.

### Article 94 — The Constitutional Court {core}

1. The Constitutional Court consists of nine judges, each appointed under Article 93 for a single term of twelve years, with terms staggered.
2. It decides whether laws and acts of State conform to this Constitution, on referral by the President, the Citizens' Chamber, one fifth of the members of the National Assembly, or any court, or on the complaint of any person whose rights are affected.
3. It enforces the social rights in Part II as Article 27 provides.

### Article 95 — The Ombudsman {standard}

1. The Ombudsman investigates complaints against any public body or socially owned enterprise, may enter any premises and see any record, and publishes reports.

### Article 96 — Protection of dissent {core}

1. No person shall suffer any detriment in employment, housing, education or public service for their opinions or lawful political activity.
2. Every public servant, and every worker in a socially owned enterprise, who discloses wrongdoing in the public interest is protected by law from every detriment.
3. Proceedings brought to silence public criticism shall be struck out, and costs awarded against those who bring them.

> **Why.** Dissent is protected where it is most often punished in practice: in jobs, flats and school places, not only in courtrooms. Clause 3 bars strategic lawsuits used to silence critics. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-17, FM-01.

### Article 97 — Juries {standard}

1. Every person charged with a serious offence has the right to trial by jury.

## Part XII — Integrity Bodies

*Cuid XII — Comhlachtaí Ionracais*

### Article 98 — Independence of integrity bodies {core}

1. The Audit Office, the Pay and Privileges Panel, the Electoral Commission, the Open Data Office, the Ombudsman, the Policing Authority, the Police Ombudsman, the Media Regulator and the Ecological Authority are independent. Each is subject only to this Constitution and the law.
2. The budget of each is fixed by law by formula for periods of five years and may not be reduced during a period.
3. The head of each may be removed only for stated misbehaviour or incapacity, by two thirds of the National Assembly after a finding of a court.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q6: the bodies that check power stay independent of the power they check. Fixed budgets remove the most common quiet lever. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-02, FM-04, FM-12.

### Article 99 — The Audit Office {standard}

1. The Comptroller and Auditor General audits every public body, socially owned enterprise, charter enterprise in respect of its charter, and every party and association receiving public funds, and reports publicly to the National Assembly and the Citizens' Chamber.

### Article 100 — The Pay and Privileges Panel {core}

1. The Pay and Privileges Panel consists of twenty-one members of the Citizens' Chamber chosen by lot each year.
2. It fixes, by binding decision, the skilled worker's wage that applies under Article 28 and every benefit permitted to any office-holder. It audits the declarations made under Article 28 with the help of the Audit Office, and may refer any breach to the courts.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q5: binding sortition where elected politicians have a conflict of interest. Nobody should set their own pay. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-12.

### Article 101 — The Electoral Commission {standard}

1. An Coimisiún Toghcháin conducts all elections and referendums, maintains the register, regulates campaign finance, and draws constituency boundaries.

### Article 102 — The Open Data Office {standard}

1. The Open Data Office ensures that Article 59 is observed and publishes the data of the State in usable form.

### Article 103 — Standards in public office {standard}

1. No person holding office under Article 28 shall hold any interest in an enterprise or body that they regulate, fund or oversee, or appoint to any post a member of their household or a person with whom they have a close business or political relationship without declaring it.
2. The Standards Commission investigates breaches, including networks of influence that do not appear in formal posts.

> **Why.** Privilege moves into informal networks when formal privilege is abolished. Clause 2 lets the investigators follow it there. **Guards against:** FM-01, FM-12.

## Part XIII — External Relations

*Cuid XIII — Caidreamh Seachtrach*

### Article 104 — Outside the European Union {core}

1. The State is not a member of the European Union. On the adoption of this Constitution, the Executive Council shall give notice of withdrawal under Article 50 of the Treaty on European Union, and the State shall leave the euro on the day its withdrawal takes effect.
2. On that day every sum of money expressed in euro in any contract, obligation or enactment governed by the law of the State is converted into the currency of the State at par, one for one. Law shall provide for contracts governed by other law.
3. The Executive Council shall seek agreements with the European Union and with the United Kingdom on trade, on the rights of citizens, on the land border and on co-operation in every field of common interest. Every agreement shall be negotiated in public, published in draft, and laid before the National Assembly for approval.
4. No agreement may commit the State to restrict the social ownership in Article 5, the forms of ownership and planning in Part V, or the regulation of capital under Article 61, unless approved by the procedure of Article 109.
5. Every citizen of a member state of the European Union lawfully resident in the State on the day withdrawal takes effect keeps, for life, the right to live, work, study and receive public services in the State on the same terms as before. The State shall seek the same protection for its own citizens living in the Union.

> **Why.** The owner's revised answer to Q4 (DECISIONS D-43). The party settlement of Article 30 cannot meet the values of Article 2 TEU, which the Court of Justice says *"cannot be … disregard[ed] after accession"*, and the design's economic instruments (capital controls, state support for co-operatives, procurement) collide with Articles 63 and 107 TFEU. The EU cannot expel a member, so the republic leaves by its own decision rather than live in permanent breach. Clause 2 uses *lex monetae*: the State can convert contracts under its own law, not others. Clause 4 stops a trade deal from quietly undoing Part V: the EU's price for tariff-free access is likely to be subsidy-control rules, and accepting them must clear the same bar as amending the core. Clause 5 protects people who built lives here. **Guards against:** FM-05, FM-02 (no permanent selective legality). **Worsens:** FM-11 (a new currency, tariffs, capital flight) and FM-17 (support for EU membership was 82% in 2025 and 2026). **Residual risk:** the largest economic shock in the design, and a hard external frontier on the land border (Art. 107). **Sources:** [13](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md); [CJEU press release 28/22](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-02/cp220028en.pdf); [TEU Art. 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eut/teu/article/50).

### Article 105 — International law and human rights {standard}

1. Ireland accepts the generally recognised principles of international law, and remains a party to the European Convention on Human Rights. The rights it protects are a floor beneath the rights in this Constitution.

> **Why.** ECHR membership comes through the Council of Europe and is independent of the EU, so it survives withdrawal (Art. 104). Outside the EU it is the republic's main outside check. **Unresolved tension with the default Q1 answer:** the Strasbourg court holds that *"there can be no democracy without pluralism"* and that *"only convincing and compelling reasons can justify restrictions on"* parties' freedom of association (*United Communist Party of Turkey v. Turkey*, 1998). Article 30 is likely to fail that test. The design keeps the Convention anyway, and says so. **Guards against:** FM-02, FM-04. **Source:** [13 §1.4](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md).

### Article 106 — Trade and solidarity {standard}

1. The State shall diversify its trade and supply, secure its food and energy, and pursue solidarity with peoples struggling for democracy, independence and social justice. It shall not export arms.
2. The Executive Council shall keep, and publish each year, a schedule of essential imports (fuel, food, medicines and the inputs to make them) and the means of paying for them, including reserves of foreign currency and stocks held in the State.

> **Why.** A small, open economy must assume pressure from outside. Cuba and Nicaragua show what blockade does. **Guards against:** FM-11. **Source:** [05 §5](research/05-counter-revolution-and-external-pressure.md).

### Article 107 — All-island co-operation and the land border {core}

1. The State shall take part in the North/South bodies established by the 1998 Agreement and shall seek co-operation across the island in every field where both jurisdictions consent.
2. The State shall keep the Common Travel Area with the United Kingdom. No check on persons shall be made at the land border.
3. The State shall build no customs post, checkpoint or other border infrastructure on its side of the land border, and shall seek, by agreement with the United Kingdom, to charge no tariff on goods made in Northern Ireland. It shall seek with the European Union and the United Kingdom whatever arrangements keep checks on goods away from the border and as few as possible.
4. Every person born in Northern Ireland keeps the full rights of Irish citizenship under Article 3, including access to every public service in the State on the same terms as its residents.

> **Why.** Leaving the EU (Art. 104) turns the land border into the EU's external frontier for goods, because Northern Ireland applies the Union Customs Code under the Windsor Framework. The republic cannot stop checks on the northern side. It can refuse to build anything on its own side, keep people moving freely under the Common Travel Area (which its two governments say is *"not dependent on, EU citizenship or EU membership"*), and keep the North's Irish citizens inside the State's public life when they lose EU citizenship. **Guards against:** FM-14. **Residual risk:** checks on the northern side would still happen, at some of the 208 or more crossing points, and border infrastructure on this island has been a target before. This is one of the heaviest costs of Q4, and it falls on people in the North who have no vote in the State's referendum. **Sources:** [13 §4.4–4.7](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md).

## Part XIV — Amendment

*Cuid XIV — Leasú*

### Article 108 — The tiers of amendment {eternal}

1. The democratic core in Article 14 may not be amended.
2. Articles marked as core in this Constitution, and the social ownership in Article 5, may be amended only under Article 109.
3. Every other Article may be amended under Article 110.
4. This Article may not be amended so as to weaken the protection it gives.

### Article 109 — Amending the core {core}

1. A proposal to amend a core Article shall be passed by the National Assembly, then passed again unchanged by the National Assembly elected at the next general election, and then approved by the people in a referendum.

> **Why.** The owner's answer to Q2: hard, but not impossible. A quiet elite cannot privatise the grid; a majority that has persuaded two elected Assemblies and then the people can. **Entrenchment is a secondary defence**: organised popular strength is the real guarantee. **Guards against:** FM-12, FM-11 (a credible commitment). **Residual risk:** the asymmetry objection, that the transition used ordinary procedures but reversal needs extraordinary ones. Article 111 answers it by requiring the transition itself to clear the same bar.

### Article 110 — Ordinary amendment {core}

1. A proposal to amend any other Article shall be passed by the National Assembly and approved by the people in a referendum, or initiated by citizens under Article 41.

## Part XV — Transition

*Cuid XV — Aistriú*

### Article 111 — Adoption {standard}

1. This Constitution is adopted as an amendment of the Constitution of 1937 under its Article 46: passed by the Dáil, passed again unchanged by the Dáil elected at the next general election, and approved by the people in a referendum.
2. The ballot paper for that referendum shall state, in words approved by the Electoral Commission, that adopting this Constitution means leaving the European Union and the euro.

> **Why.** The transition is lawful from end to end, using Article 46 and the 1937 precedent of adoption by plebiscite. The double passage is not required by Article 46, which needs only a single passage and a referendum. It is chosen voluntarily, so that the transition clears the same bar that reversal will need. Legality is itself a defence against counter-revolution. Clause 2 exists because support for EU membership was 82% in 2025 and 2026: a vote for this Constitution must not be mistaken, then or later, for anything other than a vote to leave. **Guards against:** FM-10, FM-17. **Sources:** [08 §1](research/08-ireland-today.md); [13 §1.6](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md).

### Article 112 — Continuity {standard}

1. All laws in force continue until amended or repealed, so far as they are consistent with this Constitution.
2. No person shall be prosecuted or suffer any detriment for any lawful act done before this Constitution came into force.

### Article 113 — Transitional powers {core}

1. For not more than five years after this Constitution comes into force, the Executive Council may exercise the transitional powers that law confers to carry out Parts V to VIII.
2. Every transitional power lapses at the end of that period, and no transitional power may limit any right in Part II.

> **Why.** The transition will need powers that ordinary times do not, and history shows such powers tend to outlive their purpose. These are fixed in advance with an end date. **Guards against:** FM-15.

### Article 114 — Sequence of the transition {standard}

1. In the first two years the State shall: ring-fence windfall revenues in the Social Wealth Fund; begin the public housing, single-tier health and National Care Service programmes; establish the local assemblies, the Citizens' Chamber and the integrity bodies; and open negotiations on foreign enterprise charters.
2. Before withdrawal from the European Union takes effect (Art. 104), the State shall have: established the Central Bank's board and published the law on capital movements (Art. 61); prepared the new currency for issue on the day of withdrawal; built reserves of foreign currency and stocks of fuel, food and medicines, and published the schedule of essential imports (Art. 106); sought agreements on trade, citizens' rights and the land border; and published, before any payment system changes, how wages, prices, rents, debts and deposits convert.
3. The first designation of sectors under Article 65 shall include data centres, aviation, advertising and the national herd, each to be planned with those who work in it, and the herd under Article 67.
4. No national public enterprise shall be created by expropriation before the Social Wealth Fund and the Job Guarantee Service are in operation.

> **Why.** Public goods come first, because that is where the case is strongest and opposition weakest. The safety nets come before any expropriation. Clause 2 orders the exit so that the most dangerous moment, the day the currency changes, comes with reserves, stocks and published rules already in place: the Czech and Slovak separation of 1993 was a *"non-event"* for most people because it was prepared, while Argentina's in 2002 was not. Named sectors follow the owner's answer to Q9. **Guards against:** FM-11, FM-17, FM-14. **Sources:** [08 §12](research/08-ireland-today.md); [13 §2.3](research/13-leaving-the-eu.md).

:::variant Q1=A
### Article 115 — The party in transition {standard}

1. The party referred to in Article 30 is the party that, being organised under Chapter 1 of Part III, is so registered by the Electoral Commission before the first general election held under this Constitution.
2. If more than one socialist party seeks registration, the Electoral Commission shall convene a founding congress open to all of their members, organised under Article 31, and the party it founds is the party referred to in Article 30.

> **Why.** Article 30 needs a lawful way to identify "the party". A founding congress with protected platforms means that existing socialist parties merge into one party with factions, rather than one of them being anointed. **Residual risk:** the founding congress is where the first and largest struggle for control will happen. It is the 1921 moment, transposed.
:::

### Article 116 — Review {standard}

1. In the tenth year after this Constitution comes into force, a citizens' assembly shall review its working and report to the people.

## Schedule A — Rules applying if the party's status is not renewed

:::variant Q1=A
If a referendum under Article 33 returns a No vote, Articles 30 to 33 are replaced by the following:

- **30.** Any association may register as a political party if its programme accepts the democratic core (Article 14) and the social ownership in Article 5. Only the Constitutional Court may refuse or cancel registration, on narrow grounds.
- **31.** Every party fills its offices by secret ballot, permits platforms, and publishes its accounts. Its officials are subject to Article 28.
- **32.** No person may hold senior office in a party and in the State at once. No party may instruct the police, intelligence, defence, courts or any independent body. A panel of the Citizens' Chamber audits every party's finances.
- **33.** Public funding favours small and new parties; private donations are capped.
